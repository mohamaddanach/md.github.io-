# The Enterprise Technology Landscape: ERP, CRM, Accounting, Banking and Supply-Chain Systems, and Business Intelligence

:::obj In this chapter
The main categories of business systems, the processes and data they manage, how they connect, and what business people should know to work with them.
:::

## The landscape

| System | Supports | Key data objects | Examples of vendors/products (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ERP (enterprise resource planning) | Finance, procurement, inventory, sales orders, manufacturing, HR | General ledger, vendors, customers, items, orders, invoices | SAP S/4HANA, Oracle Fusion/NetSuite, Microsoft Dynamics 365, Odoo, Infor |
| Accounting software (SMB) | Bookkeeping, invoicing, payables, bank reconciliation, VAT | Ledger, invoices, bank feeds | Xero, QuickBooks, Zoho Books, local e-invoicing compliant tools |
| CRM | Leads, accounts, opportunities, quotes, service cases, marketing | Contacts, opportunities, activities | Salesforce, HubSpot, Microsoft Dynamics, Zoho |
| SCM / WMS / TMS | Planning, warehouse operations, transport | Stock locations, shipments, routes | Manhattan, Blue Yonder, SAP EWM, Oracle WMS |
| HRIS / payroll | Employees, payroll, time, performance | Employee records, pay | Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, local payroll systems |
| Treasury management system (TMS) | Cash positions, payments, FX, debt, bank connectivity | Bank accounts, deals, forecasts | Kyriba, SAP Treasury, FIS, ION |
| Core banking system (banks) | Deposits, loans, customer accounts, transactions | Customers, accounts, ledgers | Temenos, Finastra, Oracle FLEXCUBE, Infosys Finacle |
| Trade-finance systems (banks) | L/Cs, guarantees, collections, SWIFT messages | Instruments, documents, limits | Bank platforms; corporate portals |
| E-commerce and POS | Online and in-store sales | Orders, payments, catalogues | Shopify, Magento/Adobe Commerce, POS systems |
| BI and analytics | Reporting, dashboards, analysis | Data warehouse models, metrics | Power BI, Tableau, Looker, Qlik |
| Data platform | Storage and processing of data | Tables, files, pipelines | Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery, Redshift |
| Document and workflow | Approvals, contracts, e-signature | Documents, workflows | SharePoint, DocuSign, workflow tools |

## How an ERP reflects the business

In an ERP, business events create documents that post accounting entries automatically:

| Business event | ERP document | Accounting entry (simplified) |
|---|---|---|
| Customer order received | Sales order | None (commitment) |
| Goods shipped | Delivery / goods issue | Dr COGS / Cr Inventory |
| Invoice issued | Customer invoice | Dr Receivables / Cr Revenue, Cr VAT payable |
| Payment received | Incoming payment | Dr Bank / Cr Receivables |
| Purchase order | PO | None |
| Goods received | Goods receipt (GRN) | Dr Inventory / Cr GR/IR (goods received–invoice received clearing) |
| Supplier invoice | Vendor invoice (three-way match) | Dr GR/IR, Dr VAT recoverable / Cr Payables |
| Payment run | Outgoing payment | Dr Payables / Cr Bank |

**Master data** (customers, suppliers, items, prices, chart of accounts) determines data quality: duplicated customers, wrong units of measure, or missing tax codes cause reporting errors and control failures. **Master data governance** — owners, approval workflows, validation rules — is a key ERP success factor.

## CRM and the revenue process

CRM records the pipeline and customer interactions; integrated with ERP, it passes won deals to orders, and gives salespeople visibility of invoices, payments and credit limits. Key business requirements: pipeline stages aligned with the sales process, forecasting, quote approval workflows (deal desk), and reporting on activity and conversion.

## Banking and treasury connectivity

Companies connect ERPs and treasury systems to banks via **host-to-host** file transfer (payment files such as ISO 20022 pain.001; statements camt.053), **SWIFT for corporates**, bank **APIs** (real-time balances, payment initiation, confirmations) and **bank portals**. Benefits: straight-through processing, automated reconciliation, real-time cash visibility, fraud controls (payment approvals and screening in one place).

## Business intelligence

BI provides governed reporting and self-service analysis. Typical architecture: source systems → data pipelines (ELT/ETL) → data warehouse/lakehouse → semantic layer (definitions of metrics such as “net revenue”, “gross margin”, “DSO”) → dashboards and reports. The **semantic layer** is crucial: it ensures that finance and sales use identical definitions.

:::exercise Practice questions
1. Explain how a duplicate supplier record could enable fraud and what ERP controls prevent it.
2. Which three integrations would you prioritise for a mid-sized importer-distributor using a cloud ERP?
:::

:::answer Answers to the practice questions
1. A duplicate vendor with different bank details can receive payments meant for the real supplier, or allow duplicate invoices to be paid twice. Controls: vendor creation/change workflow with segregation from payment approval, bank-detail verification by call-back, duplicate checks (tax ID, bank account, name similarity), periodic vendor master review.
2. Bank connectivity (statements and payments) for reconciliation and cash visibility; customs/forwarder data (shipment status, landed-cost components) to calculate landed cost and track inventory in transit; CRM/e-commerce order integration (orders, credit limits). Also e-invoicing with tax authorities where mandatory.
:::

# Data, Analytics, AI and Machine Learning in Business; Data Governance

:::obj In this chapter
Turning data into decisions: data architecture, analytics maturity, AI and machine learning use cases with their business requirements, generative AI, model risk, and data governance.
:::

## From data to decisions

| Maturity level | Question answered | Typical tools | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | What happened? | Reports, dashboards | Monthly sales by customer |
| Diagnostic | Why did it happen? | Drill-down, statistical analysis | Margin fall explained by mix and freight |
| Predictive | What will happen? | Forecasting, ML models | Probability that an invoice will be paid late |
| Prescriptive | What should we do? | Optimisation, simulation, decision rules | Optimal reorder quantities; collection prioritisation |
| Autonomous | Execute decisions automatically | Integrated systems with controls | Automatic replenishment orders within limits |

## Machine learning use cases in finance and operations

| Use case | Data | Model types | Business value | Key risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Credit scoring / limit setting | Financials, payment history, bureau data, transaction data | Logistic regression, gradient boosting | Faster decisions, lower bad debt | Bias, explainability, regulatory requirements |
| Payment-delay prediction | Invoice, customer and historical payment data | Classification / survival models | Prioritise collections; better cash forecasts | Data quality |
| Demand forecasting | Sales history, promotions, seasonality, prices, weather, events | Time-series models, gradient boosting, deep learning | Lower stock and stock-outs | Structural breaks, overfitting |
| Fraud detection | Transactions, vendor data, behaviour | Anomaly detection, supervised classifiers | Reduce losses | False positives, adaptation by fraudsters |
| Invoice and document processing | Scanned invoices, B/Ls, certificates | OCR, document AI, large language models | Faster processing, fewer errors | Extraction errors; need for validation |
| Pricing optimisation | Price, volume, competitor data | Elasticity models, optimisation | Margin improvement | Customer reaction, competition law |
| Churn prediction | Usage, support tickets, billing | Classification | Targeted retention | Acting on predictions ethically |
| Route optimisation | Orders, locations, vehicle constraints | Operations research (VRP solvers) | Lower transport cost | Data on constraints |

## Generative AI

Large language models (LLMs) and other generative models can draft documents and emails, summarise contracts and reports, answer questions over company documents (retrieval-augmented generation), extract data from unstructured documents, assist coding, and support customer service. Business requirements: data confidentiality (where data are processed and stored, vendor terms), accuracy (models can produce plausible but wrong outputs — require verification for financial and legal content), access controls, intellectual property, regulatory obligations (e.g., the EU AI Act’s risk-based requirements, entering into application in phases from 2025), and clear human accountability for decisions.

## Model risk management

Models (statistical, ML, financial spreadsheets) can be wrong or misused. Good practice — formalised in banking by supervisory guidance on model risk — includes: documentation of purpose, data and assumptions; independent validation; performance monitoring (drift, accuracy); explainability appropriate to the decision; controls on changes; and an inventory of models with owners.

## Data governance

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| Data ownership | Business owners accountable for data domains (customer, supplier, product, finance) |
| Data stewardship | Day-to-day quality management |
| Definitions and catalogue | Business glossary and metric definitions; data lineage |
| Data quality | Accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, uniqueness — measured and monitored |
| Access and security | Role-based access, classification (public, internal, confidential, restricted) |
| Privacy and compliance | Personal data handling under applicable laws |
| Retention | Legal retention periods and deletion |
| Architecture standards | Integration patterns, master data management |

:::ar البيانات والذكاء الاصطناعي في الأعمال
تتدرج التحليلات من وصف ما حدث، إلى تفسير أسبابه، إلى التنبؤ بما سيحدث، ثم اقتراح القرار الأفضل. ويستخدم تعلّم الآلة في التقييم الائتماني والتنبؤ بتأخر السداد والتنبؤ بالطلب واكتشاف الاحتيال ومعالجة الفواتير آلياً. لكن قيمة النماذج تعتمد على جودة البيانات وحوكمتها، وعلى قابلية تفسير النتائج، ومراقبة أداء النماذج، وحماية البيانات الشخصية. والنماذج اللغوية التوليدية مفيدة في الصياغة والتلخيص واستخراج البيانات، لكنها قد تخطئ، فيجب التحقق من مخرجاتها في المسائل المالية والقانونية.
:::

:::exercise Practice questions
1. A credit-scoring model has 92% accuracy on historical data but defaults are only 4% of cases. Why is accuracy a poor metric, and what would you use?
2. Propose three data-quality rules for a customer master table.
:::

:::answer Answers to the practice questions
1. A model predicting “no default” for everyone would achieve 96% accuracy. Use metrics suited to imbalanced classes: precision and recall for defaults, AUC/ROC, Gini, KS statistic, calibration of predicted probabilities, and the business cost of false negatives vs false positives (expected loss).
2. Unique tax registration number per legal entity; mandatory country, currency and payment terms fields from approved lists; valid email/phone formats and address completeness; credit limit present for credit customers; no duplicates by name + tax ID similarity.
:::

# Integration, Automation, Cloud, Cybersecurity and Technology Risk

:::obj In this chapter
How systems connect, how processes are automated, cloud service and deployment models, cybersecurity fundamentals, and technology risk management.
:::

## Integration

| Method | Description | Use |
|---|---|---|
| APIs (REST, GraphQL) | Systems request data and actions in real time | Banking, logistics tracking, e-commerce, SaaS integration |
| Webhooks / event streaming | Systems push events when something happens | Payment notifications, order status |
| EDI (e.g., UN/EDIFACT, ANSI X12) | Structured document exchange (orders, invoices, ASN) | Retail, automotive, logistics |
| File transfer (SFTP, host-to-host) | Batch files | Bank payments and statements, legacy systems |
| Middleware / iPaaS | Central integration platform | Many-to-many integrations |
| Robotic process automation (RPA) | Software bots mimic user actions on interfaces | Legacy systems without APIs (stop-gap) |

Integration design considerations: master-data ownership, error handling and reconciliation, idempotency (avoid duplicates on retries — critical for payments), security (authentication, encryption), monitoring, and versioning.

## Automation

From simple to advanced: spreadsheet macros → workflow automation (approvals, notifications) → RPA → intelligent document processing (OCR + AI) → end-to-end process automation with exception handling. Good candidates: high-volume, rule-based, stable processes with digital inputs (e.g., bank reconciliation, invoice matching, report generation). Automate **after** simplifying the process; automating a bad process just produces errors faster.

## Cloud computing

| Model | Customer manages | Provider manages | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| IaaS | OS, middleware, applications, data | Hardware, virtualisation, network | Virtual machines |
| PaaS | Applications, data | Platform runtime, OS, infrastructure | Managed databases, app platforms |
| SaaS | Configuration, data, users | Everything else | CRM, ERP, email |

Deployment: public cloud, private cloud, hybrid, multi-cloud. Business considerations: cost model (opex, usage-based — requires cost management or “FinOps”), scalability, resilience, vendor lock-in, data residency requirements (several Gulf countries have data-localisation rules for certain sectors and government data; hyperscalers have opened regional data centres), and the **shared responsibility model** for security.

## Cybersecurity fundamentals

| Domain | Key controls |
|---|---|
| Identity and access | MFA, least privilege, privileged access management, joiner/mover/leaver process |
| Endpoint and network | EDR, patching, segmentation, secure configuration |
| Data | Encryption at rest and in transit, backups (offline/immutable), data-loss prevention |
| Email and people | Phishing filtering, awareness training, payment-verification procedures |
| Application | Secure development, code review, vulnerability testing |
| Third parties | Vendor security assessments, contractual requirements |
| Detection and response | Logging, SIEM/monitoring, incident response plan, tabletop exercises |
| Governance | Policies, risk assessments, frameworks (ISO/IEC 27001, NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0), board reporting; national regulations (e.g., central-bank and national cybersecurity authority requirements) |

## Technology risk

| Risk | Example | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Project failure | ERP implementation over budget/late | Strong governance, phased rollout, experienced integrator, fixed scope |
| Availability | System outage stops order processing | Redundancy, SLAs, disaster recovery |
| Data integrity | Interface errors corrupt financial data | Reconciliations, validation rules |
| Security | Breach, ransomware | Controls above, insurance |
| Vendor | Vendor failure or price increases | Exit plans, escrow, multi-vendor strategy |
| Obsolescence / technical debt | Unsupported legacy systems | Roadmap, modernisation budget |
| Key-person | Only one person understands a system | Documentation, cross-training |
| Compliance | Data protection, e-invoicing mandates | Compliance-by-design |

:::exercise Practice questions
1. Why must payment APIs be idempotent? Give a failure example.
2. A finance team spends 40 hours a month reconciling bank statements manually across 25 accounts. Outline an automation approach.
:::

:::answer Answers to the practice questions
1. Network failures may cause a client to retry a request; without idempotency (e.g., a unique request key), the bank might execute the same payment twice — a costly and hard-to-recover error.
2. Automate statement retrieval (camt.053 via host-to-host/API), import into the ERP or a reconciliation tool, define matching rules (amount, date, reference, customer/vendor IDs, tolerance), auto-post standard items (bank charges, interest), queue exceptions for review, and produce daily reconciliation status — targeting 90%+ automatic matching.
:::
