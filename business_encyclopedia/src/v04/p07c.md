# Complete Trade Transaction Simulations

:::obj In this chapter
Three end-to-end training simulations at different scales, each with participants, documents, timeline, calculations, decision points, risks and lessons. **All companies, banks, prices, rates and documents are fictional training assumptions**, chosen to be realistic in structure; actual tariffs, taxes, freight rates and fees must be verified for real transactions.
:::

## Simulation 1 — A $5,000 first order from China to Lebanon

### Background

Cedar Kitchenware (fictional), a small Beirut retailer, wants to test a new product line: 500 sets of stainless-steel kitchen utensils from Guangzhou Hongda Housewares (fictional), found through an online B2B platform. Price: **$10.00 per set, FCA supplier’s warehouse Guangzhou, Incoterms® 2020 → $5,000**. The supplier asks for **100% payment before shipment** by bank transfer.

### Participants

Buyer; supplier; buyer’s bank in Beirut; supplier’s bank in China (and a USD correspondent); buyer’s freight forwarder (with an agent in Guangzhou); cargo insurer; Lebanese customs and a customs broker; local trucker.

### Decision point 1: is the supplier real?

| Check | Method | Result (simulated) |
|---|---|---|
| Business licence | Request copy; verify unified social credit code on official registry or via a verification service | Matches name and address |
| Bank account name | Must match the company name exactly | Matches |
| Video call / factory tour | Live walk-through of warehouse and production | Done |
| Samples | 3 sample sets by courier ($65) | Quality acceptable |
| Platform payment protection | Pay through the platform’s escrow/assurance service if available | Supplier accepts at 2% extra cost — buyer declines, pays by T/T after checks |
| Pre-shipment inspection | Third-party inspection ~$250–350 | Not justified at $5,000 (5–7% of value); buyer requests photos/video of packed goods instead |

:::warn Payment-fraud control
Before sending money, confirm the beneficiary’s bank details by a call to a known number (not one in the email). Business email compromise — fake emails announcing “new bank details” — is one of the most common frauds in small import transactions.
:::

### Decision point 2: air or sea?

Shipment: 25 cartons, each 60 × 40 × 40 cm, 16 kg → gross 400 kg; volume 2.4 m³.

| | Air freight | Sea LCL |
|---|---|---|
| Chargeable basis | Volumetric per carton 96,000 ÷ 6,000 = 16 kg = actual → 400 kg | W/M = max(0.4 t, 2.4 m³) = 2.4 |
| Origin charges (pickup, terminal, documents) | $120 | $150 |
| Main freight | 400 kg × $4.20 = $1,680 | 2.4 × $55 = $132 |
| Destination charges (terminal/CFS, delivery order) | $120 | $220 |
| Transit time door to door | ~7 days | ~40 days (with transhipment) |

### Landed cost calculation (assumptions: duty 10% and VAT 11% on CIF-based value; insurance 0.25%, minimum $35; broker $150; local delivery $60; bank charges $65 with OUR)

| Item | Sea LCL ($) | Air ($) |
|---|---|---|
| Goods (FCA) | 5,000.00 | 5,000.00 |
| Origin charges + main freight | 282.00 | 1,800.00 |
| Insurance | 35.00 | 35.00 |
| **Customs value (CIF basis)** | **5,317.00** | **6,835.00** |
| Customs duty 10% | 531.70 | 683.50 |
| Destination charges | 220.00 | 120.00 |
| Customs broker | 150.00 | 150.00 |
| Local delivery | 60.00 | 60.00 |
| Bank transfer charges | 65.00 | 65.00 |
| **Landed cost excluding VAT** | **6,343.70** | **7,913.50** |
| Per set | **12.69** | **15.83** |
| Import VAT 11% on (CIF + duty), cash outflow | 643.36 | 827.04 |

Landed cost is **27% above the supplier price** by sea and **58%** by air. At a target 40% gross margin, the selling price must be $21.15 per set (sea) — or $26.38 (air). The buyer chooses **sea**, accepting the 40-day transit for a trial order.

### Timeline

| Day | Event | Document |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Pro forma invoice received; checks completed | Pro forma invoice |
| 2 | T/T payment $5,000 (OUR) | Bank transfer confirmation (MT103 / pacs.008 reference) |
| 3 | Supplier confirms receipt; production/packing | Order confirmation |
| 12 | Forwarder’s agent collects goods (FCA delivery — risk passes) | Forwarder’s cargo receipt; commercial invoice; packing list |
| 15 | Export declaration filed by supplier; goods at CFS | Export declaration |
| 18 | Vessel departs; house B/L issued to buyer as consignee | House bill of lading |
| 58 | Arrival Beirut; forwarder issues arrival notice | Arrival notice |
| 60 | Customs declaration, duty and VAT paid, release | Import declaration, receipts, certificate of origin if required |
| 61 | Delivery to shop | Delivery note |

### Risks, controls and lessons

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Supplier non-delivery after prepayment | Verification, samples, small first order, platform escrow for higher amounts |
| Quality differs from samples | Photos/video before shipment; specifications in PO; for larger orders, pre-shipment inspection |
| Underestimating landed cost | Full landed-cost calculation before pricing |
| Customs classification/duty error | Ask broker to confirm HS code and duty before ordering |
| Damage in transit | Insurance (ICC A) — cheap relative to value |

**Lesson:** at small scale, the main risks are counterparty fraud and hidden costs; bank instruments such as L/Cs are uneconomic (minimum fees would exceed 5% of value).

## Simulation 2 — A $480,000 import under a usance letter of credit (UAE)

### Background

Gulf Cool Appliances FZE (fictional, Dubai) distributes air-conditioners to retailers in the UAE and Oman. It agrees to buy **1,600 split air-conditioner units (18,000 BTU inverter, model SB-18INV)** from Siam Breeze Manufacturing (fictional, Thailand) at **$300 per unit CFR Jebel Ali, Incoterms® 2020 = $480,000**, payment by **irrevocable letter of credit available by acceptance, drafts at 90 days after B/L date**. The buyer will insure the cargo itself (ICC A). Shipment: 8 × 40ft high-cube containers.

### Participants

Applicant: Gulf Cool. Issuing bank: Training Bank Dubai (fictional). Beneficiary: Siam Breeze. Advising and nominated bank: Training Bank Bangkok (fictional) — no confirmation requested (seller is comfortable with the UAE issuing bank). Carrier: a container line. Inspection: none required by the credit (buyer relies on supplier warranty). Insurer: buyer’s marine open-cover policy. Customs broker and haulier in Dubai.

### The credit (training summary of key fields)

:::doc Documentary Credit — key terms (training simulation)
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Form of credit | Irrevocable |
| Applicable rules | UCP latest version (UCP 600) |
| Date and place of expiry | 30 September, Bangkok |
| Applicant | Gulf Cool Appliances FZE, Jebel Ali Free Zone, Dubai, UAE |
| Beneficiary | Siam Breeze Manufacturing Co., Ltd., Chonburi, Thailand |
| Amount | USD 480,000.00 |
| Percentage tolerance | 5/5 (±5%) |
| Available with / by | Training Bank Bangkok by acceptance |
| Drafts at | 90 days after B/L date, drawn on the issuing bank |
| Partial shipments | Not allowed · Transhipment: Allowed |
| Port of loading / discharge | Laem Chabang, Thailand / Jebel Ali, UAE |
| Latest date of shipment | 15 September |
| Description of goods | 1,600 units split air-conditioners 18,000 BTU inverter, model SB-18INV, CFR Jebel Ali Incoterms® 2020 |
| Documents required | Signed commercial invoice in 3 originals; full set of clean on-board ocean B/Ls made out to order of Training Bank Dubai, notify applicant, marked freight prepaid; packing list; certificate of origin issued by a chamber of commerce; manufacturer’s warranty certificate |
| Additional conditions | Documents must show L/C number; insurance covered by applicant; third-party documents acceptable except invoice and draft |
| Charges | All charges outside UAE for beneficiary’s account |
| Period for presentation | 21 days after date of shipment but within the validity of the credit |
| Confirmation instructions | Without |

*Fictional training document — not a valid instrument.*
:::

### Decision point: should the seller accept these terms?

The seller’s export manager reviews the credit on receipt:

- Latest shipment 15 September vs production completion 5 September — tight but feasible; requests extension to 25 September and expiry to 15 October as buffer → **amendment requested and agreed** (amendment fee $80 for the applicant).
- “Partial shipments not allowed” — all 8 containers must be on one B/L date; the booking must secure all 8 on one vessel.
- Warranty certificate issued by the manufacturer — the seller is the manufacturer, so acceptable.
- Usance 90 days: the seller wants cash at sight → will **discount the accepted draft** with the nominated bank.

### Timeline

| Day | Event |
|---|---|
| 0 | Contract signed |
| 5 | Buyer applies; issuing bank approves under its L/C line (10% cash margin) and issues via SWIFT |
| 6 | Advising bank authenticates; seller reviews credit |
| 9 | Amendment requested; issued day 12 |
| 45 | Production complete; containers stuffed; VGM declared |
| 49 | Containers loaded; on-board B/L dated day 49 |
| 53 | Seller presents documents at Training Bank Bangkok |
| 55 | Nominated bank finds a **discrepancy**: packing-list gross weight 52,480 kg vs B/L 52,840 kg |
| 56 | Seller corrects the packing list (typo) and re-presents within the 21-day period — compliant |
| 58 | Nominated bank accepts the draft (on behalf of the issuing bank) and sends documents; maturity = B/L date + 90 = day 139 |
| 59 | Seller discounts the accepted draft: receives proceeds |
| 63 | Issuing bank receives documents, finds them compliant, releases to applicant against acceptance |
| 70 | Vessel arrives Jebel Ali; buyer obtains delivery order with endorsed B/L; customs clearance; containers delivered by day 73 |
| 139 | Issuing bank debits buyer and pays at maturity |

:::tip What saved the deal
The discrepancy was caught on day 55 with time remaining in the presentation period, so the seller could correct it. Had it been found after the expiry or after 21 days, the seller would have depended on the buyer’s waiver — at a time when the buyer’s market might have weakened.
:::

### Buyer’s calculation

| Item | USD |
|---|---|
| Goods CFR Jebel Ali | 480,000.00 |
| Marine insurance ICC (A): 0.12% × 110% × 480,000 | 633.60 |
| **CIF value** | **480,633.60** |
| Customs duty 5% of CIF (assumed for goods released into the UAE) | 24,031.68 |
| Import VAT 5% on (CIF + duty), recoverable | (25,233.26 cash, recovered later) |
| Port, delivery order, terminal and clearance charges (8 containers) | 2,800.00 |
| Haulage to warehouse 8 × $250 | 2,000.00 |
| L/C issuance commission 0.25% per quarter × 2 quarters | 2,400.00 |
| Acceptance commission 1.5% p.a. × 90/360 | 1,800.00 |
| Amendment and SWIFT/courier | 230.00 |
| **Landed cost excluding VAT** | **513,895.28** |
| **Per unit** | **321.18** |

Sales plan: retailers pay $385 per unit, 30 days after delivery; all units sold over ~60 days.

| Item | USD |
|---|---|
| Revenue 1,600 × 385 | 616,000.00 |
| Landed cost | (513,895.28) |
| **Gross profit (16.6%)** | **102,104.72** |
| Sales commission 1.5% | (9,240.00) |
| Warranty provision 1% | (6,160.00) |
| Financing: average 30-day gap between L/C maturity and collections on ~$480,000 at 7.5% | (3,000.00) |
| **Contribution after direct costs (13.6%)** | **83,704.72** |

The 90-day usance L/C is the key financing: it covers transit (21 days), clearance, and most of the selling and collection period. Without it, the buyer would need ~$480,000 of working capital for roughly 4 months.

### Seller’s calculation

| Item | USD |
|---|---|
| Revenue | 480,000.00 |
| Production cost 1,600 × $258 | (412,800.00) |
| Ocean freight 8 × $1,450 | (11,600.00) |
| Origin charges 8 × $180 + export documents $300 | (1,740.00) |
| Advising fee + discrepancy fee | (200.00) |
| Acceptance/negotiation commission 0.125% | (600.00) |
| Discount of accepted draft: 480,000 × 6.5% × 81/360 (days 58 → 139) | (7,020.00) |
| **Margin before overheads (9.6%)** | **46,040.00** |

### Risks and controls

| Party | Risk | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Seller | Discrepancies → loss of bank undertaking | Credit review on receipt; checklist; pre-check |
| Seller | Issuing bank / UAE country risk | Low here; confirmation available if needed |
| Seller | Discount rate rises | Negotiate “usance payable at sight” (buyer bears discount) or fix discount at acceptance |
| Buyer | Paying for wrong/defective goods (banks check documents only) | Inspection certificate requirement; supplier reputation; warranty |
| Buyer | Price drop in AC market before goods sold | Pre-sell to retailers; limit order size |
| Buyer | D&D charges | Clear quickly; free-time negotiation |
| Issuing bank | Buyer cannot pay at maturity | Credit line approval, 10% margin, title to goods via B/L to its order, trust receipt |

## Simulation 3 — A $25 million export contract with ECA-backed financing

### Background

Anatolia Pipe Works A.Ş. (fictional, Turkey) wins an international tender to supply **ductile iron pipes and fittings** for a water-supply project of the Water Development Authority of the Republic of Kairos (a fictional country with moderate-to-high country risk). Contract price **USD 25,000,000, CIF Port of Kairos, Incoterms® 2020**, four shipments over 12 months. The buyer cannot pay cash: the deal works only with **export-credit-agency (ECA) backed buyer credit**.

### Structure

| Element | Terms (training assumptions) |
|---|---|
| Advance payment | 15% ($3.75m) within 30 days of contract effectiveness, against an **advance-payment guarantee** (APG) from the exporter’s bank, reducing pro rata with shipments |
| Balance | 85% ($21.25m) paid to the exporter by the lending bank from an **ECA-covered buyer credit loan** to the buyer’s Ministry of Finance, disbursed against shipping documents |
| Performance guarantee | 10% ($2.5m), valid until 2 months after final delivery, then replaced by a 5% warranty guarantee for 24 months |
| Liquidated damages | 0.5% of the delayed shipment value per week, capped at 10% of contract value |
| Inspection | Third-party inspection at the plant by the buyer’s engineer before each shipment; inspection certificate required for disbursement |
| Governing law / disputes | English law; arbitration (ICC Rules), seat London |
| Loan terms (buyer side) | 10-year repayment in semi-annual instalments starting 6 months after the final shipment; interest at a fixed rate or floating reference + margin; ECA premium financed |
| Effectiveness conditions | Signing of loan agreement; ECA policy issued; receipt of advance payment; APG issued |

### Participants

Exporter; buyer (Water Authority) and borrower (Ministry of Finance); the exporter’s national ECA (fictional “ExportCover”); lending bank (fictional “Bosphorus Training Bank”) as lender and agent; exporter’s guarantee-issuing bank; buyer’s legal counsel; inspection company; carrier(s) and cargo insurer; customs; the buyer’s engineer.

### Exporter’s project economics

| Cost element | USD m | % of contract |
|---|---|---|
| Raw materials (pig iron, scrap, alloys) — priced in USD/EUR | 12.00 | 48.0% |
| Energy and labour (TRY) | 4.50 | 18.0% |
| Coatings, linings, packaging | 1.50 | 6.0% |
| Sea freight and insurance (CIF) | 1.75 | 7.0% |
| Allocated plant overheads | 1.50 | 6.0% |
| **Total cost** | **21.25** | **85.0%** |
| **Gross margin** | **3.75** | **15.0%** |

Transaction costs:

| Item | Calculation | USD |
|---|---|---|
| APG fee | Outstanding amount × time ≈ 30 USD m-months = 2.5 USD m-years × 1.2% p.a. | 30,000 |
| Performance guarantee fee | 2.5m × 1.0% p.a. × 14/12 | 29,167 |
| Warranty guarantee fee | 1.25m × 0.8% p.a. × 2 years | 20,000 |
| Pre-shipment working-capital finance | Average $8m for 6 months at 8% | 320,000 |
| Legal, ECA processing, inspection coordination | Estimate | 60,000 |
| **Total** | | **459,167** |

Net margin before risks ≈ 3.75m − 0.46m = **$3.29m (13.2%)**.

### Risk sensitivity

| Risk event | Impact on margin |
|---|---|
| One shipment ($6.25m) 4 weeks late | LDs 0.5% × 4 × 6.25m = **−$125,000** |
| EUR strengthens 8% on EUR-priced inputs of $4.0m (unhedged) | **−$320,000** |
| Pig-iron prices +10% on un-fixed half of materials ($6.0m) | **−$600,000** |
| Performance guarantee called unfairly | Up to **−$2.5m** (cash), recoverable only through arbitration |
| Buyer delays inspection, stopping disbursements | Working-capital cost; e.g., 2-month delay on $6.25m at 8% ≈ **−$83,000** |

Actions taken: fixed-price purchase contracts for 70% of pig iron at contract signing; EUR forward purchases for the coating materials; production schedule with 3 weeks of buffer; guarantee wording negotiated under URDG 758 with a requirement for a statement of breach and fixed expiry dates (no “extend or pay”); disbursement mechanism allowing payment against inspection certificate **or** an engineer’s waiver if inspection is not performed within 10 days of notice.

### Buyer’s perspective

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Advance from budget | $3.75m |
| ECA-backed loan | $21.25m |
| ECA premium (illustrative 5% of loan, financed) | ~$1.06m |
| Interest (illustrative fixed 6.5% over a 10-year amortising profile) | ~$7.6m over the life |
| Benefit | Infrastructure delivered now; long-tenor financing unavailable from domestic banks |

### Document chain per shipment (for disbursement)

Commercial invoice; packing list; full set of B/Ls (or as specified in the loan agreement); certificate of origin (ECAs often require minimum national content); inspection certificate; insurance certificate; disbursement request signed by the borrower or an irrevocable authorisation; exporter’s certificate of shipment. The lending bank checks these like an L/C presentation — so discrepancy discipline matters here too.

### Failure scenarios and controls

| Scenario | Control |
|---|---|
| Loan agreement signing delayed → contract never becomes effective while the exporter has started production | Do not start large purchases before effectiveness (or only with limited, cancellable commitments) |
| Country downgrade or sanctions on the buyer country | ECA cover for the bank; exporter’s own political-risk insurance on advance-period exposure |
| Corruption risk in public tender | Anti-bribery due diligence on agents; transparent commission agreements; compliance with home-country and international anti-bribery laws; ECAs require anti-bribery declarations |
| Quality claim after installation | Warranty guarantee capped at 5%; clear specifications and acceptance tests |
| Currency convertibility on local-currency costs (installation supervision) | Price supervision services in USD; keep local spending minimal |

### Lessons across the three simulations

| Lesson | Small ($5k) | Medium ($480k) | Large ($25m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main risk | Counterparty fraud, hidden costs | Documentary compliance, market price | Contract performance, guarantees, country risk, financing |
| Main tool | Verification and landed-cost calculation | L/C with usance financing | ECA buyer credit, guarantees, hedging |
| Cost of security | Low | ~1.5–2% of value | ~2% + financing |
| Critical skill | Due diligence | Document discipline | Structuring and negotiation |

:::answer Answers to the practice questions
1. Duty 20% × 5,317 = 1,063.40 → landed = 6,343.70 + 531.70 = 6,875.40 → **$13.75 per set**.
2. Seller no longer bears $7,020 of discount → margin ≈ **$53,060 (11.1%)**. Buyer bears it → contribution ≈ 83,704.72 − 7,020 = **$76,685 (12.4%)** — a negotiation over who funds the credit period.
3. Demand must be accompanied by a statement of breach (URDG 758 art. 15) and possibly by a certificate from the engineer; fixed expiry date and automatic reduction on delivery milestones; no “extend or pay” clause; counter-guarantee terms aligned; arbitration clause and, where available, injunction relief for manifest fraud; political-risk/unfair-calling insurance (available in some markets, including some ECA bond covers).
4. Example rows: (a) Discrepant documents — medium probability, high impact — checklist, bank pre-check — export documentation manager; (b) Late production — low, high — buffer, amendment request early — production manager; (c) Freight rate increase before shipment (CFR) — medium, medium — fixed booking contract — logistics; (d) Discount rate rise — medium, low — fix discount at acceptance — treasury; (e) Buyer insolvency before acceptance — low, high — issuing-bank undertaking covers; (f) Sanctions/AML hold on payment — low, medium — screening, complete documentation — compliance.

:::summary Part VII summary
- A trade transaction synchronises flows of goods, documents, money and information among many parties.
- Incoterms® 2020 allocate delivery, risk and costs — not title or payment; choose rules that fit the transport mode.
- Documents must be accurate and mutually consistent; banks pay on documents, not goods.
- Customs outcomes depend on classification, valuation and origin; landed cost must include duties, taxes and charges.
- Payment methods trade off risk and cost; UCP 600, URC 522 and URDG 758 define how bank instruments work.
- Most problems — discrepancies, D&D, unfair calls, cargo claims — are preventable through contract design and document discipline.
- Scale changes the risk profile: fraud and hidden costs dominate small deals; structuring, guarantees and country risk dominate large ones.
:::
