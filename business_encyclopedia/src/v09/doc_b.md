## Purchase order

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Buyer’s formal order; becomes a contract when accepted |
| Users | Buyer’s procurement, supplier, warehouse (receiving), accounts payable (three-way match) |
| Important fields | PO number; buyer and supplier details; item codes and descriptions; quantities and units; unit prices and currency; Incoterms rule and named place; delivery dates; payment terms; reference to terms and conditions; approvals |
| Common mistakes | Wrong units of measure; missing Incoterms; conflicting terms with supplier’s acknowledgement; issued after delivery (“retrospective POs”) |
| Consequences | Disputes over price and delivery; failed three-way match; control weakness |

## Supplier quotation

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Supplier’s price and terms offer |
| Users | Buyer’s procurement and finance; supplier’s sales |
| Important fields | Validity; price basis (Incoterms); lead time; MOQ; payment terms; exclusions; price-adjustment conditions; specifications offered vs requested |
| Common mistakes (buyer side) | Comparing quotes on different Incoterms or specs; ignoring validity and exclusions |
| Consequences | Choosing an apparently cheap offer that costs more |

## Sales contract (supply of goods)

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Binding agreement for the sale of goods |
| Users | Seller, buyer, banks (for L/C consistency), arbitrators |
| Important fields | Parties; goods and specifications; quantity and tolerance; price, currency and adjustments; Incoterms rule; delivery schedule; inspection and acceptance; payment terms and security; documents; title and risk; warranties and claims procedure; LDs; force majeure; sanctions/compliance; limitation of liability; termination; law and disputes |
| Common mistakes | Payment terms inconsistent with the L/C; no claims procedure or time limits; vague quality standard; missing title-transfer clause |
| Consequences | Unenforceable claims; documentary discrepancies; disputes |

:::doc Sales contract — key clauses (training extract)
**Contract No. NC-SA-2026-03** between Nile Ceramics (fictional), Seller, and Desert Homes Retail (fictional), Buyer.
1. **Goods:** porcelain floor tiles, sizes and grades per Annex 1 (first grade per ISO 13006 classification as specified).
2. **Quantity:** 1,500,000 m² per contract year; minimum 1,200,000 m²; monthly call-offs with 45 days’ notice; ±5% per shipment.
3. **Price:** USD 4.00/m² CFR Dammam, Incoterms® 2020, fixed for 12 months; annual adjustment per Annex 2 (gas and freight indices, 50/50 sharing beyond ±8%).
4. **Payment:** 90 days from B/L date by bank transfer; Buyer to maintain an SBLC for USD 600,000 in Seller’s favour.
5. **Inspection:** pre-shipment inspection by Buyer’s nominated inspector; certificate final as to quality and quantity at loading, except for latent defects notified within 6 months.
6. **Title:** title passes on payment in full (subject to applicable law); risk passes per Incoterms.
7. **Claims:** written notice within 30 days of discharge with surveyor’s report; Seller to replace or credit.
8. **Performance guarantee:** 5% of annual value, URDG 758, demand to be supported by a statement of breach.
9. **Force majeure:** per ICC Force Majeure Clause (as adapted), excluding payment obligations.
10. **Compliance:** anti-bribery; sanctions; product conformity at Seller’s cost.
11. **Limitation:** neither party liable for indirect or consequential loss; total liability capped at contract-year value, except fraud and wilful misconduct.
12. **Law and disputes:** English law; arbitration under the rules of a named institution; seat in a neutral jurisdiction.
:::

## Distribution agreement

Key clauses (see {{ch:Commercial Agreements and Intellectual Property}}): appointment and territory; exclusivity and conditions; products; minimum targets; prices and payment; orders and delivery; marketing and MDF; trademarks; service and spare parts; reporting; competition-law compliance; term and termination; consequences of termination (stock buy-back, transition); law and disputes.

:::doc Distribution agreement — extract (training)
**Clause 4 — Exclusivity and minimum purchases.** 4.1 The Principal appoints the Distributor as its exclusive distributor of the Products in the Territory for the Initial Term, provided the Distributor purchases at least the Minimum Annual Quantities in Schedule 3. 4.2 If the Distributor fails to purchase the Minimum Annual Quantity in any Contract Year, the Principal may, by 60 days’ written notice, (a) convert the appointment to non-exclusive, or (b) terminate this Agreement, unless the shortfall is cured within the notice period.
**Annotation:** links exclusivity to performance and gives the principal proportionate remedies; check mandatory local rules on termination of distributors/agents.
:::

## Non-disclosure agreement

Key elements are listed in {{ch:Commercial Agreements and Intellectual Property}}.

:::doc Mutual NDA — core operative clause (training)
“Each Party (the ‘Recipient’) shall keep confidential all Confidential Information disclosed by the other Party (the ‘Discloser’) and shall use it solely for the Purpose. The Recipient may disclose Confidential Information only to its directors, employees and professional advisers who need to know it for the Purpose and who are bound by obligations of confidentiality no less protective than this Agreement. These obligations do not apply to information that (a) is or becomes public other than through breach, (b) was lawfully known to the Recipient before disclosure, (c) is independently developed, or (d) is required to be disclosed by law, provided the Recipient gives prompt notice where lawful. These obligations last for three years from the date of this Agreement.”
:::

## Bill of lading

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Receipt for goods, evidence of the contract of carriage, and (if negotiable) document of title |
| Users | Shipper, consignee, banks, carrier, customs, insurers |
| Important fields | Shipper; consignee (named / to order / to order of bank); notify party; vessel and voyage; ports; place of receipt/delivery; description, marks, packages, weights; freight prepaid/collect; number of originals; on-board notation and date; place and date of issue; carrier identification and signature |
| Common mistakes | Consignee wrongly named for L/C; “freight collect” under CFR/CIF; claused (unclean) B/L; wrong port names; missing on-board date |
| Consequences | Discrepancies; loss of control of goods; delivery to the wrong party |

## Insurance certificate

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Evidence that goods are insured under a cargo policy, enabling claims by the holder |
| Users | Buyer, seller (under CIF/CIP), banks |
| Important fields | Insured/assured; certificate number; insured value (e.g., CIF + 10%) and currency; conveyance and voyage; goods description; conditions (ICC A/B/C, war, strikes); claims payable at; surveyor/settling agent; date of issue; endorsement |
| Common mistakes | Insured value below 110%; dated after shipment; wrong clauses; not endorsed |
| Consequences | Uncovered losses; L/C discrepancy |

|---|---|
| Purpose | Show the full economics of a physical commodity deal, including hedging and all costs |

:::exercise Practice questions on commercial and trade documents
1. List four fields you would cross-check between a commercial invoice, packing list and B/L before an L/C presentation.
2. A PO says “Delivery: ASAP”. What problems can this cause?
3. An insurance certificate is dated two days after the on-board date. Is that a problem?
:::

:::answer Answers to the practice questions on commercial and trade documents
1. Goods description; quantities/packages; gross and net weights; marks and numbers; shipper/consignee/notify parties; ports; container numbers; Incoterms and freight notation.
2. No enforceable delivery date, so no basis for late-delivery remedies, planning conflicts, and disputes; specify dates or periods.
3. Yes, potentially: under UCP 600 the insurance document must be dated no later than shipment unless it shows cover effective from no later than the date of shipment; otherwise it is a discrepancy — and goods may have been uninsured during loading.
:::
