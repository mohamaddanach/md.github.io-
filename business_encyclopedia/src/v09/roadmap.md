# The Six-Month Learning Roadmap

:::obj In this chapter
A 26-week plan with weekly objectives, study time, chapters, terminology targets, exercises, cases, reviews, self-assessments and projects, plus guidance on separating academic knowledge from practical skill.
:::

## Design of the programme

- **Time budget:** 8–10 hours per week (about 90 minutes on weekdays plus a longer weekend session). Weeks marked *review* are lighter (5–6 hours).
- **Weekly rhythm:** read and annotate (40%), terminology drill EN/AR (15%), exercises (25%), case or document work (20%).
- **Terminology target:** about 25–40 new terms a week, totalling roughly 800 terms by week 26. Use the Volume 10 glossary to make flashcards; review with spaced repetition (day 1, 3, 7, 21).
- **Monthly checkpoint:** score yourself with the personal knowledge assessment framework in Part XXIII and adjust the next month.

:::note Academic knowledge vs practical skill
Each week below separates **Academic** objectives (understand concepts, definitions, models — what exams test) from **Practical** objectives (produce something a manager, bank or client would use: a calculation, a document, an email, a recommendation). Employers judge mainly the practical column; the academic column makes it reliable.
:::

## Capstone project

Choose a real listed company in an industry you want to work in and produce, using public information only: (1) a two-page business and financial analysis with ratios for three years; (2) a simple DCF and peer-multiple valuation; (3) a mock credit memo for a facility request; (4) a trade transaction file for one of its typical import or export flows (Incoterms, documents, payment method, landed cost); (5) a risk register with ten risks; (6) a five-slide executive presentation and a cover email in English, with an Arabic executive summary. Have a professional review it if possible.

## Reviews and self-assessments

- **Weekly (30 minutes):** redo three exercises from the previous week without notes; review flashcards due.
- **Monthly (2 hours):** self-test (terms + calculations + one case question), then score the knowledge framework.
- **After each project:** compare against the assessment criteria in the relevant chapter and the Document Library templates; list three improvements.

# Final Assessment

:::obj In this chapter
A comprehensive assessment of nine competencies — business vocabulary, financial literacy, accounting, banking, international trade, investment analysis, risk assessment, business communication and strategic decision-making — with an answer key and scoring guide.
:::

## Instructions

Allow **three hours**. Use a calculator; do not use notes. Each section is worth 20 marks (total 180). Grades: ≥ 150 excellent; 120–149 competent; 90–119 developing; < 90 repeat the weakest months of the roadmap. All companies and figures are fictional.

## Questions

:::exercise Section A — Business vocabulary (20 marks)
1. Define and translate into Arabic: (a) deferred revenue; (b) negative pledge; (c) demurrage; (d) take-or-pay; (e) basis point. (10)
2. Explain the difference in each pair: (a) EBITDA vs operating cash flow; (b) bill of lading vs sea waybill; (c) hedging vs speculation; (d) gross vs net leverage; (e) recourse vs non-recourse factoring. (10)
:::

:::exercise Section B — Financial literacy (20 marks)
3. A product costs 42. What price gives a 30% margin? What markup is that? (4)
4. 250,000 is invested at 6% compounded annually for 5 years. Find the final value. (4)
5. A loan of 60,000 at 9% for 90 days on actual/360. Compute interest. (4)
6. Fixed costs 180,000; price 50; variable cost 30. Compute break-even units and operating leverage at 12,000 units. (8)
:::

:::exercise Section C — Accounting (20 marks)
7. Record the entries: goods bought on credit 20,000; sold half for 16,000 cash; paid supplier 12,000. (6)
8. Net income 120,000; depreciation 40,000; receivables up 30,000; inventory down 10,000; payables down 5,000. Compute CFO. (6)
9. Explain why a profitable company can run out of cash, giving two mechanisms and the statement where each appears. (8)
:::

:::exercise Section D — Banking (20 marks)
10. A company has EBITDA 8m, debt 26m, cash 4m and annual debt service of 5m; CFADS is 6.5m. Compute net leverage and DSCR. (6)
11. Covenants are net leverage ≤ 3.0× and DSCR ≥ 1.25×. By what percentage can EBITDA fall before the leverage covenant is breached (debt constant)? (6)
12. Recommend a facility structure for a distributor with seasonal inventory peaks and a planned warehouse purchase. (8)
:::

:::exercise Section E — International trade (20 marks)
13. Under CIF Jebel Ali, who arranges insurance, and to what minimum cover under Incoterms® 2020? Where does risk pass? (6)
14. FOB 80,000; freight 6,000; insurance 300; duty 5% on CIF; clearance 1,200; units 4,000. Compute landed cost per unit (VAT recoverable). (6)
15. An L/C demands shipment by 30 June and presentation within 21 days of shipment; the B/L is dated 28 June, documents presented 22 July. Is there a discrepancy? What can the beneficiary do? (8)
:::

:::exercise Section F — Investment analysis (20 marks)
16. Price a 2-year 5% annual-coupon bond (face 1,000) at a 6% yield. (6)
17. EBITDA 30m; net debt 60m; 20m shares; peer EV/EBITDA 7×. Value per share? (6)
18. Explain why two companies with the same P/E may deserve different valuations. Give three reasons. (8)
:::

:::exercise Section G — Risk assessment (20 marks)
19. PD 2%, LGD 50%, EAD 1.5m. Compute EL. (4)
20. An exporter will receive EUR 2m in 6 months; the EUR/USD forward is 1.1000. Compute USD proceeds hedged and unhedged at 1.05 and 1.15; recommend. (8)
21. Build a five-line risk register for a new import business (risk, cause, impact, likelihood, response). (8)
:::

:::exercise Section H — Business communication (20 marks)
22. Write an email (≤ 150 words) to a supplier rejecting a 9% price increase and proposing a meeting. (10)
23. Give the Arabic equivalent of: “Please find attached”, “at your earliest convenience”, “subject to contract”, “we regret to inform you”, “going forward”. (5)
24. Write a three-sentence executive summary of the Section D company’s situation. (5)
:::

:::exercise Section I — Strategic decision-making (20 marks)
25. A competitor cuts prices by 10%. Your gross margin is 35% and demand elasticity for your product is estimated at −1.5. Should you match? Show the effect on gross profit of matching (assume volume restored) vs not matching (lose 15% of volume). (10)
26. Recommend whether a Gulf-based trading firm should enter a new market through an agent, a distributor or a subsidiary. State your criteria and decision. (10)
:::

## Answer key

:::answer Sections A–C
1. (a) Cash received before revenue is earned; a liability — إيراد مؤجل. (b) Borrower’s promise not to grant security over its assets to other creditors — تعهد بعدم رهن الأصول. (c) Charge for keeping a carrier’s container or vessel beyond free time — غرامة التأخير. (d) Buyer pays for minimum quantity whether taken or not — الاستلام أو الدفع. (e) 0.01% — نقطة أساس. (2 each)
2. (a) EBITDA ignores working capital, tax and interest timing; operating cash flow includes them. (b) A B/L to order is a document of title and negotiable; a sea waybill is not. (c) Hedging offsets an existing exposure; speculation creates exposure for profit. (d) Gross leverage uses total debt; net leverage deducts cash. (e) With recourse, the seller bears buyer default; without, the factor does. (2 each)
3. Price = 42 ÷ 0.70 = **60**; markup = 18 ÷ 42 = **42.9%**.
4. 250,000 × 1.06⁵ = 250,000 × 1.338226 = **334,556**.
5. 60,000 × 0.09 × 90/360 = **1,350**.
6. Contribution 20 → break-even **9,000 units**. At 12,000: contribution 240,000, EBIT 60,000 → DOL **4.0×**.
7. Dr Inventory 20,000 / Cr AP 20,000; Dr Cash 16,000 / Cr Revenue 16,000 and Dr COGS 10,000 / Cr Inventory 10,000; Dr AP 12,000 / Cr Cash 12,000.
8. CFO = 120,000 + 40,000 − 30,000 + 10,000 − 5,000 = **135,000**.
9. Examples: growth tying up cash in receivables and inventory (cash-flow statement, working-capital lines; balance sheet); capital expenditure or debt repayment exceeding operating cash (investing and financing sections); revenue recognised before cash collected (income statement vs cash flow). (4 per mechanism)
:::

:::answer Sections D–F
10. Net debt 22m → **2.75×**; DSCR = 6.5 ÷ 5 = **1.30×**.
11. Breach when EBITDA < 22 ÷ 3 = 7.33m → fall of **8.3%**.
12. Revolving credit facility (or borrowing-base facility on inventory and receivables) sized to the seasonal peak, with clean-down or borrowing-base controls; separate amortising term loan or mortgage for the warehouse matched to its life; trade lines (L/C, import loans) for supplier purchases; covenants on leverage and DSCR. (Marks for matching tenor to use and for security/controls.)
13. Seller arranges insurance; minimum cover Institute Cargo Clauses (C) at 110% of the contract value; risk passes on board the vessel at the port of shipment (not Jebel Ali). (2 each)
14. CIF 86,300; duty 4,315; landed = 86,300 + 4,315 + 1,200 = 91,815 → **22.95 per unit**.
15. Presentation deadline is 19 July (21 days after 28 June); 22 July is late (assuming the credit is still valid, presentation after the period is a discrepancy). The beneficiary can ask the applicant to waive via the issuing bank, or send the documents on a collection basis; payment then depends on the applicant. (8)
16. 50 ÷ 1.06 + 1,050 ÷ 1.1236 = 47.17 + 934.50 = **981.67**.
17. EV 210m; equity 150m → **7.50 per share**.
18. Differences in growth prospects, risk (leverage, cyclicality), return on capital and cash conversion, accounting quality, and one-off items in earnings. (Any three well explained.)
:::

:::answer Sections G–I
19. EL = 0.02 × 0.5 × 1.5m = **15,000**.
20. Hedged: **2.20m USD**. Unhedged: at 1.05 → 2.10m; at 1.15 → 2.30m. Recommend hedging most of the committed receivable with a forward (or an option if the receipt is uncertain), because the firm’s margin should not depend on FX. (8)
21. Example lines: supplier non-delivery (advance paid) → loss of deposit → medium/high → L/C or staged payments; FX on USD purchases → margin erosion → high → forwards; customs misclassification → penalties/delays → medium → broker review, binding ruling; customer non-payment → bad debt → medium → credit limits, insurance; shipping delays/demurrage → cost and stockouts → medium → buffer stock, free-time negotiation. (Marks for causes, impact and specific responses.)
22. Criteria: polite opening, clear rejection with reasons (index data, volume history), counter-proposal or willingness to discuss, specific meeting proposal with dates, professional close. (10)
23. «مرفق طيه»، «في أقرب وقت ممكن لكم»، «رهناً بإبرام العقد»، «نأسف لإبلاغكم»، «من الآن فصاعداً». (1 each)
24. Example: “The company’s net leverage is 2.75× and DSCR 1.30×, both within covenants but with limited headroom: an 8% EBITDA decline would breach the leverage covenant. We recommend no additional debt until EBITDA grows or cash builds, and close monitoring of quarterly results. Any new facility should include a cash sweep.” (5)
25. Base: price 100, cost 65, gross profit 35 per unit. Match: price 90, GP 25 per unit, volume restored → GP falls **28.6%**. Not matching: GP 35 on 85% of volume = 29.75 → GP falls **15%**. Not matching is better on these numbers, unless losing volume damages scale, customer relationships or long-term share; respond with value arguments, targeted offers to at-risk customers, and cost reduction. (10)
26. Criteria: market size and certainty, control needs, investment and risk appetite, speed, local legal requirements (some countries restrict foreign ownership or protect commercial agents — jurisdiction-specific), knowledge of the market. Typical decision: start with a distributor or agent to test demand at low investment, with KPIs and a termination path that respects local agency law; move to a subsidiary when volume and margins justify fixed costs. (Marks for reasoning, not for a particular choice.)
:::
