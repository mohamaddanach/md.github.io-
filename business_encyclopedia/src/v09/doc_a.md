This library contains annotated educational templates of the documents professionals meet most often. For each document you will find its **purpose**, its **users**, the **important fields**, **common mistakes** and the **commercial consequences** of getting it wrong, followed by a training template.

:::warn Important
Every document in this library is a **fictional training template**. Names, companies, banks, numbers and references are invented. The templates are not legally valid instruments, must not be used as if issued by any bank, carrier, chamber of commerce, insurer, government or other institution, and do not imitate any real organisation’s forms. For regulated or jurisdiction-specific documents (letters of credit, bills of lading, certificates of origin, insurance certificates, customs documents, contracts), verify the current legal, regulatory and institutional requirements and use the official forms of the relevant institution before any real use.
:::

:::obj Learning objectives for the Document Library
- Recognise each document and know who issues and who relies on it.
- Read every important field and understand its commercial consequence.
- Prepare internal documents (plans, budgets, reports, memos, minutes) to a professional standard.
- Check external documents for consistency and risk.
:::

# Planning, Financial and Management Documents

## Business plan

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Explain the opportunity, strategy, operations and financial plan to obtain funding, partners or internal approval |
| Users | Founders, investors, banks, grant bodies, boards |
| Important fields | Executive summary; market and customer evidence; business model; competition; go-to-market; operations; team; risks; financial plan with assumptions; funding request |
| Common mistakes | Top-down market sizes; no evidence of demand; hockey-stick forecasts without drivers; ignoring working capital; vague use of funds |
| Consequences | Rejected funding; under-capitalisation; credibility loss |

:::doc Business plan — outline template (Cedar Kitchenware, fictional)
1. **Executive summary** (1 page): what we do, for whom, why now, traction, financials summary, funding needed and use.
2. **Problem and opportunity:** customer pains with evidence (interviews, data).
3. **Solution and value proposition:** product, differentiation, pricing.
4. **Market:** segments; bottom-up SAM/SOM; trends.
5. **Competition:** alternatives; our advantages; risks of imitation.
6. **Business model and unit economics:** revenue streams; contribution per order/customer; CAC; LTV.
7. **Go-to-market:** channels, sales process, partnerships, marketing plan and budget.
8. **Operations:** sourcing, logistics, suppliers, systems, quality, licences.
9. **Team and organisation:** founders, key hires, advisers, governance.
10. **Financial plan:** assumptions table; 3–5-year P&L, balance sheet, cash flow; break-even; funding requirement; scenarios.
11. **Risks and mitigation.**
12. **Funding request:** amount, instrument, use of funds, milestones.
13. **Appendices:** CVs, market data, customer letters, detailed model.
:::

## Financial forecast

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Project future results and cash needs based on explicit drivers |
| Users | Management, lenders, investors, treasury |
| Important fields | Assumptions (volumes, prices, costs, working-capital days, capex, financing); P&L; balance sheet; cash flow; KPIs; scenarios |
| Common mistakes | No link between P&L and cash; constant working capital with growing sales; no downside case |

See the full example in {{ch:Performance Management}}. Purpose: explain performance against budget and prior year, forecast the year, highlight risks and request decisions. ## Financial model

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Integrated, driver-based projection for decisions (investment, financing, valuation) |
| Users | Analysts, management, banks, investors |
| Important fields | Cover/contents; inputs and scenarios; calculations (revenue build, costs, working capital, capex, debt, tax); statements; outputs (KPIs, valuation, covenants); checks |
| Common mistakes | Hard-coded numbers; circularity errors; balance sheet not balancing; no version control |
| Consequences | Wrong decisions with false precision |

:::doc Financial model — sheet structure (template)
| Sheet | Contents | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Cover | Purpose, version, author, date, status | Version log |
| Inputs | All assumptions by scenario (base/upside/downside); switch cell | Blue font for inputs; units and sources |
| Revenue | Volume × price by product/region | One formula per row |
| Costs | Variable and fixed costs; headcount | Linked to inputs |
| Working capital | DSO, DIO, DPO → balances | Monthly for seasonal businesses |
| Fixed assets | Capex schedule; depreciation | By asset class |
| Debt | Facilities, drawdowns, repayments, interest; revolver plug | Circularity switch |
| Tax | Taxable profit; losses carried forward; payment timing | — |
| Statements | P&L, balance sheet, cash flow | Balance check = 0 |
| Outputs | KPIs, covenants, NPV/IRR, charts | Clear labels |
| Checks | Summary of all error checks | Must show “OK” |
:::

## Project budget

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Estimate and control the cost of a project |
| Users | Sponsor, project manager, finance, steering committee |
| Important fields | Work packages; one-off vs recurring costs; internal vs external; capex vs opex; phasing; contingency; funding approvals |
| Common mistakes | Omitting internal staff costs, data migration, training; no contingency; no link to benefits |

## Board presentation

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Inform the board and obtain decisions |
| Users | Directors, CEO, CFO, company secretary |
| Important fields | Decision requested; executive summary; context; options and analysis; financial impact; risks; recommendation; resolution wording |
| Common mistakes | Too many slides; decision buried; no alternatives; no risk analysis |
| Consequences | Delayed or poor decisions; governance weaknesses |

:::doc Board paper — cover template (fictional)
**Board of Directors — Gulf Industrial Supplies (fictional)** · Meeting of 20 May · Agenda item 4 · **For decision**
**Title:** Approval of USD 2.2m cold-storage warehouse in Dammam
**Decision requested:** Approve capital expenditure of USD 2.2m and a USD 1.5m term-loan drawdown.
**Executive summary:** NPV USD 0.28m at 11% hurdle; IRR 15.5%; payback 3.7 years; key risk third-party occupancy, mitigated by pre-letting 50% before construction.
**Options considered:** (1) continue renting (rising costs); (2) build (recommended); (3) long-term lease of an existing facility (limited availability).
**Financial impact:** EBITDA +USD 0.65m from year 2; leverage peaks at 2.6× (covenant 3.0×).
**Risks:** construction overrun; occupancy; energy costs — mitigations in appendix.
**Proposed resolution:** “The Board approves the construction of the Dammam cold-storage facility at a cost not exceeding USD 2.2m and authorises the CEO and CFO to sign the construction contract and term-loan drawdown.”
:::

## Risk register

See the template in {{ch:Enterprise Risk Management}}. Key fields: ID, risk description (cause–event–consequence), likelihood, impact, score, existing controls, actions, owner, due date, KRI, status. Common mistakes: vague risks (“market risk”), no owners, never updated.

## Meeting minutes

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Record decisions, actions and key discussions |
| Users | Participants, absent stakeholders, auditors, regulators (board minutes) |
| Important fields | Date, attendees, apologies, agenda items, decisions, action items with owners and deadlines, next meeting |
| Common mistakes | Transcript-style minutes; unclear decisions; no owners |
| Consequences | Disputes about what was agreed; actions not done; governance gaps |

## Professional business email

See the templates in {{ch:Professional Writing}}: subject with topic and action; purpose in the first sentence; facts; request with deadline; courteous close. Common mistakes: vague subjects, buried requests, emotional tone, commitments beyond authority.

:::exercise Practice questions on planning and management documents
1. Identify three weaknesses in a business plan whose market section says “The global food market is worth USD 9 trillion; capturing 0.001% gives us USD 90m.”
2. In the annual budget template, why might utilities peak in Q3?
3. Rewrite this minute: “Discussed receivables. Will follow up.”
:::

:::answer Answers to the practice questions on planning and management documents
1. Top-down TAM with no relevance to the actual serviceable market; no evidence of customer demand or reachable channels; ignores competition and pricing; no bottom-up build (customers × purchases × price).
2. Summer cooling loads in hot climates (and possibly higher production for seasonal demand).
3. “Receivables over 120 days total USD 1.6m (two contractors). Decision: no new orders to these customers without payment security. Action: Credit Manager to request SBLCs and report status by 15 June.”
:::
