## Set 1 — Business terminology

:::exercise Set 1 — Terminology questions
**Easy**

1. Define in one English sentence and one Arabic sentence each: accrual, collateral, consignee, covenant, working capital.
2. Expand and translate: DSO, EBITDA, UBO, L/C, FOB.

**Intermediate**

3. Explain the difference within each pair: (a) margin vs markup; (b) liquidity vs solvency; (c) revenue vs cash receipts; (d) guarantee vs standby L/C; (e) consignor vs consignee.
4. A colleague writes: “The customer’s covenants are fine because we hold good collateral.” What confusion does this sentence reveal?

**Advanced**

5. Read the message below, list every technical term, and rewrite it in plain Arabic for a non-specialist manager.
“Bank confirmed the RCF renewal at SOFR + 225 bps, subject to a 3.0× net leverage covenant tested semi-annually and a negative pledge. The CP list includes the audited accounts and a board resolution.”
:::

:::answer Set 1 — Solutions
1. **Accrual**: recognising revenue or expense when earned or incurred, not when cash moves — الاستحقاق: تسجيل الإيراد أو المصروف عند تحققه لا عند تحصيله أو دفعه. **Collateral**: an asset pledged to secure a debt — الضمان العيني: أصل مرهون لضمان سداد الدين. **Consignee**: the party to whom goods are shipped — المرسل إليه: الطرف الذي تُشحن إليه البضاعة. **Covenant**: a promise in a loan agreement to do or not do something or to meet financial tests — التعهد: التزام في اتفاقية القرض بالقيام بعمل أو الامتناع عنه أو تحقيق نسب مالية. **Working capital**: current assets minus current liabilities, the funds tied up in daily operations — رأس المال العامل: الأصول المتداولة ناقص الخصوم المتداولة.
2. DSO = Days Sales Outstanding (أيام التحصيل); EBITDA = Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortisation (الأرباح قبل الفوائد والضرائب والإهلاك والإطفاء); UBO = Ultimate Beneficial Owner (المستفيد الحقيقي); L/C = Letter of Credit (الاعتماد المستندي); FOB = Free on Board (التسليم على ظهر السفينة).
3. (a) Margin divides profit by price; markup divides profit by cost. (b) Liquidity is the ability to pay short-term obligations as they fall due; solvency is assets exceeding liabilities over the long term. (c) Revenue is recognised when performance obligations are met; cash receipts are actual collections, which may come earlier (deposits) or later (credit sales). (d) Both are bank undertakings to pay on default; demand guarantees usually follow URDG 758 and local law, standbys usually ISP98 or UCP 600 and are common in US practice. (e) The consignor sends the goods; the consignee receives them.
4. It confuses **covenants** (behavioural and financial promises that give the lender early warning and control) with **collateral** (an asset to recover from after default). Good collateral does not cure a covenant breach; covenant compliance is assessed separately.
5. Terms: bank confirmation, RCF (revolving credit facility), renewal, SOFR (reference interest rate), 225 bps (2.25%), net leverage covenant (net debt ÷ EBITDA), tested semi-annually, negative pledge, CP (condition precedent), audited accounts, board resolution. Plain Arabic: «وافق البنك على تجديد خط الائتمان المتجدد بسعر فائدة يساوي السعر المرجعي SOFR مضافاً إليه 2.25٪، بشرط ألا يتجاوز صافي الدين ثلاثة أضعاف الأرباح التشغيلية قبل الإهلاك، ويُفحص ذلك كل ستة أشهر، وبشرط ألا نرهن أصولنا لجهات أخرى. وقبل السحب يجب تقديم القوائم المالية المدققة وقرار من مجلس الإدارة.»
:::

## Set 2 — Business calculations

:::exercise Set 2 — Calculation questions
**Easy**

1. A product costs 80 and sells for 100. Compute margin and markup.
2. Cost is 65. What price gives a 35% gross margin?

**Intermediate**

3. A loan of 100,000 at 8% per year is repaid in five equal annual instalments. Compute the instalment and total interest.
4. Revenue grew from 4.0m to 6.5m in four years. Compute the CAGR.

**Advanced**

5. EUR/USD spot is 1.0800. The 90-day USD rate is 4.5% and the EUR rate 2.5% (actual/360). Compute the 90-day forward rate and explain why it is above spot.
6. Fixed costs are 240,000, price 60 and variable cost 36 per unit. Compute break-even units and the units needed for a profit of 60,000. What is the margin of safety at 15,000 units?
:::

:::answer Set 2 — Solutions
1. Profit 20. Margin = 20 ÷ 100 = **20%**; markup = 20 ÷ 80 = **25%**.
2. Price = 65 ÷ (1 − 0.35) = **100**.
3. Instalment = 100,000 × 0.08 ÷ [1 − 1.08⁻⁵] = 8,000 ÷ (1 − 0.680583) = 8,000 ÷ 0.319417 = **25,045.65**. Total paid 125,228.25 → total interest **25,228.25**.
4. CAGR = (6.5 ÷ 4.0)^(1/4) − 1 = 1.625^0.25 − 1 = **12.9%**.
5. F = 1.0800 × (1 + 0.045 × 90/360) ÷ (1 + 0.025 × 90/360) = 1.0800 × 1.01125 ÷ 1.00625 = **1.0854**. The currency with the higher interest rate (USD) trades at a forward discount, so more USD are needed per EUR forward: interest-rate parity removes the arbitrage of borrowing EUR and investing USD.
6. Contribution per unit = 24. Break-even = 240,000 ÷ 24 = **10,000 units**. Target profit: (240,000 + 60,000) ÷ 24 = **12,500 units**. Margin of safety at 15,000 units = (15,000 − 10,000) ÷ 15,000 = **33.3%**.
:::

## Set 3 — Accounting

:::exercise Set 3 — Accounting questions
**Easy**

1. Record the journal entries: (a) goods costing 6,000 are sold on credit for 10,000; (b) twelve months’ rent of 12,000 is paid in advance — record the payment and the first month’s expense; (c) a machine is bought for 50,000 cash, life five years, no residual value — record the purchase and annual depreciation.

**Intermediate**

2. Revenue 500,000; cost of goods sold 300,000; operating expenses (excluding depreciation) 120,000; depreciation 20,000; interest 10,000; tax rate 20%. Prepare the income statement and compute EBITDA.
3. Using question 2, receivables increased by 15,000, inventory by 10,000 and payables by 8,000. Compute cash flow from operations (indirect method).

**Advanced**

4. On 1 October a customer pays 36,000 for a twelve-month subscription. Year-end is 31 December. How much revenue is recognised this year and what is on the balance sheet?
5. Receivables ageing: current 150,000 (loss rate 1%); 31–60 days 30,000 (5%); 61–90 days 15,000 (15%); over 90 days 5,000 (50%). Compute the expected credit loss allowance. If last year’s allowance was 6,000, what is this year’s expense?
:::

:::answer Set 3 — Solutions
1. (a) Dr Accounts receivable 10,000 / Cr Revenue 10,000; Dr Cost of goods sold 6,000 / Cr Inventory 6,000. (b) Dr Prepaid rent 12,000 / Cr Cash 12,000; month 1: Dr Rent expense 1,000 / Cr Prepaid rent 1,000. (c) Dr Machinery 50,000 / Cr Cash 50,000; each year: Dr Depreciation expense 10,000 / Cr Accumulated depreciation 10,000.
2. Gross profit 200,000; EBITDA = 200,000 − 120,000 = **80,000**; EBIT = 80,000 − 20,000 = 60,000; EBT = 50,000; tax 10,000; **net income 40,000**.
3. CFO = 40,000 + 20,000 − 15,000 − 10,000 + 8,000 = **43,000**.
4. Three months earned: **9,000 revenue**; **deferred revenue (contract liability) 27,000**; cash 36,000.
5. ECL = 1,500 + 1,500 + 2,250 + 2,500 = **7,750**. Expense = increase in allowance = 7,750 − 6,000 = **1,750** (assuming no write-offs during the year; write-offs used against the allowance would increase the expense).
:::

## Set 4 — Company analysis

Data for **Delta Distribution Co.** (fictional, USD millions):

| Item | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 40.0 | 46.0 |
| Cost of goods sold | 28.0 | 33.6 |
| EBITDA | 6.0 | 6.2 |
| Net income | 3.0 | 2.8 |
| Trade receivables | 6.6 | 9.5 |
| Inventory | 5.0 | 7.6 |
| Trade payables | 4.0 | 4.2 |
| Total debt | 10.0 | 16.0 |
| Cash | 2.0 | 1.5 |

:::exercise Set 4 — Company analysis questions
**Easy**

1. Compute revenue growth and gross margin for both years.

**Intermediate**

2. Compute DSO, DIO, DPO and the cash conversion cycle for both years (365 days, year-end balances).
3. Compute net debt and net debt/EBITDA for both years.

**Advanced**

4. Explain the story behind the numbers in five sentences, quantify the working-capital build, and list four questions you would ask management before lending more.
:::

:::answer Set 4 — Solutions
1. Growth = 46 ÷ 40 − 1 = **15%**. Gross margin 2024 = 12.0 ÷ 40 = **30.0%**; 2025 = 12.4 ÷ 46 = **27.0%**.
2. 2024: DSO = 6.6 ÷ 40 × 365 = 60.2; DIO = 5.0 ÷ 28 × 365 = 65.2; DPO = 4.0 ÷ 28 × 365 = 52.1; **CCC = 73.3 days**. 2025: DSO = 75.4; DIO = 7.6 ÷ 33.6 × 365 = 82.6; DPO = 4.2 ÷ 33.6 × 365 = 45.6; **CCC = 112.4 days**.
3. Net debt 2024 = 8.0 → **1.33×**; 2025 = 14.5 → **2.34×**.
4. Delta grew 15% but margin fell three points, suggesting growth bought with lower prices or a weaker mix. Customers were given longer credit (DSO +15 days) and inventory built up (DIO +17 days), while supplier credit shortened. Trade working capital rose from 7.6 to 12.9, a **5.3m** cash absorption — nearly twice net income — financed by 6.0m of new debt. Leverage rose from 1.3× to 2.3× EBITDA although EBITDA grew only 3%. Profitable growth is consuming cash. Questions: Which customers drive the DSO increase and are any overdue? Is inventory slow-moving or obsolete? Why did suppliers shorten terms (credit concerns)? What price and mix changes caused the margin decline, and is it reversible?
:::

## Set 5 — Business model analysis

:::exercise Set 5 — Business model questions
**Easy**

1. Identify the main revenue model of: (a) a cloud accounting software firm; (b) an online marketplace for spare parts; (c) a building-materials distributor; (d) a commercial bank.

**Intermediate**

2. A subscription costs 50 per month with 80% gross margin; monthly churn is 2.5%; CAC is 600. Compute LTV, LTV/CAC and CAC payback.

**Advanced**

3. A marketplace has GMV of 8m, take rate 10%, payment costs 2.5% of GMV and fixed costs 900,000. (a) Compute contribution and profit. (b) Compute break-even GMV. (c) Management proposes a 12% take rate, expecting GMV to fall 10%. Evaluate.
:::

:::answer Set 5 — Solutions
1. (a) Recurring subscription (SaaS); (b) commission / take rate on transactions, possibly plus listing or advertising fees; (c) trading margin on resale plus logistics services; (d) net interest margin plus fees and commissions.
2. Monthly gross profit = 40. LTV = 40 ÷ 0.025 = **1,600**. LTV/CAC = **2.67×** (below the common 3× rule of thumb). Payback = 600 ÷ 40 = **15 months**.
3. (a) Net revenue 800,000; payment costs 200,000; contribution **600,000**; profit **−300,000**. (b) Break-even GMV = 900,000 ÷ (0.10 − 0.025) = **12.0m**. (c) GMV 7.2m; contribution = 7.2m × 9.5% = 684,000; loss falls to −216,000. Financially better in the short term, but risks: sellers moving to competitors (GMV may fall more than 10%), weaker network effects, and break-even GMV at 12% becomes 9.47m — achievable sooner only if liquidity on the platform holds. Recommend testing in one category first.
:::

## Set 6 — Banking and credit

:::exercise Set 6 — Banking questions
**Easy**

1. Match each need with the most suitable product: (a) seasonal stock build; (b) a new warehouse to be used for fifteen years; (c) occasional short-term overdrawing; (d) a tender requiring a bid bond.

**Intermediate**

2. Cash flow available for debt service is 1.5m; interest is 0.4m and principal 0.8m. Compute DSCR. The covenant minimum is 1.20×: by what percentage can CFADS fall before breach?

**Advanced**

3. A borrower has EBITDA 5m, debt 12m and cash 2m, and requests a further 8m term loan. Bank policy caps net debt/EBITDA at 3.0×. Compute post-deal leverage, the maximum additional debt within policy, and propose a structure.
:::

:::answer Set 6 — Solutions
1. (a) Revolving credit facility or import/working-capital loans; (b) amortising term loan (or lease) matching the asset’s life; (c) overdraft; (d) bank guarantee (bid bond).
2. DSCR = 1.5 ÷ 1.2 = **1.25×**. Breach when CFADS < 1.44m → maximum fall **4%**. Very thin headroom.
3. Post-deal net debt = 12 + 8 − 2 = 18 → **3.6×**, above policy. Maximum net debt = 15 → additional debt **5m** (assuming the cash balance is kept). Proposal: 5m amortising term loan; the remaining 3m from equity or deferred payment; leverage covenant 3.25× stepping down to 2.75×; cash sweep of excess cash flow; security over the financed assets. Alternatively, approve 8m only with a committed equity injection of 3m as a condition precedent.
:::

## Set 7 — Trade finance

:::exercise Set 7 — Trade finance questions
**Easy**

1. Rank from most to least secure for the exporter: open account, confirmed L/C, documentary collection D/A, advance payment, unconfirmed L/C, documentary collection D/P.

**Intermediate**

2. An L/C requires: “500 MT white sugar ICUMSA 45, CIF Jebel Ali”; latest shipment 15 March; documents to be presented within 21 days after shipment and within validity; expiry 10 April; insurance for 110% of invoice value. Documents presented on 12 April: B/L shipped on board 18 March; invoice describing “500 MT sugar ICUMSA 45”; insurance certificate for 100% of invoice value. List the discrepancies.

**Advanced**

3. An exporter receives a 2m USD L/C, 180 days usance, from a bank in a higher-risk country. Options: (a) confirmation at 1.2% p.a. plus discounting at 6.3% p.a. (actual/360); (b) unconfirmed L/C plus credit insurance at 0.8% flat covering 90%, with discounting unavailable. Compute the cost of each and recommend.
:::

:::answer Set 7 — Solutions
1. Advance payment → confirmed L/C → unconfirmed L/C → D/P → D/A → open account.
2. (1) Late shipment: on board 18 March vs latest 15 March. (2) Late presentation: 21 days after 18 March is 8 April; presented 12 April — also after expiry 10 April. (3) Invoice description omits “white”; under UCP 600 art. 18(c) the invoice description must correspond with the credit. (4) Insurance under-insured: 100% vs required 110%. The bank will refuse unless the applicant waives; the beneficiary has lost the bank undertaking for this presentation.
3. (a) Confirmation = 2m × 1.2% × 180/360 = **12,000**; discounting = 2m × 6.3% × 180/360 = **63,000**; the exporter receives cash now with no recourse for issuing-bank or country risk (subject to complying documents). (b) Premium **16,000**; 10% (200,000) retained risk; no early cash, so a 2m receivable sits on the balance sheet for six months (funding cost if borrowed at 6.3% ≈ 63,000 anyway). Recommend (a) where liquidity matters and the country risk is high; (b) is cheaper only if the exporter does not need the cash and accepts claims-process risk.
:::

## Set 8 — Import and export operations

:::exercise Set 8 — Import-export questions
**Easy**

1. Under FOB Shanghai (Incoterms® 2020), who pays ocean freight and where does risk pass? Under DAP Riyadh warehouse?

**Intermediate**

2. FOB value USD 40,000 for 2,000 units; ocean freight 3,200; insurance 150; import duty 5% on CIF; VAT 5% on (CIF + duty), recoverable; port and clearance charges 900; inland transport 400. Compute landed cost per unit.

**Advanced**

3. A 1,000 kg shipment of 4 m³ worth USD 60,000 can go by air at USD 4.50/kg chargeable (volumetric factor 6,000) or by sea LCL at USD 90/m³ plus 300 charges, taking 30 days longer. Cost of capital is 12%. Compare total costs and state when air is justified.
:::

:::answer Set 8 — Solutions
1. FOB: buyer pays ocean freight; risk passes when goods are on board the vessel at Shanghai. DAP Riyadh: seller pays carriage to the named place; risk passes when goods are made available on the arriving vehicle, ready for unloading; buyer clears imports and pays duties.
2. CIF = 43,350; duty = 2,167.50; VAT = 5% × 45,517.50 = 2,275.88 (recoverable, so excluded from cost but needs cash flow). Landed cost = 43,350 + 2,167.50 + 900 + 400 = **46,817.50** → **USD 23.41 per unit**.
3. Air: volumetric weight = 4,000,000 cm³ ÷ 6,000 = 667 kg; chargeable 1,000 kg → **4,500**. Sea: 360 + 300 = 660, plus 30 days’ carrying cost 60,000 × 12% × 30/365 = 592 → **1,252**. Sea is cheaper by 3,248. Air is justified only if the expected lost margin or penalties from the 30-day delay (e.g., stockout, LDs, production stoppage) exceed about **3,250**.
:::

## Set 9 — Contracts

:::exercise Set 9 — Contract questions
**Easy**

1. Name the clause: (a) “Neither party shall be liable for failure to perform caused by events beyond its reasonable control.” (b) “This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of …” (c) “The Seller shall pay 0.5% of the contract price per week of delay.”

**Intermediate**

2. Identify the weaknesses in: “Seller shall deliver the goods promptly. Payment 60 days. Seller’s liability is unlimited.” Rewrite each element as a seller.

**Advanced**

3. A 1.2m contract has delay LDs of 0.5% per week capped at 10%. Compute LDs for 9 weeks and for 25 weeks of delay. The buyer’s actual loss for the 25-week delay is 200,000. Discuss what the buyer can recover.
:::

:::answer Set 9 — Solutions
1. (a) Force majeure; (b) governing law; (c) liquidated damages for delay.
2. “Promptly” is undefined → specify a delivery date or period and the Incoterms® rule with place. “Payment 60 days” lacks a trigger and currency → “USD, 60 days from B/L date, by bank transfer; late interest at x% p.a.”. Unlimited liability → cap at contract value (or a percentage), exclude indirect and consequential loss, with exceptions for fraud and wilful misconduct.
3. 9 weeks: 4.5% × 1.2m = **54,000**. 25 weeks: 12.5% → capped at 10% = **120,000**. Whether the buyer can recover the extra 80,000 depends on the contract wording (whether LDs are the sole and exclusive remedy for delay) and on governing law: some legal systems allow courts to adjust agreed damages, while common-law systems generally enforce genuine pre-estimates or legitimate-interest clauses. The buyer may also have a termination right after the cap is reached. Legal advice under the governing law is required.
:::

## Set 10 — Negotiation

:::exercise Set 10 — Negotiation questions
**Easy**

1. Define BATNA, reservation price and ZOPA. A seller’s minimum price is 92 and the buyer’s maximum is 100: what is the ZOPA?

**Intermediate**

2. A buyer of a 500,000 order demands a 5% discount. The seller’s cost of funds is 10%. Instead of the discount, the seller considers offering payment terms of 30 days instead of 60 and a 2% discount. Compare the seller’s cost of the two options.

**Advanced**

3. Prepare a negotiation plan for renewing a supply contract with a key supplier who announced a 12% price increase. Include objectives, BATNA, issues to trade, concessions plan and opening position.
:::

:::answer Set 10 — Solutions
1. BATNA: best alternative if no agreement is reached. Reservation price: the walk-away point. ZOPA: the overlap of acceptable prices — here **92 to 100**.
2. 5% discount = **25,000**. Alternative: 2% = 10,000, but receiving cash 30 days earlier is worth 500,000 × 10% × 30/365 = 4,110, so the net cost is **≈ 5,890**, about 19,110 less than the 5% discount, if the buyer accepts it.
3. Model plan: **Objectives** — hold the increase to ≤ 5%, secure supply, keep 60-day terms. **Facts** — request the supplier’s cost breakdown; check index movements for its raw materials and energy. **BATNA** — qualified alternative supplier at +7% landed cost with 3-month switching time; can also hold extra stock. **Their BATNA** — loss of 18% of their volume. **Tradeables** — volume commitment, contract length, forecast sharing, payment terms, price-adjustment formula linked to an index, specification simplification. **Concessions plan** — give a 2-year commitment and forecast visibility in exchange for a lower increase; never concede without getting something. **Opening** — accept index-linked adjustment of 3% now with a formula going forward; target 5%; walk away above 7% (BATNA cost).
:::

## Set 11 — Investment

:::exercise Set 11 — Investment questions
**Easy**

1. A share trades at 45 with EPS of 3.00 and a dividend of 1.20. Compute P/E and dividend yield.

**Intermediate**

2. Price a 3-year bond with face value 1,000, an annual coupon of 6% and a yield of 7%.
3. A portfolio is 60% equities expected to return 10% and 40% bonds expected to return 4%. Compute the expected return.

**Advanced**

4. Using question 3, equity volatility is 18%, bond volatility 6% and correlation 0.2. Compute portfolio volatility and comment.
5. A company has EBITDA 20m, net debt 45m and 10m shares. Peers trade at 8× EV/EBITDA. Estimate value per share and its sensitivity to a one-turn change in the multiple.
:::

:::answer Set 11 — Solutions
1. P/E = **15×**; dividend yield = 1.20 ÷ 45 = **2.67%**.
2. 60 ÷ 1.07 + 60 ÷ 1.07² + 1,060 ÷ 1.07³ = 56.07 + 52.41 + 865.28 = **973.76** (below par because yield > coupon).
3. 0.6 × 10% + 0.4 × 4% = **7.6%**.
4. Variance = 0.36 × 0.0324 + 0.16 × 0.0036 + 2 × 0.6 × 0.4 × 0.2 × 0.18 × 0.06 = 0.011664 + 0.000576 + 0.002074 = 0.014314 → σ = **11.96%**, below the weighted average of 13.2% thanks to diversification.
5. EV = 160m; equity = 115m → **11.50 per share**. At 7× → 9.50; at 9× → 13.50: each turn moves the share value by **±2.00 (±17%)**, because leverage magnifies the effect on equity.
:::

## Set 12 — Risk management

:::exercise Set 12 — Risk questions
**Easy**

1. Classify each risk: (a) a customer goes bankrupt; (b) the euro strengthens against the dollar before a euro payment; (c) an employee approves his own expense claim; (d) a port strike delays shipments; (e) a new law bans a product.

**Intermediate**

2. A customer has PD 3%, LGD 60% and exposure 400,000. Compute expected loss. Credit insurance costs 0.5% of exposure and covers 90% of the loss. Is the insurance worth buying?

**Advanced**

3. A US importer owes EUR 1m in three months. The forward rate is 1.0900. Show the USD cost unhedged at spot 1.05 and 1.15, and hedged. Recommend a hedging policy.
:::

:::answer Set 12 — Solutions
1. (a) Credit; (b) FX (market); (c) operational / internal control (segregation of duties); (d) supply chain / operational; (e) regulatory / legal.
2. EL = 0.03 × 0.60 × 400,000 = **7,200**. Insurance premium 2,000; expected recovery 0.9 × 7,200 = 6,480. On expected values the insurance pays (6,480 > 2,000) — and it also protects against the tail (a 240,000 loss) and can support bank financing of the receivable. (Real premiums reflect insurer pricing of PD; if they appear cheap relative to your PD estimate, recheck the estimate.)
3. Unhedged: 1.05 → **1.05m USD**; 1.15 → **1.15m USD**. Hedged: **1.09m USD** in both cases. Policy: hedge committed exposures (e.g., 80–100% of confirmed payables) with forwards; hedge forecast exposures partially (e.g., 50% for 3–6 months), consider options where volume is uncertain; never speculate; review monthly.
:::

## Set 13 — Management decisions

:::exercise Set 13 — Management decision questions
**Easy**

1. Producing 10,000 components in-house costs 18 per unit variable plus 50,000 of fixed costs that would be avoided if production stopped. A supplier offers 21 per unit. Make or buy?

**Intermediate**

2. A factory has spare capacity. A one-off customer offers to buy 2,000 units at 30; variable cost is 26 and normal price is 40. Should the order be accepted? What could change the answer?

**Advanced**

3. Capital budget 1.0m. Projects (indivisible): A cost 600k NPV 180k; B 400k NPV 140k; C 500k NPV 160k; D 300k NPV 75k. Select the best portfolio and explain why ranking by profitability index alone fails here.
:::

:::answer Set 13 — Solutions
1. In-house relevant cost = 180,000 + 50,000 = 230,000 (23 per unit) vs buy 210,000 → **buy**, saving 20,000 — provided the fixed costs are truly avoidable and quality/supply risks are acceptable.
2. Contribution = (30 − 26) × 2,000 = **+8,000** → accept on financial grounds. The answer changes if regular customers learn of the price and demand the same, if capacity is not really spare, or if the order displaces higher-margin work.
3. Feasible combinations: A+B (1.0m) NPV **320k**; B+C (0.9m) 300k; C+D (0.8m) 235k; A+D (0.9m) 255k; B+D (0.7m) 215k. **Choose A+B.** PI ranking (B 0.35, C 0.32, A 0.30, D 0.25) would pick B then C, using 0.9m and earning 300k; with indivisible projects, unused budget makes greedy PI ranking suboptimal, so compare combinations.
:::

## Set 15 — Meeting simulations

:::exercise Set 15 — Meeting simulation tasks
**Easy**

1. Draft a 45-minute agenda for a quarterly review meeting with a key supplier.

**Intermediate**

2. Script the opening and the request in a meeting with your bank relationship manager to increase your working-capital facility from 2m to 3m.

**Advanced**

3. You present a recommendation to the board to open a distribution subsidiary in a new country. Prepare the structure of a ten-minute presentation and model answers to three difficult questions.
:::

:::answer Set 15 — Solutions
1. Agenda (45 min): (1) Welcome and objectives — 5; (2) performance review: OTIF, quality, invoice accuracy — 10; (3) open issues and corrective actions — 10; (4) forecast for next two quarters and capacity — 10; (5) price and cost-index review — 5; (6) actions, owners and dates — 5.
2. See the dialogue below.
3. Structure: (1) recommendation in one sentence and the decision requested; (2) the opportunity — market size, customers, why now; (3) the plan — entity, team, phasing; (4) financials — investment, break-even, NPV/IRR, funding; (5) risks and mitigations; (6) alternatives considered (distributor, agent, JV) and why rejected; (7) decision and next steps. Model answers to difficult questions are in the second dialogue below.
:::

:::dialog Model dialogue — Facility increase meeting
**Treasurer:** Thank you for meeting us. Our purpose today is to discuss increasing our working-capital line from 2 million to 3 million ahead of the new distribution contract.
**Relationship manager:** Happy to discuss. What is driving the need?
**Treasurer:** The new contract adds about 9 million of annual revenue on 60-day terms. Our cash-flow forecast shows a peak additional requirement of 0.9 million in the fourth quarter; we have prepared the 13-week forecast and the contract summary.
**Relationship manager:** How does this affect your covenants?
**Treasurer:** Net leverage rises from 1.6 to 1.9 times at the peak, against a 3.0 covenant, and falls back below 1.7 by year-end as receivables convert.
**Relationship manager:** Would you consider assigning the receivables from the new customer?
**Treasurer:** Yes, we are open to that if it improves the pricing. Could you indicate the timeline and information you will need for credit approval?
:::

:::dialog Model dialogue — Board questions
**Board member:** What happens if sales are 30% below plan?
**Presenter:** Break-even moves from year 2 to year 3, and the cumulative cash need rises from 1.8 million to 2.4 million, still within the approved funding envelope. We have defined a stop-loss review at month 18 if revenue is below 60% of plan.
**Board member:** Why not continue with the distributor?
**Presenter:** The distributor covers only one region and earns a 25% margin we could capture; our analysis shows the subsidiary creates more value from year 3 and gives us control of key accounts. We keep the distributor in the north during the transition.
**Board member:** What is the biggest risk you are worried about?
**Presenter:** Hiring a capable country manager. We have two candidates in the final stage, and the launch is conditional on that appointment.
:::
