# Banking Terminology

:::obj In this chapter
Domain 5: the words used by relationship managers, credit officers, trade-finance specialists and treasury teams. Part VI explains the products and processes; Part VII the trade-finance instruments.

## Lending and credit terms

:::term Credit facility | التسهيلات الائتمانية | 
def: An arrangement under which a bank makes credit available to a borrower up to an agreed limit, on agreed terms — e.g., overdraft, revolving credit, term loan, trade-finance lines, guarantee lines.
ar: ترتيب يتيح فيه البنك للمقترض ائتماناً حتى حدّ متفق عليه وبشروط محددة، مثل الحساب الجاري المدين والقرض الدوّار والقرض لأجل وخطوط تمويل التجارة وخطوط خطابات الضمان.
why: Companies negotiate facilities in advance so funding is available when needed; banks approve them after credit analysis.
use: “Funded” facilities involve lending cash (loans, overdrafts); “unfunded” or “non-cash” facilities involve contingent commitments (L/Cs, guarantees).
ex: A bank grants a $5m package: $2m overdraft, $2m L/C line, $1m guarantee line.
say: “Our facilities come up for annual review in March — we should ask for an increase in the L/C line.”
rel: Credit limit; facility letter; funded/unfunded; drawdown; utilisation.
vs: Facility (the available limit) vs utilisation (the amount actually used).
:::

:::term Overdraft | الحساب الجاري المدين / السحب على المكشوف | OD
def: A facility allowing a current account to go into a negative balance up to an agreed limit; interest is charged on the overdrawn amount, and it is usually repayable on demand.
ar: تسهيل يسمح بأن يصبح رصيد الحساب الجاري سالباً حتى حد متفق عليه، وتُحتسب الفائدة على المبلغ المسحوب فقط، ويكون عادةً قابلاً للاستدعاء عند الطلب.
why: Flexible day-to-day working-capital cover; but “on demand” means the bank can cancel it.
use: Used for timing gaps in payments and collections.
ex: Limit $1m; average utilisation $400k at 9% → interest about $36k a year.
say: “We’re running close to the overdraft limit at month-end because of salary payments.”
rel: Revolving credit facility; working capital; on demand; utilisation.
vs: Overdraft (on-demand, usually annual) vs committed RCF (bank must lend during the commitment period if conditions are met).
:::

:::term Revolving credit facility | التسهيل الائتماني المتجدد (الدوّار) | RCF
def: A committed facility under which a borrower can draw, repay and redraw loans up to a limit during an availability period (often 1–5 years), paying interest on drawn amounts and a commitment fee on undrawn amounts.
ar: تسهيل ملزم للبنك يسمح للمقترض بالسحب والسداد وإعادة السحب حتى حد معيّن خلال فترة الإتاحة (عادة من سنة إلى خمس سنوات)، ويدفع فائدة على المسحوب وعمولة التزام على غير المسحوب.
why: Gives certainty of liquidity; standard for mid-size and large corporates.
use: Drawn in “loans” of 1, 3 or 6 months; often used as a liquidity backstop.
ex: $50m 3-year RCF: margin 1.75% over SOFR on drawn; commitment fee 35% of margin on undrawn.
say: “We keep the RCF largely undrawn as liquidity headroom.”
rel: Commitment fee; utilisation fee; margin; availability period; covenants.
vs: RCF (revolving) vs term loan (once repaid, cannot be redrawn).
:::

:::term Term loan | القرض لأجل | 
def: A loan of a fixed amount for a fixed period, repaid by instalments (amortising) or at maturity (bullet), typically used to finance capex, acquisitions or long-term needs.
ar: قرض بمبلغ محدد ولمدة محددة، يُسدَّد على أقساط أو دفعة واحدة عند الاستحقاق، ويُستخدم عادة لتمويل النفقات الرأسمالية أو الاستحواذات أو الاحتياجات طويلة الأجل.
why: Matches long-term assets with long-term funding.
use: Terms include tenor, grace period, repayment profile, pricing, security and covenants.
ex: $10m, 5 years, 1-year grace, then 16 equal quarterly instalments.
say: “We’d like a 7-year tenor with a 30% balloon to match the asset life.”
rel: Tenor; amortisation; bullet; balloon; grace period; covenant.
vs: Term loan (capex/long-term) vs working-capital facilities (short-term, revolving).
:::

:::term Tenor | مدة التسهيل / أجل القرض | 
def: The length of time until a loan, bill, letter of credit or other credit instrument matures.
ar: المدة المتبقية حتى استحقاق القرض أو الكمبيالة أو الاعتماد المستندي أو أي أداة ائتمانية.
why: Longer tenor means more risk for the bank (and usually higher pricing) and lower annual repayments for the borrower.
use: Bankers say “5-year tenor”; in trade finance “90-day usance L/C”.
ex: An import loan with 120-day tenor matches the time to sell and collect.
say: “The bank is comfortable with the amount but wants to shorten the tenor to three years.”
rel: Maturity; usance; average life; duration.
vs: Tenor (length) vs maturity date (end date).
:::

:::term Collateral | الضمان العيني / الرهن | 
def: Assets pledged by a borrower to secure an obligation, which the lender can enforce against if the borrower defaults: property mortgages, pledges of shares, cash deposits, assignments of receivables, charges over inventory or equipment.
ar: أصول يرهنها المقترض لضمان التزامه، ويحق للمقرض التنفيذ عليها في حال التعثر، مثل رهن العقارات أو الأسهم أو الودائع النقدية أو التنازل عن الذمم المدينة أو رهن المخزون والمعدات.
why: Reduces the lender’s loss given default and may lower pricing; but enforcement depends on local law and the asset’s liquidity.
use: Lenders apply haircuts: a property worth $10m may support a loan of only $6m (60% loan-to-value).
ex: Cash collateral covering 100% of a guarantee issued for a client with weak credit.
say: “The facility is secured by a first-ranking mortgage over the warehouse and an assignment of insurance.”
rel: Security; mortgage; pledge; LTV; haircut; guarantee.
vs: Collateral is an asset; a guarantee is a promise by a third party to pay. Both are “credit support”.
:::

:::term Bank guarantee | خطاب الضمان | BG / LG
def: An irrevocable undertaking by a bank to pay a beneficiary up to a stated amount if the applicant fails to perform an obligation, typically payable on first written demand (demand guarantee, often subject to URDG 758).
ar: تعهد غير قابل للإلغاء من البنك بدفع مبلغ محدد للمستفيد إذا أخلّ العميل (طالب الضمان) بالتزامه، ويُدفع عادةً عند أول طلب كتابي، وكثيراً ما يخضع للقواعد الموحدة للضمانات عند الطلب (URDG 758).
why: Gives the beneficiary security without cash deposits; essential in tenders, construction and supply contracts.
use: Types: bid bond, performance bond, advance payment guarantee, retention guarantee, payment guarantee, customs guarantee.
ex: A contractor gives a 10% performance guarantee on a $20m contract: $2m.
say: “The client requires a 5% bid bond valid for 120 days.”
rel: Standby L/C; URDG 758; counter-guarantee; performance bond.
vs: A demand guarantee is independent of the underlying contract (bank pays on compliant demand); a surety/accessory guarantee depends on proving the default.
:::

:::term Covenant | التعهّد | 
def: A promise in a loan agreement by the borrower to do (positive covenant), not to do (negative covenant), or to maintain financial ratios (financial covenant). Breach can be an event of default.
ar: التزام في اتفاقية القرض يتعهد فيه المقترض بالقيام بأمر ما (تعهد إيجابي) أو الامتناع عنه (تعهد سلبي) أو المحافظة على نسب مالية معينة (تعهد مالي). مخالفته قد تُعدّ حالة تعثر.
why: Covenants are the bank’s early-warning and control system; breaches give lenders leverage to renegotiate.
use: Common financial covenants: net debt/EBITDA ≤ 3.0×; interest cover ≥ 4.0×; DSCR ≥ 1.25×; minimum tangible net worth.
ex: Q3 net debt/EBITDA = 3.3× vs covenant 3.0× → breach; borrower seeks a waiver.
say: “We have limited headroom on the leverage covenant at year-end; let’s talk to the bank before the test date.”
rel: Negative pledge; event of default; waiver; headroom; compliance certificate.
vs: Covenant (borrower’s promise) vs condition precedent (must be satisfied before drawdown).
:::

:::term Reference rate and margin | السعر المرجعي والهامش | 
def: Floating-rate loans are priced as a reference (benchmark) rate plus a credit margin. Major references include SOFR (USD), €STR and EURIBOR (EUR), SONIA (GBP), and local interbank rates such as EIBOR or SAIBOR.
ar: تُسعَّر القروض ذات الفائدة المتغيرة على أساس سعر مرجعي مضافاً إليه هامش ائتماني. من الأسعار المرجعية: SOFR للدولار، وEURIBOR و€STR لليورو، وSONIA للجنيه الإسترليني، وأسعار محلية مثل EIBOR وSAIBOR.
why: The reference rate reflects market funding cost; the margin reflects the borrower’s credit risk. LIBOR was phased out (USD LIBOR panels ceased in June 2023); verify current benchmarks for any contract.
use: “3M SOFR + 225 bps”; interest periods of 1, 3 or 6 months.
ex: 3M SOFR 4.30% + 2.25% = 6.55% all-in.
say: “The margin ratchets down 25 bps if leverage falls below 2.0×.”
rel: Basis point; margin ratchet; floor; fixed vs floating; interest rate swap.
vs: Margin (bank’s credit spread) vs all-in rate (reference + margin) vs fees.
:::

:::term Letter of credit | الاعتماد المستندي | L/C, LC
def: A bank’s undertaking, on behalf of the buyer (applicant), to pay the seller (beneficiary) a stated sum against presentation of complying documents within a set time, usually subject to UCP 600.
ar: تعهد من البنك، بناءً على طلب المشتري، بأن يدفع للبائع مبلغاً محدداً مقابل تقديم مستندات مطابقة لشروط الاعتماد خلال مدة محددة، ويخضع عادةً للأصول والأعراف الموحدة للاعتمادات المستندية (UCP 600).
why: Replaces the buyer’s credit risk with a bank’s, and gives the buyer assurance that payment is made only against shipping documents.
use: Sight or usance (deferred); confirmed or unconfirmed; transferable; back-to-back. Banks deal in documents, not goods.
ex: An L/C for $480,000 requires a clean on-board bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list and certificate of origin.
say: “We’ll ship only against a confirmed irrevocable L/C from a first-class bank.”
rel: UCP 600; confirmation; discrepancy; advising bank; SBLC.
vs: Commercial L/C (payment mechanism, expected to be drawn) vs standby L/C (guarantee-like, drawn only on default).
:::

:::term Correspondent bank | البنك المراسل | 
def: A bank that provides services (payments, account holding, trade finance) on behalf of another bank, usually in another country or currency.
ar: بنك يقدّم خدمات لبنك آخر، كتنفيذ المدفوعات والاحتفاظ بالحسابات وتمويل التجارة، وغالباً في بلد آخر أو بعملة أخرى.
why: International payments in USD normally settle through US correspondent accounts; loss of correspondent relationships (“de-risking”) can cut a country off from dollar payments.
use: Nostro account (“our account with you”) and vostro account (“your account with us”).
ex: A Lebanese bank pays USD to a Chinese supplier via its correspondent in New York.
say: “The payment is delayed at the correspondent bank for compliance screening.”
rel: Nostro/vostro; SWIFT; intermediary bank; de-risking; AML.
vs: Correspondent bank (service provider to banks) vs branch (part of the same bank).
:::

:::term Know your customer | اعرف عميلك | KYC
def: Procedures by which financial institutions verify the identity of clients, understand ownership and control, the nature of the business and the expected activity, and assess money-laundering and sanctions risk.
ar: إجراءات تتحقق بها المؤسسات المالية من هوية العملاء وهيكل الملكية والسيطرة وطبيعة النشاط والتعاملات المتوقعة، وتقيّم مخاطر غسل الأموال والعقوبات.
why: Regulatory requirement under AML laws based on FATF Recommendations; poor KYC exposes banks to large fines and loss of correspondent relationships.
use: Onboarding, periodic reviews and event-driven reviews; enhanced due diligence (EDD) for high-risk clients and PEPs.
ex: Account opening needs CR, articles, UBO declaration, IDs of signatories, source of funds, expected transaction profile.
say: “Onboarding will take two weeks because the ownership structure has three layers of holding companies.”
rel: CDD; EDD; UBO; PEP; AML; sanctions screening.
vs: KYC (know the client) vs KYT (monitor transactions) vs KYS/KYB (know suppliers/businesses).
:::

:::term Syndicated loan | القرض المشترك (المجمّع) | 
def: A loan provided by a group (syndicate) of lenders under one agreement, arranged by one or more lead arrangers and administered by an agent bank.
ar: قرض يقدّمه تجمّع من المقرضين بموجب اتفاقية واحدة، ويرتّبه بنك أو أكثر بصفة مرتّب رئيسي، ويديره بنك وكيل.
why: Allows large loans beyond one bank’s appetite and spreads risk.
use: Roles: mandated lead arranger (MLA), bookrunner, agent, security agent, participants. Fees: arrangement, participation, agency.
ex: $600m 5-year loan for a petrochemical company from 12 banks.
say: “We’ve mandated two banks to arrange a $300m syndicated facility; general syndication launches next week.”
rel: Club deal; agent bank; arrangement fee; LMA documentation.
vs: Syndicated loan vs club deal (small group of relationship banks, often without wide syndication) vs bilateral loan (one bank).
:::

:::term Non-performing loan | القرض غير العامل (المتعثر) | NPL
def: A loan on which the borrower is in default or unlikely to pay, commonly defined as 90 or more days past due or classified as credit-impaired (Stage 3 under IFRS 9).
ar: قرض تعثّر فيه المقترض أو يُرجّح عدم قدرته على السداد، ويُعرَّف عادةً بالتأخر 90 يوماً أو أكثر أو بتصنيفه منخفض القيمة الائتمانية (المرحلة الثالثة وفق IFRS 9).
why: NPLs reduce bank income, require provisions and consume capital; the NPL ratio is a key bank-health indicator.
use: Banks restructure, enforce collateral or sell NPL portfolios.
ex: Loans $10bn; NPLs $450m → NPL ratio 4.5%; provisions $360m → coverage 80%.
say: “The NPL ratio rose to 4.5%, mainly from the construction book.”
rel: Default; Stage 1/2/3; provision coverage; restructuring; write-off.
vs: NPL (bad loan) vs watch-list loan (early warning, still performing).
:::

:::exercise Practice: Chapter 5
1. A borrower has a $10m RCF, drawn $4m. Margin 2.0% over 3M SOFR (4.3%), commitment fee 35% of margin on undrawn. What is the annual cost?
2. Explain to a non-banker the difference between collateral and a guarantee.
3. A client wants a 10-year term loan to finance inventory. What is wrong with this request?
4. A covenant requires interest cover ≥ 4.0×. EBIT is $6m, interest $1.6m. Is the covenant met? How much can EBIT fall before a breach?
:::

:::answer Answers: Chapter 5
1. Drawn interest: 4m × 6.3% = $252,000. Commitment fee: 6m × 0.70% = $42,000. Total ≈ **$294,000** (plus any arrangement fee amortisation).
2. Collateral is an asset the lender can take and sell (property, cash, shares). A guarantee is a promise by another person or bank to pay if the borrower does not. Collateral’s value depends on the asset; a guarantee’s value depends on the guarantor’s creditworthiness.
3. Mismatch: inventory turns over within months; it should be financed with short-term working-capital lines that are repaid as goods are sold. Long-term debt for short-term assets wastes interest and hides poor inventory management.
4. 6.0 ÷ 1.6 = 3.75× → **breach** (below 4.0×). It is already in breach; EBIT must rise to at least $6.4m to comply.
:::
