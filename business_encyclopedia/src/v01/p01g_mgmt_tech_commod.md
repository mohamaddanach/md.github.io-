## Management and leadership terms

:::term Key performance indicator | مؤشر الأداء الرئيسي | KPI
def: A quantifiable measure used to evaluate progress toward an important objective.
ar: مقياس كمي يُستخدم لتقييم مدى التقدم نحو هدف مهم.
why: What gets measured gets managed — and what is measured badly gets managed badly.
use: Good KPIs are few, owned, comparable over time, and linked to decisions: DSO, on-time delivery, gross margin, NPS.
ex: Logistics KPI: on-time-in-full (OTIF) 96% vs target 98%.
say: “Let’s cut the dashboard to the eight KPIs the board actually uses.”
rel: OKR; metric; target; balanced scorecard; dashboard.
vs: KPI (ongoing health measure) vs OKR key result (time-bound measure of an ambitious objective).
:::

:::term Objectives and key results | الأهداف والنتائج الرئيسية | OKR
def: A goal-setting framework pairing a qualitative objective with 2–5 measurable key results, usually set quarterly.
ar: إطار لتحديد الأهداف يربط هدفاً نوعياً بنتيجتين إلى خمس نتائج قابلة للقياس، ويُحدَّد عادةً كل ربع سنة.
why: Aligns teams on priorities and makes ambition measurable.
use: Common in technology companies; often not tied directly to compensation.
ex: Objective: “Become the preferred supplier for hotel groups.” KR1: sign 3 framework agreements; KR2: OTIF ≥ 98%; KR3: NPS ≥ 50.
say: “This initiative doesn’t support any of our Q3 OKRs — let’s deprioritise it.”
rel: KPI; strategy; alignment; performance management.
vs: OKRs (change goals) vs KPIs (run-the-business metrics).
:::

:::term Delegation of authority | تفويض الصلاحيات | DoA
def: The formal framework specifying which roles may approve which decisions and commitments, up to which limits.
ar: الإطار الرسمي الذي يحدد الأدوار المخوّلة باعتماد القرارات والالتزامات المختلفة والحدود المالية لكل منها.
why: Prevents unauthorised commitments; speeds routine decisions; auditors test compliance with it.
use: E.g., purchase orders up to $50k by department head; up to $500k by CFO; above by CEO/board.
ex: A sales manager offers a 15% discount, but the DoA allows only 8% without commercial director approval.
say: “This exceeds my authority under the DoA — I’ll need the CFO’s sign-off.”
rel: Approval matrix; internal control; authorised signatory; governance.
vs: Internal DoA vs external authority to bind the company (powers of attorney, board resolutions).
:::

:::term Stakeholder management | إدارة أصحاب المصلحة | 
def: Identifying stakeholders, understanding their interests and influence, and planning engagement to secure support and manage resistance.
ar: تحديد أصحاب المصلحة وفهم اهتماماتهم ومدى تأثيرهم، والتخطيط للتواصل معهم لكسب دعمهم والتعامل مع مقاومتهم.
why: Many good proposals fail politically rather than technically.
use: Power-interest grid: manage closely (high power, high interest), keep satisfied, keep informed, monitor.
ex: Before proposing a new ERP, the project lead meets finance, operations and IT heads individually.
say: “We need to socialise the proposal with the regional heads before the ExCo.”
rel: Change management; sponsorship; RACI; communication plan.
vs: Stakeholder management (relationships) vs project management (tasks, time, budget).
:::

:::term RACI | مصفوفة المسؤوليات | RACI
def: A matrix assigning, for each task or decision, who is Responsible (does it), Accountable (owns the outcome — one person), Consulted and Informed.
ar: مصفوفة تحدد لكل مهمة أو قرار: المنفّذ (Responsible)، والمساءَل عن النتيجة (Accountable — شخص واحد)، ومن يُستشار (Consulted)، ومن يُبلَّغ (Informed).
why: Clarifies ownership and prevents “everyone thought someone else was doing it”.
use: Projects, process design, cross-departmental activities.
ex: Customer credit limit increase: R = credit analyst; A = credit manager; C = sales; I = finance director.
say: “Who is accountable for supplier onboarding? Let’s put it in a RACI.”
rel: Roles and responsibilities; governance; process map.
vs: Responsible (does the work) vs Accountable (answerable for it).
:::

## Corporate strategy terms

:::term Competitive advantage | الميزة التنافسية | 
def: An attribute that allows a company to earn higher returns than competitors over time — typically through lower cost, differentiation, or focus on a niche.
ar: خاصية تمكّن الشركة من تحقيق عوائد أعلى من منافسيها على المدى الطويل، عادةً عبر التكلفة الأقل أو التمايز أو التركيز على شريحة محددة.
why: Without it, competition drives returns down to the cost of capital.
use: Strategy discussions test whether an advantage is real, durable and defensible.
ex: A distributor with exclusive rights and the region’s only certified service team.
say: “Price isn’t our advantage — speed of delivery and technical support are.”
rel: Moat; differentiation; cost leadership; Porter’s generic strategies; value proposition.
vs: Competitive advantage (relative superiority) vs core competence (internal capability that may create it).
:::

:::term Economic moat | الخندق الاقتصادي | 
def: A durable structural advantage that protects a company’s profits from competitors: network effects, switching costs, intangible assets (brands, patents, licences), cost advantages, efficient scale.
ar: ميزة هيكلية دائمة تحمي أرباح الشركة من المنافسين، مثل تأثيرات الشبكة وتكاليف التحول والأصول غير الملموسة كالعلامات وبراءات الاختراع والتراخيص، والمزايا في التكلفة.
why: Investors pay higher multiples for businesses with wide moats.
use: Term popularised by investors to evaluate durability of returns.
ex: A payment network where more merchants attract more cardholders and vice versa.
say: “The software has high switching costs — that’s the moat, not the features.”
rel: Competitive advantage; network effects; switching costs; barriers to entry.
vs: A temporary lead (first-mover) is not a moat unless it creates structural advantages.
:::

:::term SWOT analysis | تحليل نقاط القوة والضعف والفرص والتهديدات | SWOT
def: A framework listing internal Strengths and Weaknesses and external Opportunities and Threats.
ar: إطار يحدد نقاط القوة والضعف الداخلية، والفرص والتهديدات الخارجية.
why: Simple structuring tool for strategy discussions; weak if not followed by choices and actions.
use: Combine into strategies: use strengths to seize opportunities (SO), fix weaknesses that block opportunities (WO), etc.
ex: Strength: exclusive brand rights; Weakness: single warehouse; Opportunity: new tourism projects; Threat: low-cost online competitors.
say: “The SWOT is fine, but what decisions follow from it?”
rel: PESTLE; Five Forces; TOWS matrix; strategic planning.
vs: Strengths/weaknesses are internal; opportunities/threats external — mixing them is the most common error.
:::

:::term Porter’s Five Forces | قوى بورتر الخمس | 
def: A framework for analysing industry attractiveness through five forces: rivalry among competitors, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, bargaining power of buyers, bargaining power of suppliers.
ar: إطار لتحليل جاذبية الصناعة من خلال خمس قوى: حدة المنافسة، وتهديد الداخلين الجدد، وتهديد البدائل، والقوة التفاوضية للمشترين، والقوة التفاوضية للموردين.
why: Explains why some industries are structurally more profitable than others.
use: Market-entry decisions, M&A screening, investment theses.
ex: Commodity trading: powerful producers and buyers, thin margins, high rivalry.
say: “Buyer power is the issue — three supermarket chains take 70% of volume.”
rel: Industry structure; competitive advantage; barriers to entry; SWOT.
vs: Five Forces analyses the industry; SWOT analyses the company within it.
:::

:::term PESTLE analysis | تحليل العوامل الخارجية PESTLE | PESTLE
def: A scan of external macro-environment factors: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental.
ar: مسح لعوامل البيئة الخارجية الكلية: السياسية والاقتصادية والاجتماعية والتكنولوجية والقانونية والبيئية.
why: Identifies external drivers and risks before market entry or major investment.
use: Feeds into scenario planning and risk registers.
ex: Entering a new country: political stability, inflation, demographics, digital adoption, labour law, climate regulation.
say: “The PESTLE flags currency convertibility as the main economic risk.”
rel: SWOT; country risk; scenario planning.
vs: PESTLE (macro environment) vs Five Forces (industry environment).
:::

## Entrepreneurship and startup terms

:::term Minimum viable product | الحد الأدنى من المنتج القابل للتطبيق | MVP
def: The simplest version of a product that allows a team to test a key hypothesis with real users and learn with the least effort.
ar: أبسط نسخة من المنتج تتيح للفريق اختبار فرضية أساسية مع مستخدمين حقيقيين والتعلم بأقل جهد ممكن.
why: Avoids spending years building something nobody wants.
use: MVPs can be landing pages, manual services (“concierge MVP”) or prototypes.
ex: Before building a freight-booking app, a founder books shipments manually via WhatsApp for 20 customers.
say: “Let’s ship an MVP to five pilot customers before we hire more engineers.”
rel: Product-market fit; lean startup; prototype; validation.
vs: MVP (learning tool) vs first commercial release (polished product).
:::

:::term Product-market fit | ملاءمة المنتج للسوق | PMF
def: The state in which a product satisfies a strong market demand, shown by retention, organic growth, willingness to pay and customer pull.
ar: الحالة التي يلبّي فيها المنتج طلباً قوياً في السوق، ويظهر ذلك في احتفاظ العملاء والنمو العضوي والاستعداد للدفع وطلب العملاء المتزايد.
why: Scaling before PMF burns cash on customers who will churn.
use: Indicators: flat retention cohorts, low CAC through referrals, sales cycle shortening.
ex: 40% of monthly users say they would be “very disappointed” without the product (a commonly cited survey heuristic).
say: “We have PMF in the SME segment, not yet in enterprise.”
rel: MVP; retention; traction; scaling.
vs: Traction (some growth) vs PMF (sustainable demand that pulls the product).
:::

:::term Burn rate and runway | معدل الحرق والمدة المتبقية | 
def: Burn rate is the net cash a company spends per month; runway is how many months its cash will last at that rate.
ar: معدل الحرق هو صافي النقد الذي تنفقه الشركة شهرياً، والمدة المتبقية (Runway) هي عدد الأشهر التي يكفيها النقد المتاح بهذا المعدل.
why: Startups die when runway ends before the next funding round or profitability.
use: Runway = cash ÷ net monthly burn. Founders aim to raise with 12–18 months of runway left.
ex: Cash $1.8m; net burn $150k/month → runway 12 months.
say: “With 9 months of runway we need to start the Series A process now.”
rel: Cash flow; fundraising; break-even; default alive.
vs: Gross burn (all spending) vs net burn (spending minus revenue).
:::

:::term Term sheet | ورقة الشروط | 
def: A document summarising the key terms of a proposed investment or financing (valuation, amount, instrument, rights, conditions), mostly non-binding except for clauses like confidentiality and exclusivity.
ar: وثيقة تلخّص الشروط الرئيسية للاستثمار أو التمويل المقترح (التقييم والمبلغ ونوع الأداة والحقوق والشروط)، وتكون غالباً غير ملزمة باستثناء بنود كالسرية والحصرية.
why: Sets the framework for long-form documents; terms beyond valuation (liquidation preference, control rights) can matter more than price.
use: VC term sheets; bank loan term sheets.
ex: $3m Series A at $12m pre-money, 1× non-participating liquidation preference, one board seat.
say: “The valuation is fine, but the participating preference in the term sheet is off-market.”
rel: Valuation; liquidation preference; anti-dilution; SHA; LOI.
vs: Term sheet (summary) vs definitive agreements (binding contracts).
:::

:::term Pre-money and post-money valuation | التقييم قبل الاستثمار وبعده | 
def: Pre-money valuation is the company’s value before new investment; post-money = pre-money + new investment. The investor’s ownership = investment ÷ post-money.
ar: التقييم قبل الاستثمار هو قيمة الشركة قبل ضخ الأموال الجديدة، والتقييم بعد الاستثمار = التقييم قبله + مبلغ الاستثمار. نسبة ملكية المستثمر = مبلغ الاستثمار ÷ التقييم بعد الاستثمار.
why: Determines dilution of founders.
use: Option pools are often required to be included in pre-money, increasing founder dilution.
ex: $2m invested at $8m pre → post $10m → investor 20%.
say: “Is the 10% option pool in the pre-money or the post?”
rel: Dilution; cap table; option pool; valuation.
vs: Pre-money vs post-money — confusing them changes ownership by several percentage points.
:::

## Technology and digital business terms

:::term Enterprise resource planning | تخطيط موارد المؤسسة | ERP
def: An integrated software system that manages core business processes — finance, procurement, inventory, sales, manufacturing, HR — on a shared database.
ar: نظام برمجي متكامل يدير العمليات الأساسية للمنشأة كالمالية والمشتريات والمخزون والمبيعات والتصنيع والموارد البشرية على قاعدة بيانات مشتركة.
why: The ERP is the operational backbone and the source of financial reporting; implementations are major, risky projects.
use: Examples of vendors include SAP, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics, Odoo. Key concepts: modules, master data, workflows, customisation vs configuration.
ex: A sales order in the ERP reserves stock, triggers picking, creates an invoice and posts to the general ledger automatically.
say: “The ERP go-live slipped three months because of data-migration issues.”
rel: CRM; WMS; master data; integration; cloud/SaaS.
vs: ERP (internal operations) vs CRM (customer-facing activity) — often integrated.
:::

:::term Application programming interface | واجهة برمجة التطبيقات | API
def: A defined interface allowing software systems to request data or services from each other.
ar: واجهة محددة تتيح للأنظمة البرمجية طلب البيانات أو الخدمات من بعضها البعض.
why: APIs connect banks, ERPs, marketplaces, carriers and payment providers, enabling automation and new business models (open banking, embedded finance).
use: REST/JSON APIs; authentication via keys/OAuth; rate limits; SLAs.
ex: An importer’s ERP pulls daily FX rates and pushes payment instructions to the bank via API.
say: “Does the carrier offer a tracking API, or do we need EDI?”
rel: Integration; EDI; webhook; open banking; middleware.
vs: API (real-time, flexible) vs EDI (older standard for structured business documents, still common in retail and logistics).
:::

:::term Software as a service | البرمجيات كخدمة | SaaS
def: Software delivered over the internet on a subscription basis and hosted by the provider.
ar: برمجيات تُقدَّم عبر الإنترنت باشتراك دوري ويستضيفها المزوّد.
why: Shifts IT spending from capex to opex; creates recurring revenue businesses valued on ARR.
use: Metrics: ARR, MRR, churn, NRR, CAC payback, gross margin.
ex: A CRM at $60 per user per month for 200 users = $144k ARR.
say: “The SaaS contract auto-renews with a 7% uplift — we should negotiate a cap.”
rel: Cloud; subscription; ARR; IaaS/PaaS.
vs: SaaS (subscription, hosted) vs perpetual licence (one-off fee plus annual maintenance, often on-premise).
:::

:::term Cybersecurity | الأمن السيبراني | 
def: Protection of systems, networks and data from attack, damage or unauthorised access.
ar: حماية الأنظمة والشبكات والبيانات من الهجمات والأضرار والوصول غير المصرح به.
why: Ransomware and business email compromise cause direct losses, downtime and regulatory penalties.
use: Controls: MFA, patching, backups, least privilege, security awareness, incident response; frameworks such as ISO/IEC 27001 and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework.
ex: Business email compromise: a fake supplier email changes bank details — prevented by call-back verification.
say: “Payments over $10k require call-back verification to a known number — that’s a cyber control, not just finance.”
rel: Operational risk; data protection; IT governance; business continuity.
vs: Cybersecurity (protecting systems) vs data privacy (lawful handling of personal data).
:::

:::term Machine learning | تعلّم الآلة | ML
def: Methods that let computers learn patterns from data to make predictions or decisions without being explicitly programmed with rules.
ar: أساليب تمكّن الحواسيب من تعلّم الأنماط من البيانات للتنبؤ أو اتخاذ القرار دون برمجتها بقواعد صريحة.
why: Used in credit scoring, fraud detection, demand forecasting, pricing and document processing.
use: Business value depends on data quality, integration into decisions, monitoring and governance (bias, explainability).
ex: A gradient-boosting model predicts which invoices will be paid late, prioritising collection calls.
say: “The model’s accuracy is fine, but the credit team needs explainable reasons for declines.”
rel: AI; predictive analytics; model risk; data governance.
vs: ML (learns from data) vs rule-based automation (explicit if-then rules).
:::

:::term Fintech | التقنية المالية | 
def: Technology-enabled financial services and the companies that provide them: digital payments, lending platforms, neobanks, wealth tech, insurtech, regtech.
ar: الخدمات المالية المعتمدة على التكنولوجيا والشركات التي تقدّمها، مثل المدفوعات الرقمية ومنصات الإقراض والبنوك الرقمية والتأمين الرقمي وتقنيات الامتثال.
why: Changes distribution, cost and risk in finance; often operates under specific licences or partnerships with banks.
use: Business models: transaction fees, interest spread, subscription, interchange share.
ex: A BNPL provider earns merchant fees of 3–6% per transaction (illustrative range; varies).
say: “Are they licensed, or operating under a bank partner’s licence?”
rel: Payments; open banking; embedded finance; regulatory sandbox.
vs: Fintech (technology firms in finance) vs banks’ digital channels.
:::

## Business intelligence and analytics terms

:::term Business intelligence | ذكاء الأعمال | BI
def: Technologies and practices for collecting, integrating, analysing and presenting business data to support decisions — dashboards, reports, ad hoc analysis.
ar: التقنيات والممارسات الخاصة بجمع بيانات الأعمال ودمجها وتحليلها وعرضها لدعم اتخاذ القرار، مثل لوحات المعلومات والتقارير والتحليلات الفورية.
why: Converts transactional data into management information; a “single source of truth” prevents conflicting numbers.
use: Tools such as Power BI, Tableau, Looker; semantic layers define metrics consistently.
ex: A daily dashboard shows sales, margin, stock and overdue receivables by branch.
say: “Finance and sales report different revenue numbers — we need one governed BI definition.”
rel: Dashboard; data warehouse; KPI; analytics; semantic layer.
vs: BI (what happened, mostly descriptive) vs advanced analytics (why and what will happen).
:::

:::term Descriptive, predictive and prescriptive analytics | التحليلات الوصفية والتنبؤية والتوجيهية | 
def: Descriptive analytics explains what happened; diagnostic why it happened; predictive what is likely to happen; prescriptive what action to take.
ar: التحليلات الوصفية تشرح ما حدث، والتشخيصية لماذا حدث، والتنبؤية ما يُرجَّح أن يحدث، والتوجيهية الإجراء الذي ينبغي اتخاذه.
why: Clarifies the maturity and value of analytics work.
use: Prescriptive examples: price optimisation, inventory replenishment recommendations.
ex: Descriptive: Q2 sales fell 8%. Diagnostic: two distributors lost. Predictive: churn model flags 15 more at risk. Prescriptive: offer retention terms to the top 5.
say: “We’re strong on descriptive reporting; the opportunity is predictive collections.”
rel: BI; machine learning; forecasting; optimisation.
vs: Correlation in descriptive data does not prove causation.
:::

:::term Cohort analysis | تحليل المجموعات (الأفواج) | 
def: Tracking groups of customers who share a starting characteristic (e.g., month of acquisition) over time to measure retention, revenue and behaviour.
ar: تتبّع مجموعات من العملاء يشتركون في خاصية بداية (كشهر الاكتساب) عبر الزمن لقياس الاحتفاظ والإيرادات والسلوك.
why: Reveals whether newer customers behave better or worse — hidden in aggregate numbers.
use: Essential in subscription, e-commerce and lending (vintage analysis).
ex: Customers acquired in January retain 60% at month 6; those from March only 40% after a discount campaign.
say: “The cohort chart shows the promotion brought low-quality customers.”
rel: Churn; LTV; vintage analysis; retention curve.
vs: Cohort view (by start group) vs period view (all customers in a month).
:::

## Commodity trading terms

:::term Physical trading | التجارة الفعلية للسلع | 
def: Buying, transporting, storing, blending and selling actual commodities (as opposed to only trading financial contracts), earning margins from location, time, quality and logistics arbitrage.
ar: شراء السلع الفعلية ونقلها وتخزينها وخلطها وبيعها (وليس الاكتفاء بتداول العقود المالية)، وتحقيق الهوامش من فروقات المكان والزمان والجودة والخدمات اللوجستية.
why: Margins are typically thin and depend on logistics, financing and risk management; operational failures can wipe out profits.
use: Traders combine physical contracts with futures/swaps hedges.
ex: Buy wheat FOB Black Sea, sell CFR Egypt, earning the spread minus freight, insurance, financing and losses.
say: “It’s a physical deal — we need vessel nomination, inspection and an L/C, not just a futures position.”
rel: Paper trading; arbitrage; basis; freight; trade finance.
vs: Physical trading (delivery of goods) vs paper/financial trading (cash-settled or closed out before delivery).
:::

:::term Benchmark price | السعر المرجعي | 
def: A published reference price for a commodity used to price physical contracts and derivatives — e.g., Brent and WTI for crude oil, LME prices for base metals, exchange futures for grains.
ar: سعر مرجعي منشور لسلعة ما يُستخدم لتسعير العقود الفعلية والمشتقات، مثل برنت وغرب تكساس للنفط، وأسعار بورصة لندن للمعادن، وعقود البورصات للحبوب.
why: Most physical contracts price as benchmark ± premium/discount (differential), so benchmark moves create price risk until hedged.
use: Price-reporting agencies (e.g., Platts, Argus) publish assessments; exchanges publish settlement prices.
ex: Cargo priced at the average of Dated Brent over 5 days around B/L date, plus $1.20/bbl.
say: “We’re long the cargo at a fixed price; let’s hedge with Brent futures until we sell on floating terms.”
rel: Differential; premium; pricing period; futures; basis.
vs: Benchmark (reference) vs actual transaction price (benchmark ± differential).
:::

:::term Basis | الأساس (فرق السعر) | 
def: The difference between the local physical (cash) price of a commodity and the price of a related futures contract: basis = cash − futures.
ar: الفرق بين السعر الفعلي المحلي للسلعة وسعر العقد الآجل المرتبط بها في البورصة: الأساس = السعر النقدي − سعر العقد المستقبلي.
why: Hedging with futures removes outright price risk but leaves basis risk.
use: Grain merchants trade basis; contracts are quoted as basis over a futures month.
ex: Local corn $4.10/bu; futures $4.40 → basis −$0.30 (“30 under”).
say: “Our hedge worked on flat price, but the basis moved 15 cents against us.”
rel: Basis risk; hedging; spread; futures; differential.
vs: Basis (cash vs futures) vs spread (difference between two futures months or two products).
:::

:::term Contango and backwardation | الكونتانجو والباكوردايشن | 
def: Contango: futures prices for later delivery are higher than nearer ones (upward-sloping forward curve). Backwardation: later prices are lower than nearer ones (downward-sloping).
ar: الكونتانجو: أسعار العقود الآجلة للتسليم البعيد أعلى من القريبة (منحنى صاعد). الباكوردايشن: أسعار التسليم البعيد أقل من القريبة (منحنى هابط).
why: Contango rewards storage (buy now, sell forward) if the spread exceeds storage and financing costs; backwardation signals tight current supply.
use: Storage plays, roll yield for index investors, inventory decisions.
ex: Spot $70, 6-month $73.50; storage + financing $2.80 → carry trade profit about $0.70/bbl.
say: “The curve is in steep contango — floating storage economics work again.”
rel: Forward curve; carry; storage; roll yield; spread.
vs: Contango/backwardation describe the curve shape, not whether prices are rising or falling.
:::

:::term Laytime and demurrage (charter party) | مهلة التحميل والتفريغ والديمرج | 
def: Laytime is the time allowed in a charter party for loading and discharging; demurrage is the agreed daily compensation paid to the shipowner if laytime is exceeded (despatch may be paid for finishing early).
ar: مهلة التحميل والتفريغ هي الوقت المسموح به في عقد إيجار السفينة لعمليات التحميل والتفريغ، والديمرج تعويض يومي متفق عليه يُدفع لمالك السفينة عند تجاوز هذه المهلة.
why: Port congestion can make demurrage a major cost in commodity deals; it must be passed through sales contracts.
use: Notice of readiness (NOR) starts laytime under contract conditions.
ex: Laytime 3 days, actual 6 days, demurrage $25,000/day → $75,000.
say: “Make sure our sales contract passes demurrage back to the buyer on the same terms as the charter party.”
rel: Charter party; NOR; despatch; voyage charter.
vs: Vessel demurrage (charter party) vs container demurrage (liner terminal charges).
:::

:::answer Answers: Chapters 10–12
1. Objective: “Make our supply chain cost-competitive.” KR1: reduce average landed cost per unit by 6% by Q4; KR2: qualify two alternative suppliers for the top 10 SKUs; KR3: cut demurrage and detention charges by 50%.
2. Rivalry (many small truckers, price competition), buyer power (shipping lines and large forwarders), threat of entry (low capital barriers), supplier power (fuel, drivers, port access). Substitutes are limited (rail, if available).
3. Post-money $12m; investor 25%; founders **75%**. With the pool pre-money: pool 10% of post-money ($1.2m) comes from the pre-money; founders = 100% − 25% − 10% = **65%**.
4. Spread 9,120 − 9,000 = $120 > carry $85 → profit about **$35/t** (before transaction costs). Curve in **contango**.
5. Basis risk (local price may move differently from futures), quantity/timing mismatch, quality differences, and margin-call liquidity needs.
