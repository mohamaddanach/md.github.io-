# Accounting Terminology

:::obj In this chapter
Domain 3: the vocabulary of measurement and reporting. Part III explains the mechanics in depth; here the goal is to recognise and use the terms correctly.
:::

## Core accounting terms

:::term Accrual basis of accounting | أساس الاستحقاق | 
def: Recording revenues when earned and expenses when incurred, regardless of when cash is received or paid. Required by IFRS and US GAAP for general-purpose financial statements.
ar: تسجيل الإيرادات عند تحققها والمصاريف عند تكبّدها بصرف النظر عن موعد استلام النقد أو دفعه. وهو الأساس المطلوب في المعايير الدولية (IFRS) والمبادئ الأمريكية (US GAAP).
why: Matches costs to the revenue they generate, giving a truer picture of performance than cash timing — but creates the gap between profit and cash.
use: Accountants book accruals (expenses incurred, not invoiced) and prepayments (paid in advance) at month-end.
ex: December electricity used but billed in January is accrued as a December expense.
say: “On an accruals basis the project made money in Q2, even though most cash came in Q3.”
rel: Cash basis; matching principle; accrued expenses; prepayments; deferred revenue.
vs: Cash basis records only cash movements; used by some very small businesses and for some tax purposes.
:::

:::term Double-entry bookkeeping | القيد المزدوج | 
def: The system in which every transaction is recorded in at least two accounts, with total debits equal to total credits, keeping Assets = Liabilities + Equity in balance.
ar: نظام يُسجَّل فيه كل حدث مالي في حسابين على الأقل، بحيث يتساوى مجموع المدين مع مجموع الدائن، فتبقى معادلة الميزانية متوازنة: الأصول = الالتزامات + حقوق الملكية.
why: It provides an internal check on accuracy and makes the three financial statements consistent.
use: Every ERP and accounting system is built on double entry; reading a journal entry is a basic professional skill.
ex: Buying inventory on credit for $10,000: Debit Inventory 10,000; Credit Accounts payable 10,000.
say: “What’s the other side of that entry — did it hit cash or payables?”
rel: Debit; credit; journal; general ledger; trial balance.
vs: Debit and credit do not mean “good” and “bad”: a debit increases assets and expenses; a credit increases liabilities, equity and revenue.
:::

:::term Asset | الأصل | 
def: A resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events, from which future economic benefits are expected (IFRS Conceptual Framework: a present economic resource with the potential to produce economic benefits).
ar: مورد تسيطر عليه المنشأة نتيجة أحداث سابقة، ويُتوقّع أن تتدفق منه منافع اقتصادية مستقبلية، مثل النقد والذمم المدينة والمخزون والمعدات.
why: Assets show what the company has to generate revenue and what lenders could rely on as collateral.
use: Classified as current (expected to be realised within 12 months) and non-current.
ex: A delivery truck, a customer receivable and a patent are all assets.
say: “Most of the asset base is receivables, which is good for a borrowing-base facility.”
rel: Current asset; fixed asset; intangible asset; balance sheet.
vs: An employee’s skill is valuable but is not recognised as an accounting asset because the company does not control it.
:::

:::term Liability | الالتزام / المطلوبات | 
def: A present obligation of the entity to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events: loans, payables, taxes owed, provisions, deferred revenue.
ar: التزام حالي على المنشأة بتحويل مورد اقتصادي نتيجة أحداث سابقة، مثل القروض والذمم الدائنة والضرائب المستحقة والمخصصات والإيرادات المؤجلة.
why: Liabilities determine solvency and liquidity; hidden or off-balance-sheet liabilities are a classic DD finding.
use: Current liabilities (due within 12 months) vs non-current.
ex: A customer’s $50k advance payment is a liability (deferred revenue) until goods are delivered.
say: “There may be an unrecorded liability for end-of-service benefits.”
rel: Debt; payables; provision; contingent liability.
vs: “Liability” in accounting (obligation on the balance sheet) vs legal liability (responsibility for damages).
:::

:::term Equity | حقوق الملكية | 
def: The residual interest in the assets after deducting liabilities: share capital, retained earnings and other reserves.
ar: الحق المتبقي في أصول المنشأة بعد طرح جميع الالتزامات، ويشمل رأس المال والأرباح المحتجزة والاحتياطيات.
why: Equity is the shareholders’ cushion that absorbs losses before creditors are affected.
use: Book value of equity (accounting) differs from market value (share price × shares).
ex: Assets $10m, liabilities $6.5m → equity $3.5m.
say: “Book equity is only $3.5m, so a $1m loss would be material for the bank.”
rel: Net worth; book value; retained earnings; tangible net worth.
vs: Book equity (accounting) vs market capitalisation (market value) vs “equities” (shares as an asset class).
:::

:::term Depreciation | الإهلاك / الاستهلاك | 
def: The systematic allocation of the cost of a tangible fixed asset, less residual value, over its useful life.
ar: التوزيع المنتظم لتكلفة الأصل الثابت الملموس، بعد طرح القيمة المتبقية، على عمره الإنتاجي.
why: Spreads the cost of assets over the periods that benefit; it is a non-cash expense that reduces taxable profit in most tax systems (tax depreciation rules may differ).
use: Methods: straight-line, declining balance, units of production.
ex: Truck cost $60k, residual $10k, life 5 years → straight-line $10k per year.
say: “Depreciation is non-cash, so add it back when you estimate operating cash flow.”
rel: Amortisation; useful life; residual value; accumulated depreciation; net book value.
vs: Depreciation (tangible assets) vs amortisation (intangibles) vs impairment (sudden write-down).
:::

:::term Amortisation | الإطفاء | 
def: (1) The systematic allocation of the cost of an intangible asset with a finite life (software, licences, patents). (2) In lending, the gradual repayment of loan principal over time.
ar: (1) التوزيع المنتظم لتكلفة أصل غير ملموس ذي عمر محدد كالبرمجيات والتراخيص وبراءات الاختراع. (2) وفي الإقراض: السداد التدريجي لأصل القرض على أقساط.
why: Both meanings are common; context tells you which.
use: “Amortising loan” = principal repaid in instalments; “bullet loan” = principal repaid at maturity.
ex: Software licence $300k over 3 years → $100k amortisation per year.
say: “The term loan amortises over five years with a 30% balloon.”
rel: Depreciation; intangible asset; amortisation schedule; bullet; balloon.
vs: Goodwill is not amortised under IFRS (it is tested for impairment); private companies under some GAAP options may amortise it.
:::

:::term Impairment | انخفاض القيمة | 
def: A reduction in the carrying amount of an asset to its recoverable amount when that amount is lower than the book value.
ar: تخفيض القيمة الدفترية للأصل إلى قيمته القابلة للاسترداد عندما تكون أقل من قيمته المسجلة.
why: Impairments reveal that past investments or acquisitions have not delivered expected returns; large goodwill impairments often follow overpriced acquisitions.
use: Tested annually for goodwill (IAS 36) and when indicators exist for other assets; for financial assets, expected credit loss (IFRS 9) applies.
ex: A plant carried at $20m generates cash flows worth $14m → impairment loss $6m.
say: “The goodwill impairment suggests we overpaid for the acquisition.”
rel: Write-down; recoverable amount; goodwill; expected credit loss.
vs: Impairment (unexpected loss in value) vs depreciation (planned allocation).
:::

:::term Provision | المخصص | 
def: A liability of uncertain timing or amount (IAS 37), recognised when there is a present obligation from a past event, an outflow is probable and the amount can be reliably estimated — e.g., warranties, legal claims, restructuring.
ar: التزام غير مؤكد التوقيت أو المبلغ، يُعترف به عند وجود التزام حالي ناتج عن حدث سابق، ويكون خروج الموارد محتملاً ويمكن تقدير المبلغ بشكل موثوق، مثل الضمانات والقضايا وإعادة الهيكلة.
why: Provisions bring future costs into today’s profit; analysts watch for under-provisioning or “cookie-jar” reserves.
use: Banks also use “provision” loosely for loan-loss allowances.
ex: Warranty claims average 2% of sales → provision of $240k on $12m sales.
say: “We should provide for the claim — our lawyers think it’s more likely than not we’ll pay.”
rel: Contingent liability; accrual; allowance; IAS 37.
vs: Provision (recognised liability) vs contingent liability (possible obligation, disclosed only) vs reserve (part of equity).
:::

:::term Deferred revenue | الإيرادات المؤجلة | 
def: Cash received (or receivable) for goods or services not yet delivered; a liability (contract liability) until the performance obligation is satisfied.
ar: نقد مقبوض مقدماً عن بضائع أو خدمات لم تُسلَّم بعد، ويُعدّ التزاماً (التزام تعاقدي) إلى أن يتم الوفاء بالأداء.
why: Common in subscriptions, software and projects; high deferred revenue is often a sign of strong cash dynamics.
use: SaaS investors watch growth in deferred revenue as a leading indicator.
ex: Annual subscription $12k paid upfront in January → $1k revenue per month; deferred revenue at end-January $11k.
say: “Cash is ahead of revenue because customers prepay annually — deferred revenue is up 30%.”
rel: Contract liability; unearned revenue; billings; IFRS 15.
vs: Deferred revenue (we received, not yet earned) vs accrued revenue (earned, not yet billed).
:::

:::term Cost of goods sold | تكلفة البضاعة المبيعة | COGS
def: The direct costs of the goods sold in a period: purchase or production cost, inbound freight, duties and other costs to bring inventory to its present location and condition.
ar: التكاليف المباشرة للبضائع المبيعة خلال الفترة، وتشمل تكلفة الشراء أو الإنتاج والشحن الوارد والرسوم الجمركية وأي تكاليف لازمة لإيصال المخزون إلى موقعه وحالته الحالية.
why: Determines gross margin; misclassifying costs (e.g., putting freight in opex) distorts margins.
use: COGS = opening inventory + purchases − closing inventory.
ex: Opening stock $1.8m + purchases $9.3m − closing $2.7m = COGS $8.4m.
say: “Freight-in should sit in COGS, otherwise our gross margin looks better than it is.”
rel: Cost of sales; inventory; landed cost; gross profit.
vs: COGS (cost of what was sold) vs purchases (what was bought, including unsold stock).
:::

:::term Retained earnings | الأرباح المحتجزة | 
def: Cumulative net income kept in the company after dividends since inception; part of equity.
ar: صافي الأرباح المتراكمة التي احتفظت بها الشركة منذ تأسيسها بعد توزيع الأرباح، وهي جزء من حقوق الملكية.
why: Shows how much the company has financed itself; often limits distributable dividends.
use: Closing RE = opening RE + net income − dividends.
ex: 2,000k + 680k − 200k = 2,480k.
say: “Retained earnings have been funding growth; we have never raised new equity.”
rel: Equity; reserves; dividend; accumulated losses.
vs: Retained earnings are not cash; they may be invested in inventory or equipment.
:::

:::term Goodwill | الشهرة | 
def: The excess of the price paid in a business combination over the fair value of identifiable net assets acquired; represents expected synergies, workforce, reputation.
ar: الزيادة في ثمن الاستحواذ على شركة عن القيمة العادلة لصافي أصولها القابلة للتحديد، وتمثّل التآزر المتوقع والسمعة وفريق العمل.
why: Large goodwill means the buyer paid for future expectations; it is impaired if those expectations fail.
use: Lenders often deduct goodwill to compute tangible net worth.
ex: Price $50m; fair value of net assets $35m → goodwill $15m.
say: “Tangible net worth is negative once you strip out the goodwill from past acquisitions.”
rel: Intangible assets; purchase price allocation; impairment; tangible net worth.
vs: Acquired goodwill is recognised; internally generated goodwill is not.
:::

:::term Audit opinion | رأي المدقق | 
def: The independent auditor’s conclusion on whether financial statements are presented fairly, in all material respects, under the applicable framework. Types: unmodified (clean), qualified, adverse, disclaimer.
ar: استنتاج المدقق المستقل بشأن ما إذا كانت القوائم المالية معروضة بعدل من جميع النواحي الجوهرية وفق الإطار المحاسبي المطبق. أنواعه: رأي غير معدل (نظيف)، ومتحفظ، وعكسي، وامتناع عن إبداء الرأي.
why: A qualified opinion or “material uncertainty related to going concern” paragraph is a red flag for lenders and investors.
use: Banks usually require audited statements within a set number of days after year-end.
ex: Qualified opinion because the auditor could not verify inventory counts at a remote warehouse.
say: “The accounts carry a going-concern paragraph — we need to understand the refinancing plan.”
rel: External audit; ISA; management letter; going concern; material misstatement.
vs: Audit (reasonable assurance) vs review (limited assurance) vs compilation (no assurance).
:::

## Financial terminology

:::term Time value of money | القيمة الزمنية للنقود | TVM
def: The principle that a sum of money today is worth more than the same sum in the future, because it can be invested to earn a return and because of inflation and risk.
ar: مبدأ يقضي بأن المبلغ اليوم أعلى قيمة من المبلغ نفسه في المستقبل، لأنه يمكن استثماره لتحقيق عائد، ولتأثير التضخم والمخاطر.
why: Every investment, loan, lease and valuation decision depends on it; it is why payment terms are a price variable.
use: Discounting future cash flows to present value; comparing payment terms (cash now vs 90 days).
ex: At 8% per year, $108 received in a year is worth $100 today.
say: “Offering 120-day terms is effectively a discount worth about 2.6% at our cost of funds.”
rel: Present value; future value; discount rate; compounding; NPV.
vs: TVM is about timing; inflation is one reason for it but not the only one.
:::

:::term Net present value | صافي القيمة الحالية | NPV
def: The sum of the present values of all expected cash flows of a project or investment, including the initial outlay, discounted at an appropriate rate.
ar: مجموع القيم الحالية لجميع التدفقات النقدية المتوقعة لمشروع أو استثمار، بما فيها الإنفاق الأولي، مخصومة بمعدل خصم مناسب.
why: A positive NPV means the project is expected to create value above the required return; it is the theoretically preferred investment criterion.
use: Capital budgeting committees, project finance, M&A valuations.
ex: Invest $1,000k; receive $400k per year for 3 years; rate 10% → NPV = −1,000 + 363.6 + 330.6 + 300.5 = −$5.3k (marginally negative).
say: “At an 11% WACC the NPV is negative; the project only works if we secure the volume contract.”
rel: IRR; discount rate; WACC; DCF; payback.
vs: NPV (absolute value created, in money) vs IRR (rate of return, in %).
:::

:::term Internal rate of return | معدل العائد الداخلي | IRR
def: The discount rate at which a project’s NPV equals zero — the annualised return implied by its cash flows.
ar: معدل الخصم الذي تصبح عنده صافي القيمة الحالية للمشروع صفراً، وهو العائد السنوي الضمني لتدفقاته النقدية.
why: Intuitive (“the project returns 18%”) and widely used in private equity and project finance, but can mislead for unconventional cash flows or mutually exclusive projects.
use: Compare IRR to the hurdle rate; private equity targets equity IRRs (e.g., 20%+), project lenders look at project IRR.
ex: The project above has an IRR of about 9.7% — just below the 10% hurdle.
say: “Equity IRR is 22% in the base case and 14% in the downside.”
rel: NPV; hurdle rate; MOIC; cost of capital.
vs: IRR assumes reinvestment at the IRR; a high IRR on a small, short project can create less value than a lower IRR on a big one.
:::

:::term Weighted average cost of capital | المتوسط المرجّح لتكلفة رأس المال | WACC
def: The average rate of return a company must pay its capital providers, weighted by the market values of debt and equity, with the cost of debt taken after tax.
ar: متوسط معدل العائد الذي يجب على الشركة دفعه لمموّليها، مرجّحاً بالقيم السوقية للدين وحقوق الملكية، مع احتساب تكلفة الدين بعد الضريبة.
why: Used as the discount rate for projects of average risk and in DCF valuations.
use: WACC = E/(D+E) × Ke + D/(D+E) × Kd × (1 − t).
ex: 60% equity at 14%, 40% debt at 7% pre-tax, tax 20% → WACC = 8.4% + 2.24% = 10.64%.
say: “Using group WACC for a project in a riskier country understates the risk; add a country premium.”
rel: Cost of equity; cost of debt; CAPM; hurdle rate; capital structure.
vs: WACC (company-wide average) vs project-specific discount rate (adjusted for project risk).
:::

:::term Free cash flow | التدفق النقدي الحر | FCF
def: Cash generated after operating expenses, taxes, working-capital investment and capital expenditure — the cash available to repay debt, pay dividends or invest. Free cash flow to the firm (FCFF) is before debt service; free cash flow to equity (FCFE) is after.
ar: النقد المتولد بعد المصاريف التشغيلية والضرائب والاستثمار في رأس المال العامل والنفقات الرأسمالية، وهو النقد المتاح لسداد الديون أو توزيع الأرباح أو الاستثمار.
why: The basis of DCF valuation and of a lender’s view of repayment capacity.
use: FCFF = EBIT × (1 − t) + D&A − capex − ΔNWC.
ex: EBIT 1,200; tax 15%; D&A 300; capex 500; ΔNWC 1,800 → FCFF = 1,020 + 300 − 500 − 1,800 = −980.
say: “FCF has been negative for three years — the business consumes cash as it grows.”
rel: Operating cash flow; EBITDA; DCF; cash conversion.
vs: FCF ≠ EBITDA ≠ net income; definitions vary between companies.
:::

:::term Enterprise value | قيمة المنشأة | EV
def: The value of a company’s operating business to all capital providers: equity value + net debt (+ minority interests + preferred equity, − non-operating assets as appropriate).
ar: قيمة النشاط التشغيلي للشركة لجميع مموّليها: قيمة حقوق الملكية + صافي الدين (مع تعديلات للحصص غير المسيطرة والأسهم الممتازة والأصول غير التشغيلية).
why: Allows comparing companies regardless of capital structure; paired with EBITDA, EBIT or revenue multiples.
use: In acquisitions, the headline price is often EV (“cash-free, debt-free”), and the equity price is derived after deducting net debt.
ex: Market cap $500m + debt $200m − cash $50m = EV $650m.
say: “We agreed an EV of $120m on a cash-free, debt-free basis; the equity cheque depends on closing net debt.”
rel: Equity value; net debt; EV/EBITDA; market capitalisation.
vs: EV (whole business) vs equity value (shareholders’ part). See {{ch:Business Valuation}}.
:::

:::term Net debt | صافي الدين | 
def: Total interest-bearing debt minus cash and cash equivalents. Definitions may include leases, shareholder loans, pension deficits or exclude restricted cash.
ar: إجمالي الديون التي تحمل فوائد مطروحاً منه النقد وما في حكمه. قد يشمل التعريف عقود الإيجار وقروض المساهمين أو يستبعد النقد المقيّد.
why: Central to leverage covenants and to the EV-to-equity bridge in acquisitions; definitions are heavily negotiated.
use: Facility agreements and SPAs define net debt precisely.
ex: Bank loans 4.2m + leases 0.6m − cash 0.4m = net debt 4.4m (if leases included).
say: “Is customer prepayment treated as debt-like in the net debt definition?”
rel: Leverage; EV; debt-like items; covenant.
vs: Gross debt (total borrowings) vs net debt (after cash).
:::

:::term Hurdle rate | معدل العائد المطلوب | 
def: The minimum acceptable rate of return for an investment, set by the company or investor, often WACC plus a risk premium.
ar: الحد الأدنى للعائد المقبول على الاستثمار، وتحدده الشركة أو المستثمر، وغالباً ما يساوي تكلفة رأس المال مضافاً إليها علاوة مخاطر.
why: Projects below the hurdle destroy value (or do not compensate risk).
use: Different hurdles for different risk classes (e.g., 10% for replacement, 15% for new markets).
ex: Group hurdle 12%; project IRR 10.5% → rejected unless strategic.
say: “It clears the 12% hurdle only in the upside scenario.”
rel: WACC; IRR; cost of capital; risk premium.
vs: Hurdle rate (policy threshold) vs WACC (estimated cost of funds).
:::

:::term Payback period | فترة الاسترداد | 
def: The time required for cumulative cash inflows from a project to recover the initial investment.
ar: الفترة اللازمة لتغطية الاستثمار الأولي من التدفقات النقدية المتراكمة للمشروع.
why: Simple measure of liquidity risk; popular with owner-managers and in uncertain environments.
use: Often used together with NPV/IRR; discounted payback uses present values.
ex: Invest $300k, save $100k a year → payback 3 years.
say: “The board wants payback under three years for any IT investment.”
rel: NPV; IRR; ROI; breakeven.
vs: Payback ignores cash flows after the payback date and (in simple form) the time value of money.
:::

:::term Capital structure | هيكل رأس المال | 
def: The mix of debt and equity (and hybrid instruments) used to finance a company’s assets.
ar: مزيج الدين وحقوق الملكية (والأدوات الهجينة) المستخدم في تمويل أصول الشركة.
why: Affects risk, cost of capital, control and flexibility; too much debt risks distress, too little may waste the tax shield and dilute owners.
use: CFOs set target leverage ranges; rating agencies and banks assess it.
ex: A utility with stable cash flows may run 60% debt; a startup is mostly equity.
say: “We’d like to optimise the capital structure ahead of the expansion — perhaps a term loan instead of new equity.”
rel: Leverage; WACC; gearing; hybrid capital.
vs: Capital structure (long-term financing mix) vs working-capital financing (short-term).
:::

## Investment terminology

:::term Asset class | فئة الأصول | 
def: A group of investments with similar characteristics and behaviour: equities, fixed income, cash, real estate, commodities, private equity, hedge funds, infrastructure.
ar: مجموعة من الاستثمارات ذات خصائص وسلوك متشابه، مثل الأسهم والسندات والنقد والعقارات والسلع والملكية الخاصة.
why: Asset allocation across classes explains most of a diversified portfolio’s risk and return.
use: Investment mandates set allocation ranges per asset class.
ex: A pension fund allocates 45% equities, 35% bonds, 10% real estate, 10% alternatives.
say: “Private credit has become a distinct asset class for insurers.”
rel: Asset allocation; diversification; mandate; alternatives.
vs: Asset class (category) vs security (individual instrument).
:::

:::term Diversification | التنويع | 
def: Spreading investments across assets whose returns are not perfectly correlated to reduce portfolio risk without proportionally reducing expected return.
ar: توزيع الاستثمارات على أصول لا ترتبط عوائدها ارتباطاً تاماً، لتقليل مخاطر المحفظة دون خفض العائد المتوقع بالنسبة نفسها.
why: Eliminates idiosyncratic (company-specific) risk; systematic (market) risk remains.
use: Also applies to businesses: customer, supplier and country diversification.
ex: Two assets with 20% volatility each and correlation 0.2: an equal-weight portfolio has volatility ≈ 15.5%.
say: “Our customer base is not diversified — the top three customers are 70% of revenue.”
rel: Correlation; systematic risk; portfolio theory; concentration risk.
vs: Diversification reduces risk; it does not guarantee against loss in a market-wide fall.
:::

:::term Volatility | التقلّب | 
def: The degree of variation of an asset’s price or return over time, usually measured as the annualised standard deviation of returns.
ar: درجة تذبذب سعر الأصل أو عائده عبر الزمن، وتُقاس عادةً بالانحراف المعياري السنوي للعوائد.
why: A key risk measure and the main input to option pricing.
use: Implied volatility (from option prices) vs historical volatility (from past prices).
ex: Daily volatility of 1.2% ≈ annual 1.2% × √252 ≈ 19%.
say: “Volatility in freight rates makes fixed-price contracts dangerous.”
rel: Standard deviation; risk; VaR; option premium; beta.
vs: Volatility (fluctuation) is not the same as permanent loss of capital.
:::

:::term Yield | العائد | 
def: The income return on an investment, expressed as a percentage of its price or value: dividend yield, current yield, yield to maturity on bonds.
ar: العائد الدخلي على الاستثمار كنسبة مئوية من سعره أو قيمته، مثل عائد التوزيعات أو العائد حتى الاستحقاق على السندات.
why: Bond prices and yields move inversely; yields are the language of debt markets.
use: “The 10-year yields 4.2%”; “dividend yield 3.5%”.
ex: A bond paying $50 a year bought at $950: current yield = 5.26%.
say: “Yields have risen 80 basis points since we priced the deal, so the bond is trading below par.”
rel: Coupon; YTM; spread; basis point; yield curve.
vs: Coupon (fixed contractual interest on face value) vs yield (return based on current price).
:::

:::term Basis point | نقطة أساس | bp
def: One hundredth of one percentage point (0.01%). Used to express changes in interest rates, spreads and fees precisely.
ar: جزء من مئة من النقطة المئوية الواحدة (0.01%)، ويُستخدم للتعبير بدقة عن تغيّرات أسعار الفائدة والهوامش والرسوم.
why: Avoids ambiguity between “1% increase” (relative) and “1 percentage point” (absolute).
use: “Margin of 275 bps over SOFR”; “rates up 25 bps”.
ex: A rate rising from 5.00% to 5.25% has risen 25 bps.
say: “The bank came back at 225 bps; we were hoping for 200.”
rel: Percentage point; spread; margin; pip (FX).
vs: A move from 4% to 5% is +100 bps (+1 percentage point), which is a 25% relative increase.
:::

:::term Market capitalisation | القيمة السوقية | Market cap
def: Share price multiplied by the number of shares outstanding: the market value of a listed company’s equity.
ar: سعر السهم مضروباً في عدد الأسهم القائمة، أي القيمة السوقية لحقوق ملكية الشركة المدرجة.
why: Used to classify companies (large, mid, small cap), weight indices and compute EV.
use: Fully diluted market cap includes options and convertibles.
ex: 200m shares × $12.50 = $2.5bn.
say: “It’s a $2.5bn market cap but EV is $4bn because of the debt.”
rel: EV; float; index weighting; P/E.
vs: Market cap (equity only) vs EV (equity + net debt).
:::

:::term Liquidity (market) | سيولة السوق | 
def: The ability to buy or sell an asset quickly in size without moving its price significantly; indicated by bid-ask spreads, volume and market depth.
ar: القدرة على شراء الأصل أو بيعه بسرعة وبكميات كبيرة دون التأثير كثيراً في سعره، وتُقاس بفارق سعر العرض والطلب وحجم التداول وعمق السوق.
why: Illiquid assets require a premium and can trap investors in a crisis.
use: Fund managers match portfolio liquidity to investor redemption terms.
ex: A large-cap share trades $500m a day with a 0.02% spread; a small private bond may not trade for weeks.
say: “The position is too large relative to daily volume; exiting would take a week.”
rel: Bid-ask spread; depth; illiquidity premium; liquidity risk.
vs: Market liquidity (tradability of an asset) vs funding liquidity (a firm’s ability to pay).
:::

:::term Hedging | التحوّط | 
def: Taking a position that offsets an existing exposure to reduce risk from price, currency, interest-rate or commodity movements.
ar: اتخاذ مركز يعاكس تعرّضاً قائماً بهدف تقليل المخاطر الناتجة عن تحركات الأسعار أو العملات أو أسعار الفائدة أو السلع.
why: Protects margins and cash flows; lets a business focus on its core activity rather than on market bets.
use: Forwards, futures, options and swaps; natural hedges (matching revenue and costs in the same currency).
ex: An importer owing EUR 1m in 90 days buys EUR forward to fix the cost.
say: “We hedge 70% of forecast euro purchases on a rolling 12-month basis.”
rel: Derivative; forward; basis risk; speculation; treasury policy.
vs: Hedging reduces an existing exposure; speculation creates a new exposure to profit from price moves.
:::

:::exercise Practice questions
1. A customer prepays $24,000 for a 12-month maintenance contract on 1 April. What is deferred revenue at 30 June?
2. Distinguish provision, contingent liability and reserve with one example each.
3. A company has market cap $800m, debt $300m and cash $100m. What is EV? If EBITDA is $100m, what is EV/EBITDA?
4. A bank increases a loan margin from 2.75% to 3.10%. Express the change in basis points.
5. WACC: equity 70% at 15%, debt 30% at 8% pre-tax, tax rate 25%. Compute WACC.
:::

:::answer Answers to the practice questions
1. Three months earned ($6,000) → deferred revenue = **$18,000**.
2. Provision: warranty claims expected on products sold (probable, estimable — recognised). Contingent liability: a lawsuit the lawyers think is possible but not probable (disclosed only). Reserve: part of equity, e.g., a statutory reserve required by company law (not a liability).
3. EV = 800 + 300 − 100 = **$1,000m**; EV/EBITDA = **10.0×**.
4. +**35 bps**.
5. 0.7 × 15% + 0.3 × 8% × 0.75 = 10.5% + 1.8% = **12.3%**.
:::
