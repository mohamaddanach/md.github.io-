## Microeconomic and macroeconomic terms

:::term Inflation | التضخم | 
def: The rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services rises, reducing the purchasing power of money. Commonly measured by the consumer price index (CPI).
ar: معدل ارتفاع المستوى العام لأسعار السلع والخدمات، مما يقلل القوة الشرائية للنقود، ويُقاس عادةً بمؤشر أسعار المستهلك.
why: Inflation erodes margins on fixed-price contracts, raises wage demands and drives central-bank interest-rate decisions.
use: “Headline” CPI includes food and energy; “core” excludes them. Contracts include price-escalation clauses linked to an index.
ex: A 3-year fixed-price service contract signed when inflation was 2% becomes loss-making when costs rise 7% a year.
say: “We need an indexation clause — with inflation at 6%, a fixed price for three years is too risky.”
rel: CPI; PPI; real vs nominal; deflation; stagflation; indexation.
vs: Inflation (general price rise) vs a relative price change (one product becoming more expensive).
:::

:::term Real vs nominal | الحقيقي مقابل الاسمي | 
def: Nominal values are measured in current money; real values are adjusted for inflation. Real rate ≈ nominal rate − inflation.
ar: القيم الاسمية تُقاس بالنقود الجارية، أما القيم الحقيقية فمعدّلة بأثر التضخم. سعر الفائدة الحقيقي ≈ السعر الاسمي − التضخم.
why: Revenue growth of 8% with 9% inflation is a real decline in volume or price.
use: Economists report real GDP growth; investors target real returns.
ex: Deposit at 5% with inflation at 7% → real return ≈ −2%.
say: “In real terms, sales fell 1% — all of the growth was price.”
rel: Inflation; deflator; Fisher equation; purchasing power.
vs: The exact Fisher relation: (1 + nominal) = (1 + real)(1 + inflation).
:::

:::term Gross domestic product | الناتج المحلي الإجمالي | GDP
def: The total market value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a period.
ar: إجمالي القيمة السوقية لجميع السلع والخدمات النهائية المنتجة داخل بلد ما خلال فترة زمنية محددة.
why: The broadest measure of economic activity; drives demand for most businesses.
use: GDP = C + I + G + (X − M). Reported quarterly, often annualised (US) or year-on-year.
ex: Real GDP growth slowing from 4% to 1% signals weaker demand for construction materials.
say: “Our volumes track non-oil GDP more than headline GDP.”
rel: GNI; GDP per capita; recession; business cycle; nominal/real GDP.
vs: GDP (production within borders) vs GNI (income of residents, including from abroad).
:::

:::term Monetary policy | السياسة النقدية | 
def: Actions by a central bank to influence money supply, interest rates and credit conditions to achieve price stability and other mandates.
ar: الإجراءات التي يتخذها البنك المركزي للتأثير في عرض النقود وأسعار الفائدة وشروط الائتمان، بهدف تحقيق استقرار الأسعار وأهداف أخرى.
why: Policy rate changes pass through to loan pricing, deposit rates, exchange rates and asset prices.
use: Tools: policy rate, open-market operations, reserve requirements, quantitative easing/tightening, forward guidance. Countries with currency pegs (e.g., several GCC states to USD) generally follow the anchor’s rate moves.
ex: A 50 bps hike raises a company’s floating-rate interest bill on $20m debt by $100k a year.
say: “With a USD peg, our borrowing costs follow Fed decisions closely.”
rel: Central bank; policy rate; inflation targeting; quantitative easing; fiscal policy.
vs: Monetary policy (central bank, interest rates/money) vs fiscal policy (government spending and taxation).
:::

:::term Fiscal policy | السياسة المالية | 
def: Government decisions on spending, taxation and borrowing to influence the economy.
ar: قرارات الحكومة بشأن الإنفاق والضرائب والاقتراض للتأثير في الاقتصاد.
why: Government projects, subsidies and tax changes directly change demand in construction, healthcare, defence and many other sectors.
use: Budget deficit/surplus; public debt; stimulus; austerity.
ex: A government’s 20% cut in capital spending reduces orders for contractors and building materials.
say: “Fiscal consolidation next year will slow public project awards.”
rel: Budget deficit; government debt; taxation; stimulus.
vs: Fiscal (treasury/ministry of finance) vs monetary (central bank).
:::

:::term Exchange rate | سعر الصرف | FX rate
def: The price of one currency in terms of another. Regimes include free float, managed float, crawling peg and fixed peg.
ar: سعر عملة ما مقابل عملة أخرى. أنظمة الصرف تشمل التعويم الحر والتعويم المدار والربط الثابت.
why: Changes the cost of imports, the value of export revenue, the burden of foreign-currency debt and competitiveness.
use: Quoted as base/quote currency, e.g., EUR/USD 1.0850 means 1 EUR = 1.0850 USD.
ex: An importer paying EUR 500k at 1.05 pays $525k; at 1.15 pays $575k.
say: “Our margin is exposed to EUR/USD between order and payment.”
rel: Appreciation; depreciation; peg; FX risk; spot; forward.
vs: Depreciation (market-driven fall) vs devaluation (official lowering of a fixed rate).
:::

:::term Elasticity of demand | مرونة الطلب | 
def: The percentage change in quantity demanded resulting from a 1% change in price (price elasticity) or another variable (income, prices of substitutes).
ar: نسبة التغير في الكمية المطلوبة الناتجة عن تغير السعر بنسبة 1% (المرونة السعرية) أو عن تغير متغير آخر كالدخل أو أسعار البدائل.
why: Determines whether a price increase raises or lowers revenue.
use: Elastic (|e| > 1): price increases reduce revenue; inelastic (|e| < 1): price increases raise revenue.
ex: Price +10%, volume −4% → elasticity −0.4 (inelastic): revenue rises about 5.6%.
say: “Demand for spare parts is inelastic in an emergency — but customers remember gouging.”
rel: Demand curve; pricing power; substitutes; cross-elasticity.
vs: Elasticity varies along the curve and over time (short run vs long run).
:::

:::term Recession | الركود الاقتصادي | 
def: A significant, broad-based decline in economic activity lasting more than a few months; often (informally) two consecutive quarters of negative real GDP growth.
ar: تراجع كبير وواسع في النشاط الاقتصادي يستمر أكثر من بضعة أشهر، ويُعرَّف بشكل غير رسمي بربعين متتاليين من النمو السلبي للناتج المحلي الحقيقي.
why: Recessions raise defaults, cut demand and tighten credit; resilient business models are tested.
use: Official dating varies by country (e.g., the NBER in the US uses a broader assessment).
ex: In a recession, a bank raises credit-loss provisions and tightens lending criteria for SMEs.
say: “Our downside case assumes a mild recession with 15% lower volumes.”
rel: Business cycle; GDP; unemployment; depression; stagflation.
vs: Recession (decline) vs slowdown (slower growth, still positive).
:::

:::term Balance of payments | ميزان المدفوعات | BoP
def: The record of all economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world: current account (trade, income, transfers), capital account and financial account.
ar: سجل جميع المعاملات الاقتصادية بين المقيمين في بلد ما وبقية العالم، ويتكوّن من الحساب الجاري (التجارة والدخل والتحويلات) والحساب الرأسمالي والحساب المالي.
why: Persistent current-account deficits financed by short-term inflows can lead to currency crises, capital controls and payment delays — important country-risk signals.
use: Analysts check current account, reserves (months of import cover), external debt.
ex: A country with a current-account deficit of 8% of GDP and falling reserves may restrict FX access, delaying importers’ payments.
say: “The country’s FX reserves cover only two months of imports — we should require confirmed L/Cs.”
rel: Current account; trade balance; FX reserves; capital flows; country risk.
vs: Trade balance (goods/services only) vs current account (also income and transfers).
:::

:::term Market structure | هيكل السوق | 
def: The organisation of a market by the number and size of firms, product differentiation and entry barriers: perfect competition, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, monopoly.
ar: طريقة تنظيم السوق من حيث عدد الشركات وأحجامها وتمايز المنتجات وعوائق الدخول: منافسة كاملة، منافسة احتكارية، احتكار القلة، احتكار.
why: Structure drives pricing power, margins and strategy.
use: Competition authorities measure concentration (e.g., HHI) when reviewing mergers.
ex: Container shipping is an oligopoly with a few large carriers and alliances.
say: “It’s an oligopoly — any price move by one player is matched within weeks.”
rel: Monopoly; oligopoly; barriers to entry; HHI; competition law.
vs: Monopoly (one seller) vs monopsony (one buyer).
:::

## Sales and marketing terms

:::term Customer acquisition cost | تكلفة اكتساب العميل | CAC
def: Total sales and marketing cost incurred to acquire new customers in a period divided by the number of new customers acquired.
ar: إجمالي تكاليف المبيعات والتسويق خلال فترة مقسوماً على عدد العملاء الجدد الذين تم اكتسابهم فيها.
why: If CAC exceeds the profit a customer generates over its life, growth destroys value.
use: Compared with customer lifetime value (LTV) and payback months.
ex: $300k spent; 150 new customers → CAC = $2,000.
say: “Paid search CAC has doubled; referral CAC is a fifth of that.”
rel: LTV; LTV/CAC; payback; conversion rate; funnel.
vs: Blended CAC (all customers) vs paid CAC (only customers from paid channels).
:::

:::term Customer lifetime value | القيمة الدائمة للعميل | CLV / LTV
def: The present value of the gross profit (or contribution) a customer is expected to generate over the whole relationship.
ar: القيمة الحالية لمجمل الربح (أو المساهمة) الذي يُتوقّع أن يحققه العميل طوال فترة تعامله مع الشركة.
why: Determines how much can be spent to acquire and retain a customer.
use: Simple form: LTV = average monthly gross profit per customer ÷ monthly churn rate.
ex: $100 monthly gross profit; churn 2% → LTV ≈ $5,000; with CAC $2,000, LTV/CAC = 2.5.
say: “Our LTV to CAC is below 3, so we should slow paid acquisition until retention improves.”
rel: CAC; churn; retention; cohort analysis; unit economics.
vs: Revenue-based LTV overstates value; use gross profit or contribution.
:::

:::term Sales funnel | قمع المبيعات | 
def: The stages a prospect passes through from first awareness to purchase, with conversion rates between stages (e.g., lead → qualified lead → opportunity → proposal → won).
ar: المراحل التي يمر بها العميل المحتمل من أول معرفة بالمنتج حتى الشراء، مع نسب التحويل بين كل مرحلة وأخرى.
why: Shows where deals are lost and how many leads are needed to hit targets.
use: Sales managers forecast from pipeline value × stage probabilities.
ex: 400 leads → 120 qualified (30%) → 40 proposals (33%) → 10 won (25%).
say: “Our proposal-to-win rate is only 25%; we’re proposing to unqualified prospects.”
rel: Pipeline; conversion rate; lead; opportunity; win rate.
vs: Funnel (stage-based view of prospects) vs pipeline (value of open opportunities).
:::

:::term Lead | العميل المحتمل | 
def: A person or organisation that has shown potential interest in buying, or that matches the target profile, but has not yet been qualified.
ar: شخص أو جهة أبدت اهتماماً محتملاً بالشراء أو تطابق مواصفات العميل المستهدف، لكن لم يتم التحقق من جديته بعد.
why: Leads are the raw material of sales; quality matters more than quantity.
use: MQL (marketing-qualified lead), SQL (sales-qualified lead).
ex: A visitor downloading a whitepaper is a lead; after a call confirming budget and need, an SQL.
say: “Marketing sent 300 leads but only 20 were sales-qualified.”
rel: Prospect; qualification; BANT; MQL/SQL.
vs: Lead (unqualified interest) vs prospect (qualified potential buyer) vs opportunity (active deal).
:::

:::term Market segmentation | تجزئة السوق | 
def: Dividing a market into groups of customers with similar needs, behaviours or characteristics, so products, prices and messages can be tailored.
ar: تقسيم السوق إلى مجموعات من العملاء ذوي احتياجات أو سلوكيات أو خصائص متشابهة، لتصميم المنتجات والأسعار والرسائل التسويقية المناسبة لكل مجموعة.
why: Prevents trying to sell everything to everyone; allows price discrimination and focus.
use: B2B segmentation by industry, size, geography, buying behaviour; B2C by demographics, needs, behaviour.
ex: A logistics firm segments into e-commerce SMEs, industrial importers and project cargo.
say: “The mid-market segment has the best margins and the shortest sales cycle.”
rel: Targeting; positioning; ICP (ideal customer profile); persona.
vs: Segmentation (dividing) vs targeting (choosing which segments to serve).
:::

:::term Churn | معدل فقدان العملاء | 
def: The rate at which customers stop buying or cancel subscriptions in a period (customer churn), or the share of recurring revenue lost (revenue churn).
ar: نسبة العملاء الذين يتوقفون عن الشراء أو يلغون اشتراكاتهم خلال فترة، أو نسبة الإيرادات المتكررة المفقودة.
why: Small changes in churn create large changes in LTV and long-term growth.
use: Net revenue retention (NRR) combines churn, downgrades and upsells.
ex: Monthly churn 3% ≈ 31% of customers lost per year (1 − 0.97¹²).
say: “Gross churn is stable, but expansion revenue gives us NRR of 112%.”
rel: Retention; NRR; LTV; cohort.
vs: Customer churn (count) vs revenue churn (value).
:::

:::term Key account management | إدارة الحسابات الرئيسية | KAM
def: A strategic approach to managing a company’s most important customers with dedicated teams, plans and senior relationships.
ar: نهج استراتيجي لإدارة أهم عملاء الشركة عبر فرق وخطط مخصصة وعلاقات على مستوى الإدارة العليا.
why: A small number of accounts often produce most revenue and profit (Pareto).
use: Account plans include share-of-wallet goals, relationship maps and risk reviews.
ex: The top 12 accounts generate 55% of revenue; each has an account director.
say: “We need a joint business plan with the account for next year.”
rel: Account manager; share of wallet; customer success; CRM.
vs: Account management (growing existing customers) vs new business development (hunting new ones).
:::

## Negotiation and commercial terms

:::term BATNA | أفضل بديل للاتفاق المتفاوض عليه | BATNA
def: Best alternative to a negotiated agreement: what you will do if this negotiation fails. It determines your walk-away point.
ar: أفضل بديل متاح لك إذا فشل التفاوض الحالي، وهو الذي يحدد النقطة التي تنسحب عندها.
why: Negotiating power comes mainly from having a good alternative, not from rhetoric.
use: Prepare your BATNA and estimate the other side’s before any significant negotiation.
ex: A buyer with two qualified alternative suppliers at similar prices has a strong BATNA.
say: “Our BATNA is to stay with the current supplier at a 4% higher price — that’s our ceiling.”
rel: Reservation price; ZOPA; leverage; anchoring.
vs: BATNA (alternative course of action) vs bottom line (a number you set, which may be arbitrary).
:::

:::term ZOPA | منطقة الاتفاق الممكن | ZOPA
def: Zone of possible agreement: the range between the seller’s minimum acceptable price and the buyer’s maximum, within which a deal can be made.
ar: النطاق الواقع بين أدنى سعر يقبله البائع وأعلى سعر يقبله المشتري، والذي يمكن داخله إبرام الصفقة.
why: If there is no overlap, no deal is possible on price alone; parties must add other variables (terms, volume, timing).
use: Negotiators estimate the ZOPA and try to capture value within it.
ex: Seller’s minimum $90; buyer’s maximum $100 → ZOPA $90–$100.
say: “There’s no ZOPA on price, but extending payment terms could bridge the gap.”
rel: BATNA; reservation price; anchoring; trade-off.
vs: ZOPA is about the overlap; BATNA explains why each reservation price is where it is.
:::

:::term Quotation | عرض السعر | 
def: A seller’s statement of the price and terms on which it is willing to supply specified goods or services, usually with a validity period.
ar: بيان من البائع بالسعر والشروط التي يقبل بها توريد بضائع أو خدمات محددة، عادة مع مدة صلاحية.
why: Whether a quotation is a binding offer or merely an invitation to treat depends on wording and law; validity periods and conditions matter.
use: Includes Incoterm, delivery time, payment terms, validity, exclusions.
ex: “Price USD 48.50/unit FOB Ningbo, valid 15 days, 30% deposit, balance against B/L copy.”
say: “Please note our quotation is valid until 30 November and subject to material availability.”
rel: Offer; pro forma invoice; RFQ; acceptance; tender.
vs: Quotation (often non-binding) vs firm offer (binding if accepted within validity) vs pro forma invoice (preliminary invoice).
:::

:::term Payment terms | شروط الدفع | 
def: The agreed conditions for when and how payment is made: timing (advance, at sight, net 30/60/90), method (transfer, L/C, collection), currency, and discounts or penalties.
ar: الشروط المتفق عليها بشأن موعد الدفع وطريقته: التوقيت (مقدماً، عند الاطلاع، بعد 30 أو 60 أو 90 يوماً)، والوسيلة (تحويل، اعتماد مستندي، تحصيل)، والعملة، والخصومات أو الغرامات.
why: Payment terms move risk and financing cost between buyer and seller; they are as important as price.
use: “2/10 net 30” = 2% discount if paid in 10 days, otherwise due in 30.
ex: 30% advance, 70% against copy of bill of lading.
say: “We can meet your price if you accept 30% advance instead of 60 days open account.”
rel: Open account; L/C; DSO; early payment discount; trade credit.
vs: Payment terms (contractual) vs payment practice (what customers actually do).
:::

## Operations and supply chain terms

:::term Lead time | مهلة التوريد | 
def: The time between initiating a process (placing an order) and its completion (receipt of goods). Includes supplier production, transit and clearance.
ar: الفترة بين بدء العملية (تقديم الطلب) واكتمالها (استلام البضاعة)، وتشمل وقت الإنتاج لدى المورد والنقل والتخليص الجمركي.
why: Longer and more variable lead times require more safety stock and working capital.
use: Inputs to reorder points and production plans.
ex: China to Jebel Ali: 25 days production + 20 days transit + 5 days clearance = 50 days.
say: “Lead times have stretched to 10 weeks; we need to increase our order quantities.”
rel: Reorder point; safety stock; cycle time; transit time.
vs: Lead time (end-to-end) vs transit time (shipping only).
:::

:::term Safety stock | المخزون الاحتياطي (مخزون الأمان) | 
def: Extra inventory held to protect against variability in demand and lead time.
ar: مخزون إضافي يُحتفظ به للحماية من تقلبات الطلب وتأخر التوريد.
why: Balances service level against the cost of holding inventory.
use: Statistical formula: SS = z × σ(demand during lead time).
ex: z = 1.65 (95% service), σ of lead-time demand 400 units → SS ≈ 660 units.
say: “We cut safety stock to free cash, and stock-outs doubled.”
rel: Reorder point; service level; buffer stock; inventory turnover.
vs: Safety stock (buffer) vs cycle stock (normal replenishment quantity).
:::

:::term Reorder point | نقطة إعادة الطلب | ROP
def: The inventory level at which a new order should be placed: expected demand during lead time plus safety stock.
ar: مستوى المخزون الذي يجب عنده تقديم طلب جديد: الطلب المتوقع خلال مهلة التوريد مضافاً إليه مخزون الأمان.
why: Prevents stock-outs while avoiding excess inventory.
use: Configured in ERP/WMS systems to trigger purchase requisitions.
ex: Demand 100/day, lead time 20 days, SS 660 → ROP = 2,660 units.
say: “Reset the reorder points — lead times are no longer 20 days.”
rel: Safety stock; EOQ; lead time; MRP.
vs: ROP (when to order) vs EOQ (how much to order).
:::

:::term Landed cost | التكلفة الواصلة (حتى المستودع) | 
def: The total cost of a product delivered to the buyer’s premises: purchase price + freight + insurance + duties and taxes (non-recoverable) + port, clearance and handling charges + inland transport + financing and other costs.
ar: التكلفة الإجمالية للمنتج حتى وصوله إلى مقر المشتري: سعر الشراء + الشحن + التأمين + الرسوم والضرائب غير المستردة + رسوم الميناء والتخليص والمناولة + النقل الداخلي + تكاليف التمويل وغيرها.
why: Pricing and margin decisions based on supplier price alone systematically overstate profit.
use: Allocated per unit by value, weight or volume.
ex: Price $10.00 + freight $0.80 + insurance $0.03 + duty 5% on CIF $0.54 + local charges $0.35 → about $11.72 per unit.
say: “At landed cost the margin is 18%, not the 30% sales were quoting.”
rel: Incoterms; customs value; COGS; freight allocation.
vs: Landed cost (delivered) vs ex-works price (at seller’s door).
:::

:::term Inventory turnover | معدل دوران المخزون | 
def: COGS divided by average inventory: how many times inventory is sold and replaced in a period. Days inventory outstanding (DIO) = 365 ÷ turnover.
ar: تكلفة البضاعة المبيعة مقسومة على متوسط المخزون، أي عدد مرات بيع المخزون واستبداله خلال الفترة. وأيام المخزون = 365 ÷ معدل الدوران.
why: Low turnover ties up cash and signals obsolete or slow-moving stock.
use: Benchmarked by industry: grocery retail turns fast; heavy equipment slow.
ex: COGS $8.4m ÷ average inventory $2.25m = 3.7× → DIO ≈ 98 days.
say: “DIO increased by 20 days — there’s slow-moving stock in the outdoor range.”
rel: DIO; cash conversion cycle; obsolescence; ABC analysis.
vs: Inventory turnover (cost basis) is sometimes computed on revenue; be consistent.
:::

:::term Third-party logistics | الخدمات اللوجستية لطرف ثالث | 3PL
def: Outsourced logistics services — warehousing, transport, fulfilment, customs — provided by a specialised company.
ar: خدمات لوجستية يقدمها طرف متخصص بالنيابة عن الشركة، كالتخزين والنقل وتجهيز الطلبات والتخليص الجمركي.
why: Converts fixed logistics costs into variable ones and provides expertise and scale.
use: Contracts priced per pallet, per order, per shipment, with KPIs and SLAs.
ex: An e-commerce company pays a 3PL $1.20 per order picked and $12 per pallet per month.
say: “Moving to a 3PL would turn warehouse capex into a variable cost.”
rel: 4PL; freight forwarder; fulfilment; SLA.
vs: 3PL (executes logistics) vs 4PL (manages the whole logistics network, often orchestrating several 3PLs).
:::

## International trade terms

:::term Incoterms® 2020 | قواعد إنكوتيرمز 2020 | 
def: Eleven ICC rules defining the obligations, costs and risk transfer between seller and buyer for delivery of goods: EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP (any mode) and FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF (sea and inland waterway).
ar: إحدى عشرة قاعدة صادرة عن غرفة التجارة الدولية تحدد التزامات البائع والمشتري وتوزيع التكاليف ولحظة انتقال المخاطر عند تسليم البضائع.
why: A single three-letter rule decides who pays freight and insurance, who clears customs and when risk passes — central to pricing, insurance and L/C documents.
use: Always state the rule with a named place and version: “CIF Jebel Ali, Incoterms® 2020”.
ex: Under FOB Shanghai, risk passes when goods are on board the vessel at Shanghai; the buyer pays sea freight and insurance.
say: “Quote me CFR Dammam; we’ll arrange insurance locally.”
rel: Delivery; risk transfer; FOB; CIF; DAP; DDP.
vs: Incoterms do not deal with transfer of ownership (title), payment, or breach remedies — the sales contract does.
:::

:::term Bill of lading | بوليصة الشحن | B/L
def: A document issued by a carrier (or its agent) for sea transport that serves as (1) receipt for goods shipped, (2) evidence of the contract of carriage, and (3) when negotiable (“to order”), a document of title that controls the goods.
ar: مستند يصدره الناقل البحري أو وكيله، ويؤدي ثلاث وظائف: إيصال باستلام البضاعة، ودليل على عقد النقل، وسند ملكية يمثّل البضاعة إذا كان قابلاً للتداول (لأمر).
why: Whoever holds the original negotiable B/L can claim the goods; banks rely on it under L/Cs.
use: Clean (no notation of damage), on-board, to order, blank-endorsed; issued in sets of originals (often 3/3).
ex: “Full set of clean on-board ocean bills of lading made out to the order of the issuing bank, notify applicant.”
say: “We’ll release the original B/Ls once the balance payment is received.”
rel: Sea waybill; house B/L; master B/L; telex release; document of title.
vs: Negotiable B/L (document of title) vs sea waybill (non-negotiable; consignee named; no original needed to collect goods).
:::

:::term Customs value | القيمة الجمركية | 
def: The value of imported goods for calculating ad valorem duty, primarily the transaction value (price actually paid or payable) with adjustments, under the WTO Customs Valuation Agreement. Many countries use CIF value as the basis; some use FOB.
ar: قيمة البضائع المستوردة التي تُحتسب على أساسها الرسوم الجمركية القيمية، وتعتمد أساساً على قيمة الصفقة (الثمن المدفوع فعلاً أو المستحق) مع تعديلات، وفق اتفاقية القيمة الجمركية لمنظمة التجارة العالمية. تعتمد دول كثيرة قيمة CIF أساساً.
why: Under-valuation is customs fraud; over-valuation increases duty and can be a sign of trade-based money laundering.
use: Verify each country’s basis, duty rates by HS code, and taxes (e.g., VAT on duty-inclusive value).
ex: CIF value $50,000; duty 5% → $2,500; VAT 5% on (50,000 + 2,500) → $2,625 (illustrative; GCC rates should be verified).
say: “Customs rejected our declared value and uplifted it based on reference prices.”
rel: HS code; duty; transaction value; CIF; customs broker.
vs: Customs value vs invoice value (may differ due to adjustments: royalties, assists, freight).
:::

:::term HS code | رمز النظام المنسق | HS
def: The Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System maintained by the World Customs Organization: a 6-digit international product classification, extended to 8–12 digits nationally, used to determine tariffs, controls and statistics.
ar: النظام المنسق لتصنيف وترميز السلع الذي تديره منظمة الجمارك العالمية: تصنيف دولي من 6 أرقام، تمدده الدول إلى 8–12 رقماً، ويُستخدم لتحديد الرسوم والقيود والإحصاءات.
why: The code determines duty rate, licensing requirements and eligibility for preferential origin.
use: Misclassification leads to penalties, delays and back-duties.
ex: A different classification may change duty from 5% to 15% on the same goods.
say: “Let’s get a binding ruling on the HS code before we sign a delivered-duty-paid contract.”
rel: Tariff; customs classification; rules of origin; WCO.
vs: HS code (classification) vs certificate of origin (country of origin).
:::

:::term Freight forwarder | وكيل الشحن | 
def: A company that arranges the transport of goods on behalf of shippers: booking carriers, consolidating cargo, preparing documents, arranging insurance, customs clearance and delivery.
ar: شركة تنظم نقل البضائع نيابةً عن الشاحنين، فتحجز لدى الناقلين وتجمّع الشحنات وتعد المستندات وترتب التأمين والتخليص الجمركي والتسليم.
why: Most importers and exporters rely on forwarders rather than dealing directly with shipping lines and airlines.
use: May act as agent or as principal (NVOCC issuing house bills of lading).
ex: A forwarder consolidates five small shipments into one 40ft container (LCL to FCL).
say: “Our forwarder can get a better rate on the Shanghai–Jebel Ali lane through their contract with the carrier.”
rel: NVOCC; customs broker; carrier; house B/L; LCL/FCL.
vs: Forwarder (organises transport) vs carrier (performs it) vs customs broker (clears customs).
:::

:::term Demurrage and detention | غرامات التأخير: الديمرج والاحتجاز | D&D
def: Charges for using carrier-owned containers beyond agreed free time: demurrage typically applies while a full container remains in the port/terminal; detention while the container is outside the terminal (e.g., at the consignee’s warehouse) before being returned. Exact definitions vary by carrier and contract.
ar: رسوم تُفرض على استخدام حاويات الناقل بعد انتهاء الفترة المجانية: الديمرج عادةً عن بقاء الحاوية المحمّلة في الميناء، والاحتجاز عن بقائها خارج الميناء قبل إعادتها. تختلف التعريفات حسب الناقل والعقد. وفي الشحن الصب يعني الديمرج تعويض مالك السفينة عن تجاوز مدة التحميل أو التفريغ.
why: D&D can quickly exceed the freight cost if documents or customs clearance are delayed.
use: Negotiate free time in the freight contract; in bulk shipping, demurrage under a charter party compensates the shipowner for exceeding laytime.
ex: Free time 7 days; container collected on day 15 at $100/day → $800 demurrage.
say: “The B/Ls arrived late, so we’re paying demurrage on 12 containers.”
rel: Free time; laytime; charter party; storage charges.
vs: Container D&D (liner shipping) vs vessel demurrage (bulk/tanker charter parties).
:::

:::exercise Practice: Chapters 6–8
1. Your company’s revenue grew 7% while inflation was 9%. Describe the real change.
2. A price increase of 5% leads to a 12% fall in volume. Is demand elastic? What happens to revenue?
3. Monthly marketing spend $60k brings 40 customers; average gross profit per customer is $150/month with 2.5% monthly churn. Compute CAC, LTV and LTV/CAC.
4. Daily demand 50 units, lead time 30 days, safety stock 400. What is the reorder point?
5. Under FOB Ningbo, a container falls into the sea during loading before it is on board. Whose risk? Under CIF Jebel Ali, the vessel sinks mid-voyage. Whose risk, and who holds the insurance?
:::

:::answer Answers: Chapters 6–8
1. Real growth ≈ 1.07 ÷ 1.09 − 1 = **−1.8%**: in real terms the business shrank.
2. Elasticity = −12% ÷ 5% = −2.4 → **elastic**. Revenue ≈ 1.05 × 0.88 − 1 = **−7.6%**.
3. CAC = 60,000 ÷ 40 = **$1,500**; LTV ≈ 150 ÷ 0.025 = **$6,000**; LTV/CAC = **4.0** (healthy if the churn estimate is reliable).
4. ROP = 50 × 30 + 400 = **1,900 units**.
5. FOB: risk passes when goods are on board; before that it is the **seller’s** risk. CIF: risk passed to the **buyer** when goods were loaded at origin; the seller had to buy insurance for the buyer’s benefit (minimum Institute Cargo Clauses (C) cover under CIF in Incoterms® 2020), so the buyer claims under that policy.
:::
