# General Business and Corporate Terminology

:::obj In this chapter
Domain 1 (general business) and Domain 2 (corporate) vocabulary: the words used in every meeting regardless of industry, and the words that describe how companies are owned, governed and structured.
:::

## General business terminology

:::term Business model | نموذج العمل | 
def: The logic by which an organisation creates value for customers, delivers it, and captures part of that value as revenue and profit.
ar: نموذج العمل هو المنطق الذي تتبعه المنشأة لخلق قيمة لعملائها وتقديمها لهم والحصول على جزء من هذه القيمة في صورة إيرادات وأرباح.
why: Two companies in the same industry can have completely different economics because of their business models (e.g., selling software licences vs subscriptions).
use: Investors ask “what is the business model?” to understand who pays, for what, how often, and what it costs to serve them.
ex: An airline sells seats (transaction model); a freight marketplace earns a commission per booking (marketplace model).
say: “The business model depends on repeat orders; without them the acquisition cost never pays back.”
rel: Value proposition; revenue model; unit economics; Business Model Canvas.
vs: Business model ≠ business plan. The model is the logic; the plan is a document describing how to execute and finance it.
:::

:::term Value proposition | عرض القيمة | 
def: The specific benefit a product or service promises a defined customer, and why that customer should choose it over alternatives.
ar: عرض القيمة هو المنفعة المحددة التي يعد بها المنتج أو الخدمة لعميل معيّن، والسبب الذي يجعل هذا العميل يختاره بدلاً من البدائل.
why: Without a clear value proposition, sales become price-driven and margins collapse.
use: Written into proposals and pitch decks; tested in customer interviews.
ex: “We deliver spare parts to oil-field sites within 24 hours, cutting your downtime cost.”
say: “Our value proposition to the hotel chains is reliability of supply, not the lowest price.”
rel: Positioning; unique selling proposition; customer segment; competitive advantage.
vs: Value proposition (customer benefit) vs product features (what it does). Customers buy outcomes, not features.
:::

:::term Stakeholder | صاحب المصلحة | 
def: Any person or group that affects or is affected by an organisation’s decisions: shareholders, employees, customers, suppliers, lenders, regulators, communities.
ar: صاحب المصلحة هو أي شخص أو جهة تؤثّر في قرارات المنشأة أو تتأثر بها، مثل المساهمين والموظفين والعملاء والموردين والمقرضين والجهات الرقابية والمجتمع.
why: Projects fail when a key stakeholder (e.g., the finance director or a regulator) is not consulted.
use: Project managers produce stakeholder maps; executives “manage stakeholders” to secure approvals.
ex: For a new warehouse: board (approves capex), bank (finances), municipality (permits), staff (relocation).
say: “Before the board meeting we need to align the key stakeholders, especially the CFO.”
rel: Shareholder; stakeholder management; ESG; governance.
vs: Stakeholder ≠ shareholder. Shareholders own shares; stakeholders are anyone with an interest.
:::

:::term Fiscal year | السنة المالية | FY
def: The 12-month period a company uses for accounting and reporting. It may or may not coincide with the calendar year.
ar: السنة المالية هي فترة الاثني عشر شهراً التي تعتمدها المنشأة لأغراض المحاسبة وإعداد التقارير، وقد لا تتطابق مع السنة الميلادية.
why: Comparing companies requires aligning periods; tax and filing deadlines depend on the year-end.
use: “FY26” for a company with a March year-end usually means the year ending March 2026 — check the convention.
ex: A retailer may choose a January year-end to close the books after the holiday season.
say: “Our FY26 budget assumes 12% growth over FY25.”
rel: Quarter (Q1–Q4); year-to-date (YTD); last twelve months (LTM); reporting period.
vs: Fiscal year (company reporting period) vs tax year (set by tax law; may differ) vs government fiscal year.
:::

:::term Year-to-date | منذ بداية السنة حتى تاريخه | YTD
def: The period from the start of the current financial year to a given date, used to report cumulative results.
ar: الفترة من بداية السنة المالية الحالية حتى تاريخ معيّن، وتُستخدم لعرض النتائج التراكمية.
why: Management compares YTD actuals with YTD budget to see whether the year is on track.
use: Management reports show month, YTD and full-year forecast side by side.
ex: By 30 June, YTD revenue is $5.8m against a YTD budget of $6.1m (−5%).
say: “YTD we’re 5% behind budget, but the pipeline for Q3 should close most of the gap.”
rel: MTD; QTD; LTM/TTM; run rate; full-year forecast.
vs: YTD (cumulative this year) vs LTM (last twelve months, rolling across years).
:::

:::term Run rate | المعدّل السنوي المتوقّع | 
def: An annualised figure extrapolated from recent results, e.g., this month’s revenue × 12.
ar: رقم سنوي تقديري يُستنتج من النتائج الأخيرة، مثل ضرب إيرادات الشهر الحالي في 12.
why: Useful for fast-growing or changed businesses, but it can mislead when results are seasonal or a month was exceptional.
use: SaaS companies quote annual recurring revenue (ARR) run rate; cost-saving programmes quote “run-rate savings”.
ex: October revenue $400k → annual run rate $4.8m, although last year’s actual revenue was $3.1m.
say: “We exited the year at a $4.8m run rate, so FY27 revenue of $5.5m is achievable.”
rel: ARR; annualised; LTM; extrapolation.
vs: Run rate is an extrapolation, not a result. Always ask which period it is based on.
:::

:::term Economies of scale | وفورات الحجم | 
def: The reduction in average cost per unit as output increases, because fixed costs are spread over more units and larger buyers obtain better terms.
ar: انخفاض متوسط تكلفة الوحدة كلما زاد حجم الإنتاج، لأن التكاليف الثابتة تتوزع على عدد أكبر من الوحدات ولأن المشترين الكبار يحصلون على شروط أفضل.
why: Scale advantages create competitive moats and explain consolidation in shipping, retail and banking.
use: Used to justify mergers and capacity expansions.
ex: A factory with $2m fixed cost: at 100k units fixed cost per unit is $20; at 200k units it is $10.
say: “The merger gives us economies of scale in procurement — we expect 3% savings on raw materials.”
rel: Fixed cost; operating leverage; economies of scope; moat.
vs: Economies of scale (more of the same) vs economies of scope (cost advantages from producing related products together).
:::

:::term Market share | الحصة السوقية | 
def: A company’s sales as a percentage of total sales in a defined market.
ar: نسبة مبيعات الشركة من إجمالي مبيعات سوق محدَّد.
why: Higher share often brings pricing power and scale; the definition of the “market” determines the number.
use: Strategy decks and competition authorities both rely on market-share analysis.
ex: Industry sales $500m; company sales $75m → 15% share.
say: “We hold 15% of the GCC commercial kitchen market, up from 12% last year.”
rel: Total addressable market (TAM); market size; concentration; competitive position.
vs: Share by value (revenue) can differ from share by volume (units).
:::

:::term Overheads | المصاريف العامة غير المباشرة | 
def: Ongoing business costs not directly tied to producing a specific product or service: rent, administration, management salaries, insurance, utilities.
ar: التكاليف المستمرة التي لا ترتبط مباشرة بإنتاج منتج أو خدمة بعينها، مثل الإيجار والإدارة ورواتب المديرين والتأمين والمرافق.
why: Overheads must be covered by gross profit; allocating them correctly affects product profitability decisions.
use: Cost accountants allocate overheads to products or departments; managers try to keep overheads flat as revenue grows.
ex: A consultancy’s overheads of $1.2m must be covered before any project margin becomes profit.
say: “Overheads are growing faster than revenue — we need to look at headcount in support functions.”
rel: Indirect cost; SG&A; fixed cost; overhead absorption.
vs: Overheads (indirect) vs direct costs (traceable to a product, e.g., materials).
:::

:::term Benchmark | المؤشر المرجعي / المقارنة المعيارية | 
def: A standard or point of reference against which performance, prices or practices are compared.
ar: معيار أو نقطة مرجعية تُقارن بها النتائج أو الأسعار أو الممارسات.
why: Benchmarks turn numbers into judgements: a 6% margin is good or bad only relative to peers.
use: Benchmarking studies compare costs against peers; in markets, benchmarks are reference prices (e.g., Brent crude) or indices.
ex: Peer distributors earn 4–6% EBIT margins; ours is 3.1%, below benchmark.
say: “Our cost-to-serve is 20% above the industry benchmark.”
rel: KPI; peer group; index; best practice.
vs: An internal target is not a benchmark; benchmarks come from external comparison.
:::

:::term Due diligence | العناية الواجبة / الفحص النافي للجهالة | DD
def: A structured investigation of a company, asset, counterparty or transaction before committing, to verify facts and uncover risks.
ar: تحقيق منظّم في شركة أو أصل أو طرف مقابل أو صفقة قبل الالتزام بها، للتحقق من المعلومات وكشف المخاطر. يُسمّى في بعض الأنظمة الخليجية «الفحص النافي للجهالة».
why: Most of the value of a deal is protected or lost in due diligence: hidden debts, litigation, tax exposures, overstated revenue.
use: Types: financial, legal, tax, commercial, technical, environmental, IT, KYC due diligence on counterparties.
ex: DD reveals that 40% of a target’s revenue comes from a contract expiring in six months; price is renegotiated.
say: “The offer is non-binding and subject to satisfactory due diligence.”
rel: Data room; disclosure; representations and warranties; KYC.
vs: DD (investigation before) vs audit (periodic verification of financial statements).
:::

:::term Memorandum of understanding | مذكرة التفاهم | MoU
def: A document recording the intentions and main terms agreed between parties, usually expressed to be non-binding except for specific clauses (confidentiality, exclusivity, governing law).
ar: وثيقة تسجّل نوايا الأطراف والشروط الرئيسية المتفق عليها، وتكون عادةً غير ملزمة باستثناء بنود محددة كالسرية والحصرية والقانون الحاكم.
why: Useful to align parties before investing in full contracts, but whether it is binding depends on wording and local law.
use: Common in government deals, joint ventures and partnerships in the Middle East and Asia.
ex: Two companies sign an MoU to explore a logistics joint venture, binding only on confidentiality.
say: “Let’s sign an MoU so both boards can approve the next phase.”
rel: Letter of intent (LOI); heads of terms; term sheet; binding vs non-binding.
vs: MoU vs contract: an MoU usually expresses intention; courts may still find it binding if it contains clear terms and intention to be bound. Get legal advice.
:::

## Corporate terminology

:::term Shareholder | المساهم / الشريك | 
def: An owner of shares (equity) in a company, entitled to rights defined by law and the company’s constitution: voting, dividends, and a residual claim on assets after creditors.
ar: المساهم هو مالك أسهم أو حصص في شركة، وله حقوق يحددها القانون والنظام الأساسي، كحق التصويت والحصول على الأرباح الموزعة والحق المتبقي في الأصول بعد سداد الدائنين. في الشركات ذات المسؤولية المحدودة يُسمّى عادةً «الشريك».
why: Shareholders bear the first loss and capture the upside; their rights shape control and governance.
use: Majority shareholders control board appointments; minority shareholders rely on protections in shareholder agreements.
ex: A holds 60%, B holds 40%; B negotiates veto rights over new borrowing above $5m.
say: “Any change to the dividend policy needs a shareholder resolution.”
rel: Equity; share capital; shareholder agreement; minority protection; dividend.
vs: Shareholder (owner) vs director (manages/oversees) vs creditor (lender). One person can be all three.
:::

:::term Board of directors | مجلس الإدارة | 
def: The body elected by shareholders to oversee management, set strategy, approve major decisions and protect the company’s interests.
ar: الهيئة التي ينتخبها المساهمون للإشراف على الإدارة التنفيذية ووضع الاستراتيجية واعتماد القرارات الكبرى وحماية مصالح الشركة.
why: Major contracts, borrowing, acquisitions and dividends usually need board approval; banks ask for board resolutions.
use: Boards act through resolutions at meetings or in writing; committees (audit, risk, remuneration) handle specialised oversight.
ex: The bank requires a certified board resolution authorising the CEO and CFO to sign the facility agreement.
say: “The acquisition is subject to board approval at the meeting on the 14th.”
rel: Executive / non-executive director; chairman; board resolution; corporate governance.
vs: Board (oversight, strategy) vs management (day-to-day execution). Some jurisdictions use two-tier boards (supervisory + management).
:::

:::term Chief Executive Officer | الرئيس التنفيذي | CEO
def: The most senior executive responsible for running the company and implementing the board’s strategy.
ar: أعلى منصب تنفيذي في الشركة، وهو المسؤول عن إدارتها وتنفيذ الاستراتيجية التي يقرّها مجلس الإدارة.
why: Knowing the C-suite helps you identify decision-makers in sales, partnerships and lending.
use: Other C-level roles: CFO (finance), COO (operations), CTO (technology), CIO (information), CRO (risk or revenue), CCO (commercial or compliance), CHRO (people).
ex: A $3m software deal may need the CFO’s budget approval and the CTO’s technical sign-off.
say: “We need the CFO in the room — the CEO likes the proposal but finance owns the budget.”
rel: C-suite; managing director; general manager; delegation of authority.
vs: “Managing Director” is the CEO equivalent in UK usage, but in investment banks it is a senior rank, not the head of the firm.
:::

:::term Limited liability | المسؤولية المحدودة | 
def: The principle that shareholders’ losses are limited to the amount they invested in the company; creditors cannot normally claim shareholders’ personal assets.
ar: مبدأ يقضي بأن خسارة المساهم تقتصر على المبلغ الذي استثمره في الشركة، ولا يحق للدائنين عادةً المطالبة بأمواله الشخصية.
why: Limited liability makes it possible to take business risk without risking personal ruin. It is why lenders ask small-company owners for personal guarantees.
use: Exceptions exist: personal guarantees, fraud, “piercing the corporate veil”, director liability for wrongful trading — rules depend on jurisdiction.
ex: An LLC fails owing $2m; the owner who invested $200k loses that amount, unless he signed a personal guarantee.
say: “The bank won’t lend to the new SPV without a parent company guarantee, given limited liability.”
rel: LLC; corporation; personal guarantee; corporate veil.
vs: Limited liability company (entity type) vs limited partnership (general partner has unlimited liability).
:::

:::term Holding company | الشركة القابضة | 
def: A company whose main purpose is to own shares in other companies (subsidiaries) and often to control them.
ar: شركة غرضها الأساسي تملّك أسهم أو حصص في شركات أخرى (شركات تابعة) والسيطرة عليها في الغالب.
why: Group structures separate risks, hold assets in favourable jurisdictions and allow selling businesses separately. Lenders care about “structural subordination”: creditors of the holding company are paid after creditors of the operating subsidiaries.
use: Groups present consolidated financial statements; banks may require guarantees from subsidiaries.
ex: XYZ Holding owns 100% of a trading company, 70% of a logistics company and 30% of a joint venture.
say: “Debt sits at the holdco, but the cash is generated in the opcos — we need upstream guarantees.”
rel: Subsidiary; affiliate; parent company; consolidation; SPV; holdco/opco.
vs: Subsidiary (controlled) vs associate (significant influence, typically 20–50%) vs joint venture (joint control).
:::

:::term Special purpose vehicle | الشركة ذات الغرض الخاص | SPV
def: A legal entity created for a single, limited purpose — to hold an asset, ring-fence a project, or carry out a financing — isolating its risks from the sponsor.
ar: كيان قانوني يُنشأ لغرض واحد محدد، كحيازة أصل أو عزل مشروع أو تنفيذ عملية تمويل، بهدف فصل مخاطره عن مخاطر الجهة الراعية.
why: Project finance, securitisation, real-estate investment and many joint ventures use SPVs so lenders look only at the project’s cash flows.
use: Lenders take security over the SPV’s assets and shares; sponsors limit exposure to their equity contribution.
ex: A solar-farm SPV borrows $80m repaid only from electricity sales under a 25-year power purchase agreement.
say: “The vessel will be owned by a single-ship SPV, with the loan secured on a ship mortgage.”
rel: Project finance; non-recourse; securitisation; holding company.
vs: SPV (single purpose, ring-fenced) vs operating subsidiary (multiple activities).
:::

:::term Corporate governance | حوكمة الشركات | 
def: The system of rules, practices and processes by which a company is directed and controlled, balancing the interests of shareholders, management and other stakeholders.
ar: منظومة القواعد والممارسات والإجراءات التي تُوجَّه بها الشركة وتُراقَب، بما يحقق التوازن بين مصالح المساهمين والإدارة وأصحاب المصلحة الآخرين.
why: Good governance reduces fraud, improves decisions and lowers the cost of capital; investors and lenders assess it.
use: Elements: board independence, committees, internal audit, delegation of authority, disclosure, related-party controls. Frameworks include the G20/OECD Principles of Corporate Governance and national codes.
ex: A family company adds two independent directors and an audit committee before seeking private-equity investment.
say: “The investors’ main concern is governance — related-party transactions with the founder’s other companies.”
rel: Board; audit committee; internal control; related party; ESG.
vs: Governance (direction and control) vs management (execution) vs compliance (adherence to rules).
:::

:::term Articles of association | النظام الأساسي / عقد التأسيس | 
def: The company’s constitutional document setting rules for share classes, shareholder meetings, board powers, transfers of shares and other internal matters (names vary: bylaws, memorandum and articles, statutes).
ar: الوثيقة التأسيسية للشركة التي تحدد قواعد فئات الأسهم واجتماعات المساهمين وصلاحيات مجلس الإدارة ونقل ملكية الأسهم وغيرها من الأمور الداخلية. تختلف التسمية حسب البلد.
why: Banks and counterparties check the articles to confirm who can sign and what approvals are needed.
use: KYC files include certified copies of constitutional documents; M&A lawyers review transfer restrictions.
ex: The articles require a 75% shareholder vote to sell the main asset.
say: “Please send the latest certified articles and the commercial registration certificate for our KYC file.”
rel: Memorandum of association; bylaws; commercial registration; shareholder agreement.
vs: Articles (public constitutional document) vs shareholder agreement (private contract between shareholders).
:::

:::term Dividend | توزيعات الأرباح | 
def: A distribution of a company’s profits to its shareholders, usually in cash and in proportion to shareholdings.
ar: توزيع جزء من أرباح الشركة على المساهمين، نقداً في الغالب، بنسبة ما يملكه كل منهم.
why: Dividends return cash to owners but reduce cash available for growth or debt service; laws usually restrict dividends to distributable profits, and loan covenants may limit them.
use: Expressed per share or as a payout ratio; “interim” and “final” dividends.
ex: Net income $680k; payout ratio 30% → dividend $204k.
say: “The facility restricts dividends while leverage is above 2.5×.”
rel: Payout ratio; retained earnings; dividend yield; share buyback.
vs: Dividend (distribution of profit) vs return of capital vs salary/management fee to an owner-manager.
:::

:::term Share capital | رأس المال | 
def: The amount contributed by shareholders in exchange for shares, recorded in equity. Authorised capital is the maximum allowed; issued/paid-up capital is what has actually been issued and paid.
ar: المبلغ الذي ساهم به المساهمون مقابل الأسهم، ويُسجَّل ضمن حقوق الملكية. رأس المال المصرّح به هو الحد الأقصى المسموح، والمدفوع هو ما أُصدر وسُدّد فعلاً.
why: Some jurisdictions set minimum capital for certain activities; banks look at paid-up capital as a sign of shareholder commitment.
use: Increases in capital are made by rights issues, new investors or capitalising shareholder loans.
ex: Paid-up capital AED 1m; shareholders also provided AED 3m of shareholder loans.
say: “We could convert part of the shareholder loan into capital to strengthen the balance sheet before the bank review.”
rel: Equity; paid-up capital; retained earnings; shareholder loan.
vs: Share capital ≠ total equity (equity also includes retained earnings and reserves).
:::

تختلف تسمية الكيانات القانونية بين الدول العربية: «الشركة ذات المسؤولية المحدودة»، و«شركة المساهمة العامة/الخاصة»، و«شركة التضامن»، و«شركة التوصية البسيطة». كما تختلف الوثائق التأسيسية: «عقد التأسيس» و«النظام الأساسي». يجب دائماً الرجوع إلى قانون الشركات المحلي في كل بلد، لأن الحقوق والالتزامات تختلف من دولة إلى أخرى.

:::exercise Practice questions
1. A bank asks for “certified articles, a board resolution and UBO declaration”. Explain why it needs each.
2. Your manager says the target company has a “$12m run rate”. What two questions do you ask?
3. Classify: (a) a 35% stake with two board seats in a company; (b) a 100%-owned company created only to own one ship; (c) a 55% stake.
4. A shareholder of an LLC asks whether he is personally liable for the company’s $500k unpaid supplier invoices. What is the general answer and what exceptions should he check?
:::

:::answer Answers to the practice questions
1. Articles: confirm the company exists, its powers and who can bind it. Board resolution: proves the board authorised the transaction and named signatories. UBO declaration: KYC/AML requirement to identify natural persons who own or control the company.
2. (a) Which period is annualised (one month? one quarter?) and is it seasonal? (b) How does it compare with actual last-twelve-months revenue, and is any of it one-off?
3. (a) Likely an associate (significant influence) — or a JV if control is shared by agreement; (b) an SPV and a wholly owned subsidiary; (c) a subsidiary (control), subject to voting agreements.
4. Generally no — limited liability caps his loss at his investment. Exceptions: a personal guarantee he signed; fraud or misuse of the company; jurisdiction-specific director/manager liability rules (e.g., trading while insolvent). He should take local legal advice.
:::
