Part I builds the vocabulary used throughout the collection. It is organised by domain rather than alphabetically, because terms are easier to learn in clusters that share a logic: profit terms together, credit terms together, shipping terms together. The alphabetical index and the full English–Arabic glossary are in Volume 10, and every volume has its own index.

Each important term has a **term card**: English term, Arabic equivalent, acronym, definition, Arabic explanation, why it matters, how it is used in practice, an example, a sentence a professional might actually say or write, related terms, and the distinctions beginners most often miss. Shorter **quick-reference tables** follow each domain for additional terms that need only a definition.

The first chapter is deliberately different. It takes the twenty-one terms that cause the most confusion in meetings — revenue, profit, cash and return measures — and builds them from a single numerical example so that you can see exactly how they relate.

## Revenue, sales and turnover

:::term Revenue | الإيرادات | 
def: The income a business earns from its ordinary activities — selling goods, providing services, earning fees or interest — measured at the amount it expects to be entitled to, after deducting returns, discounts and rebates. Under IFRS 15 and ASC 606, revenue is recognised when control of goods or services passes to the customer, not when cash is received.
ar: الإيرادات هي ما تكسبه المنشأة من نشاطها المعتاد مثل بيع البضائع أو تقديم الخدمات، بعد خصم المرتجعات والخصومات والحسومات. وتُسجَّل الإيرادات عند انتقال السيطرة على البضاعة أو الخدمة إلى العميل، وليس عند استلام النقد. لذلك قد تكون الإيرادات مرتفعة بينما النقد في الصندوق منخفض.
why: Revenue is the top line: every margin is a percentage of it, many valuations use it (EV/Revenue), and growth is usually measured on it. But revenue is an accounting measure of entitlement, not cash.
use: Analysts track revenue growth, mix (by product, customer, region) and quality (recurring vs one-off). Bankers check revenue concentration: a borrower with 60% of revenue from one customer is riskier.
ex: Cedar Line invoiced $12.4m but net revenue is $12.0m after $0.4m of returns and rebates.
say: “Revenue grew 18% year on year, but most of the growth came from two hotel chains on 90-day terms, so cash conversion has weakened.”
rel: Sales; turnover; top line; gross revenue; net revenue; recurring revenue; deferred revenue; revenue recognition.
vs: Revenue ≠ cash received (customers may not have paid). Revenue ≠ profit (costs are not yet deducted). “Gross revenue” before deductions differs from “net revenue”.
:::

:::term Sales | المبيعات | 
def: The value of goods or services sold to customers in a period. In financial statements “sales” is normally a synonym for revenue from selling goods; in commercial conversation it can also mean the volume of units sold, the value of orders, or the sales department itself.
ar: المبيعات هي قيمة البضائع أو الخدمات المبيعة خلال فترة معيّنة. في القوائم المالية تُستخدم غالباً بمعنى الإيرادات من بيع البضائع، أما في الحديث التجاري فقد تعني عدد الوحدات المبيعة، أو قيمة الطلبات، أو قسم المبيعات نفسه.
why: Because “sales” is used loosely, you must check which meaning a speaker intends — booked orders, invoiced sales or recognised revenue can differ substantially.
use: A sales director may report “sales of $3m this month” meaning orders signed; finance will only recognise revenue when goods are delivered.
ex: Cedar Line’s sales team signed orders of $13.1m during the year; $12.4m was invoiced; $12.0m is net revenue.
say: “Can you confirm whether the $3m sales figure is bookings or invoiced revenue?”
rel: Bookings; orders; billings; revenue; sales volume; sales mix.
vs: Bookings (contracts signed) → billings (invoices issued) → revenue (recognised) → cash (collected). Each can be different in the same month.
:::

:::term Turnover | رقم الأعمال / حجم المبيعات | 
def: In British and Commonwealth usage, the total revenue of a business in a period. The same word also describes the rate at which something is replaced or used up: inventory turnover, asset turnover, staff turnover.
ar: في الاستخدام البريطاني يعني «رقم الأعمال» إجمالي إيرادات المنشأة خلال الفترة. وتُستخدم الكلمة أيضاً للدلالة على معدل الدوران، مثل معدل دوران المخزون أو معدل دوران الأصول أو معدل دوران الموظفين.
why: Thresholds in law and regulation (company size categories, VAT registration, audit exemption) are often expressed in “turnover” in jurisdictions influenced by UK drafting.
use: “Annual turnover of AED 50m” in a tender pre-qualification simply means revenue. “Inventory turnover of 4×” means the stock is sold and replaced four times a year.
ex: Cedar Line’s turnover is $12.0m; its inventory turnover is COGS ÷ average inventory = 8.4 ÷ 2.25 = 3.7×.
say: “The tender requires a minimum turnover of $10m over each of the last three years.”
rel: Revenue; inventory turnover; asset turnover; receivables turnover; staff turnover.
vs: Do not confuse “turnover” (revenue) with “staff turnover” (employee attrition) or with “turnover ratios” (efficiency measures).
:::

## Profit layers: gross profit to net income

The income statement is a staircase: each step deducts another category of cost. Each step answers a different question.

| Step | Question it answers | Who cares most |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit | Do we sell above the direct cost of what we sell? | Pricing, procurement, product managers |
| EBITDA | Does the core business generate operating earnings before investment and financing choices? | Bankers, private equity, valuation |
| EBIT / operating profit | After wear-and-tear of assets, is the business profitable? | Management, analysts |
| Profit before tax | After the cost of debt? | Lenders, tax |
| Net income | What is left for shareholders? | Shareholders, dividends, EPS |

:::term Gross profit | مجمل الربح | GP
def: Revenue minus cost of goods sold (or cost of sales). It measures what remains from sales after the direct cost of the products or services delivered, before overheads.
ar: مجمل الربح هو الإيرادات مطروحاً منها تكلفة البضاعة المبيعة (أو تكلفة المبيعات). يقيس ما يتبقّى من المبيعات بعد تغطية التكلفة المباشرة للمنتجات أو الخدمات، وقبل المصاريف العامة والإدارية.
why: Gross margin shows pricing power and procurement efficiency. A business with a thin gross margin has little room to absorb overheads, freight increases or discounts.
use: Traders and distributors manage obsessively to gross margin per deal; software companies report gross margins of 70–85% because the cost of serving one more customer is low.
ex: Cedar Line: 12.0m − 8.4m = $3.6m gross profit; gross margin = 3.6 ÷ 12.0 = 30%.
say: “If we accept the 5% discount, our gross margin on this account drops from 30% to about 26%.”
rel: Cost of goods sold; gross margin; contribution margin; markup.
vs: Gross profit uses COGS; contribution margin subtracts all variable costs (including variable selling costs such as commissions and delivery). They are close but not identical.
:::

:::term Operating profit | الربح التشغيلي | EBIT
def: Profit from the company’s core operations after deducting cost of sales and all operating expenses, including depreciation and amortisation, but before interest and tax. Often used interchangeably with EBIT (earnings before interest and taxes), although EBIT may include non-operating items such as gains on asset sales.
ar: الربح التشغيلي هو الربح الناتج عن النشاط الأساسي بعد خصم تكلفة المبيعات وجميع المصاريف التشغيلية بما فيها الإهلاك والإطفاء، وقبل الفوائد والضرائب. يُستخدم غالباً بمعنى الأرباح قبل الفوائد والضرائب (EBIT)، مع أن EBIT قد يشمل بنوداً غير تشغيلية كأرباح بيع الأصول.
why: Operating profit is independent of how the company is financed, so it allows comparison between companies with different debt levels. Interest cover = EBIT ÷ interest.
use: Management bonuses are often tied to operating profit; lenders compute interest cover from it.
ex: Cedar Line: EBITDA 1.5m − D&A 0.3m = EBIT 1.2m; operating margin 10%; interest cover = 1.2 ÷ 0.4 = 3.0×.
say: “Operating profit was flat because the gross-margin gain was offset by higher warehouse costs.”
rel: EBIT; EBITDA; operating margin; interest cover; operating leverage.
vs: Operating profit excludes interest; net income includes it. EBIT may include one-off non-operating gains — check the definition in each report.
:::

:::term EBITDA | الأرباح قبل الفوائد والضرائب والإهلاك والإطفاء | EBITDA
def: Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation. Operating profit with the non-cash charges for depreciation and amortisation added back. It is a proxy for operating cash earnings before investment, working capital changes, interest and tax.
ar: هي الأرباح قبل خصم الفوائد والضرائب والإهلاك والإطفاء. تمثّل الربح التشغيلي مضافاً إليه الإهلاك والإطفاء لأنهما مصروفان غير نقديين. تُعدّ مؤشراً تقريبياً للأرباح النقدية التشغيلية، لكنها لا تساوي التدفق النقدي لأنها تتجاهل الاستثمارات الرأسمالية وتغيّرات رأس المال العامل والضرائب.
why: EBITDA is the basis for leverage covenants (Net debt ÷ EBITDA), valuation multiples (EV ÷ EBITDA) and private-equity pricing. It is not defined by IFRS or US GAAP, so companies define it themselves.
use: “Adjusted EBITDA” adds back items management considers one-off. Lenders define EBITDA precisely in the facility agreement to prevent creative adjustments.
ex: Cedar Line: EBITDA $1.5m; net debt (assume cash 0.4m) = 4.2 − 0.4 = 3.8m; leverage = 3.8 ÷ 1.5 = 2.5×.
say: “The bank’s covenant is net debt to EBITDA below 3.0×, so we have headroom of about 0.5× turns.”
rel: Adjusted EBITDA; EBITDAR; EBIT; leverage ratio; EV/EBITDA; covenant.
vs: EBITDA ≠ cash flow. A company with rising receivables and inventory can have strong EBITDA and negative operating cash flow — exactly Cedar Line’s situation (see the reconciliation at the end of this chapter).
:::

:::term EBIT | الأرباح قبل الفوائد والضرائب | EBIT
def: Earnings before interest and taxes: net income with interest expense and income tax added back. Measures profitability from operations (and sometimes non-operating items) independent of capital structure and tax regime.
ar: الأرباح قبل الفوائد والضرائب، أي صافي الدخل مضافاً إليه مصروف الفائدة وضريبة الدخل. يقيس الربحية بغضّ النظر عن هيكل التمويل والنظام الضريبي.
why: Used in interest cover, return on capital employed and EV/EBIT valuation. Unlike EBITDA it charges depreciation, so it reflects capital intensity.
use: For asset-heavy businesses (shipping, manufacturing) analysts prefer EV/EBIT to EV/EBITDA because depreciation is a real economic cost of replacing assets.
ex: Cedar Line: 680k + 120k tax + 400k interest = $1.2m.
say: “On an EBIT basis the target trades at 11×, which isn’t cheap for an asset-heavy business.”
rel: Operating profit; EBITDA; ROCE; interest cover.
vs: EBIT charges depreciation; EBITDA does not. For a software firm the gap is small; for an airline it is huge.
:::

:::term Net income | صافي الدخل | NI
def: The profit remaining after all expenses, interest, taxes and minority interests have been deducted. Also called net profit, net earnings or the bottom line. It belongs to the shareholders and is the source of dividends and retained earnings.
ar: صافي الدخل هو الربح المتبقّي بعد خصم جميع المصاريف والفوائد والضرائب. يُسمّى أيضاً صافي الربح أو «السطر الأخير»، وهو حق للمساهمين ومصدر توزيعات الأرباح والأرباح المحتجزة.
why: Earnings per share (EPS), price-to-earnings ratios and return on equity all use net income. It is what accounting says the owners earned.
use: Equity investors focus on net income and EPS; lenders care more about EBITDA and cash flow.
ex: Cedar Line: $680,000; net margin 5.7%.
say: “Net income was hit by higher interest costs, even though operating profit improved.”
rel: Net profit; bottom line; EPS; retained earnings; dividend payout.
vs: Net income ≠ cash generated. It includes non-cash items (depreciation, provisions) and ignores working-capital investment and capex.
:::

## Cash flow, working capital, CAPEX and OPEX

:::term Cash flow | التدفق النقدي | 
def: The movement of cash into and out of a business in a period. The cash flow statement separates operating cash flow (from trading), investing cash flow (capex, acquisitions, asset sales) and financing cash flow (borrowing, repayments, equity, dividends).
ar: التدفق النقدي هو حركة النقد الداخل إلى المنشأة والخارج منها خلال فترة. تُقسَم قائمة التدفقات النقدية إلى: تدفقات من الأنشطة التشغيلية، ومن الأنشطة الاستثمارية (شراء الأصول وبيعها)، ومن الأنشطة التمويلية (الاقتراض والسداد وزيادة رأس المال والتوزيعات).
why: Companies fail when they run out of cash, not when they report losses. Salaries, suppliers, tax and interest are paid in cash.
use: Bankers build cash flow forecasts to test whether a borrower can service debt. Treasurers forecast weekly cash. Investors value companies on free cash flow.
ex: See the reconciliation at the end of this chapter: Cedar Line has net income of $680k but operating cash flow of about −$820k.
say: “We need more visibility on cash flow for the next 13 weeks before we commit to the new warehouse.”
rel: Operating cash flow; free cash flow; cash conversion; liquidity; burn rate.
vs: Cash flow ≠ profit. Timing (credit terms), non-cash items and investment spending separate them.
:::

:::term Working capital | رأس المال العامل | WC
def: Current assets minus current liabilities. In operational finance, “net working capital” or “trade working capital” usually means accounts receivable + inventory − accounts payable: the cash tied up in the operating cycle.
ar: رأس المال العامل هو الأصول المتداولة مطروحاً منها الالتزامات المتداولة. وفي الإدارة المالية التشغيلية يُقصد به غالباً: الذمم المدينة + المخزون − الذمم الدائنة، أي النقد المحتجز في الدورة التشغيلية.
why: Growing companies need more working capital: more stock and more customer credit. If this is not financed, the company runs out of cash while growing profitably.
use: Banks finance working capital through overdrafts, revolving facilities, receivables finance and trade finance. Treasurers aim to shorten the cash conversion cycle.
ex: Cedar Line trade working capital: start 1.5 + 1.8 − 1.1 = $2.2m; end 2.6 + 2.7 − 1.3 = $4.0m; increase $1.8m absorbed cash.
say: “The new contract needs about $1.5m of extra working capital because the client pays at 90 days.”
rel: Cash conversion cycle; DSO; DIO; DPO; liquidity; current ratio; working capital facility.
vs: Accountants’ working capital (all current items, including cash and short-term debt) differs from operational trade working capital. Always ask which definition is used.
:::

:::term Capital expenditure | النفقات الرأسمالية | CAPEX
def: Spending on long-lived assets — property, plant, equipment, vehicles, major software — that will benefit more than one year. Capex is recorded as an asset on the balance sheet and expensed gradually through depreciation or amortisation.
ar: النفقات الرأسمالية هي الإنفاق على أصول طويلة الأجل مثل العقارات والآلات والمعدّات والمركبات والبرمجيات الكبيرة التي تفيد المنشأة لأكثر من سنة. تُسجَّل كأصل في الميزانية ثم تُحمَّل على المصاريف تدريجياً عبر الإهلاك أو الإطفاء.
why: Capex consumes cash immediately but hits profit slowly; EBITDA ignores it entirely. Capital-intensive businesses need high EBITDA just to stand still.
use: Distinguish maintenance capex (replacing worn assets) from growth capex (expanding capacity). Lenders may restrict capex in covenants.
ex: Cedar Line spent $500k on forklifts and racking; only $100k depreciation of it hits this year’s profit (5-year life).
say: “We’re proposing $2m of growth capex for the second warehouse; maintenance capex stays at about $300k.”
rel: OPEX; depreciation; free cash flow; capital budgeting; fixed assets.
vs: Capex = asset now, expense later; OPEX = expense now. Leasing can convert capex-like spending into lease payments (though IFRS 16 puts most leases on balance sheet).
:::

:::term Operating expenditure | النفقات التشغيلية | OPEX
def: Ongoing costs of running the business that are expensed in the period they are incurred: salaries, rent, utilities, marketing, software subscriptions, maintenance, travel.
ar: النفقات التشغيلية هي التكاليف الجارية لتشغيل المنشأة، وتُحمَّل على المصاريف في الفترة نفسها، مثل الرواتب والإيجار والمرافق والتسويق واشتراكات البرمجيات والصيانة والسفر.
why: Opex determines operating leverage: high fixed opex means profit swings sharply with revenue.
use: Cloud computing shifted IT from capex (servers) to opex (subscriptions); budgets are usually split into opex and capex lines with separate approval.
ex: Cedar Line opex excluding D&A: $2.1m.
say: “Moving to SaaS turns this into opex, which is easier to approve than a capex request.”
rel: CAPEX; SG&A; fixed costs; overheads; operating leverage.
vs: Opex ≠ COGS. COGS is directly linked to goods sold; opex is the cost of running the organisation.
:::

## Receivables, payables, liquidity, solvency and leverage

:::term Accounts receivable | الذمم المدينة | AR
def: Amounts owed to the business by customers for goods or services delivered on credit and not yet paid. A current asset.
ar: الذمم المدينة هي المبالغ المستحقة للمنشأة على العملاء مقابل بضائع أو خدمات سُلِّمت بالآجل ولم تُدفع بعد، وهي من الأصول المتداولة.
why: Receivables are revenue that has not become cash. They carry credit risk (non-payment) and tie up working capital.
use: Credit controllers chase overdue invoices; banks lend against receivables (factoring, invoice discounting); analysts track days sales outstanding.
ex: Cedar Line receivables rose from $1.5m to $2.6m. DSO = 2.6 ÷ 12.0 × 365 ≈ 79 days.
say: “Receivables over 90 days are now 14% of the book; we need to tighten credit limits.”
rel: DSO; ageing report; bad debt; factoring; credit limit; trade credit insurance.
vs: Receivables (owed to us) vs payables (we owe). Note receivable = a formal written promise, unlike an ordinary trade receivable.
:::

:::term Accounts payable | الذمم الدائنة | AP
def: Amounts the business owes to suppliers for goods or services received on credit and not yet paid. A current liability.
ar: الذمم الدائنة هي المبالغ المستحقة على المنشأة للموردين مقابل بضائع أو خدمات استلمتها بالآجل ولم تدفع ثمنها بعد، وهي من الالتزامات المتداولة.
why: Supplier credit is interest-free financing. Extending payables improves cash flow but can damage supplier relationships or forfeit early-payment discounts.
use: Treasury balances payment timing; procurement negotiates payment terms; supply chain finance programmes let suppliers get paid early by a bank.
ex: Cedar Line payables of $1.3m on COGS of $8.4m: DPO ≈ 1.3 ÷ 8.4 × 365 ≈ 56 days.
say: “Can we move this supplier from 30 to 60 days without losing the 2% early-payment discount on other lines?”
rel: DPO; payment terms; supply chain finance; accruals.
vs: Accounts payable (invoiced) vs accrued expenses (owed but not yet invoiced).
:::

:::term Liquidity | السيولة | 
def: The ability to meet short-term obligations as they fall due, and the ease with which an asset can be converted into cash without significant loss of value.
ar: السيولة هي قدرة المنشأة على الوفاء بالتزاماتها قصيرة الأجل عند استحقاقها، وتعني أيضاً سهولة تحويل الأصل إلى نقد دون خسارة كبيرة في قيمته.
why: A company can be profitable and solvent but illiquid — unable to pay this month’s obligations — and fail.
use: Measured by current ratio, quick ratio, cash on hand, undrawn facilities and cash forecasts. Banks also manage their own liquidity under regulatory ratios (LCR, NSFR).
ex: Cedar Line has $0.4m cash and $0.6m undrawn on its overdraft: liquidity headroom $1.0m against monthly outflows of ~$0.9m — tight.
say: “Liquidity is tight until the Q3 collections come in; let’s draw on the revolver as a buffer.”
rel: Solvency; current ratio; quick ratio; cash runway; liquidity risk; market liquidity.
vs: Liquidity = short-term ability to pay; solvency = long-term ability (assets exceed liabilities, sustainable debt). “Market liquidity” means ease of trading an asset.
:::

:::term Solvency | الملاءة المالية | 
def: The ability of a business to meet its long-term obligations; broadly, having assets worth more than liabilities and a sustainable debt burden relative to earnings.
ar: الملاءة المالية هي قدرة المنشأة على الوفاء بالتزاماتها طويلة الأجل، أي أن تكون أصولها أكبر من التزاماتها وأن يكون عبء ديونها محتملاً مقارنةً بأرباحها.
why: Insolvency triggers legal consequences: directors’ duties shift towards creditors, and insolvency procedures may start. The tests differ by jurisdiction (cash-flow test vs balance-sheet test).
use: Measured by debt-to-equity, net debt/EBITDA, interest cover; for banks and insurers, by regulatory capital ratios.
ex: Cedar Line: equity $4.28m, debt $4.2m — solvent, with debt-to-equity ≈ 0.98.
say: “The group is solvent on a balance-sheet basis, but the 2027 bond maturity is a refinancing risk.”
rel: Liquidity; leverage; insolvency; capital adequacy; going concern.
vs: A firm can be solvent but illiquid (valuable assets, no cash today) or liquid but heading to insolvency (cash now, unsustainable losses).
:::

:::term Leverage | الرافعة المالية | 
def: The use of borrowed money (financial leverage) or fixed costs (operating leverage) to magnify returns. Financial leverage is measured by ratios such as debt/equity or net debt/EBITDA.
ar: الرافعة المالية هي استخدام الأموال المقترضة (أو التكاليف الثابتة في حالة الرافعة التشغيلية) لتضخيم العائد. تُقاس الرافعة المالية بنسب مثل الدين إلى حقوق الملكية أو صافي الدين إلى EBITDA.
why: Leverage increases return on equity when returns on assets exceed the cost of debt — and amplifies losses when they do not. It is the central variable in credit analysis and private equity.
use: Bankers say “the company is levered 3.5 times”, meaning net debt is 3.5× EBITDA. Traders talk about leverage in margin accounts.
ex: Cedar Line: net debt/EBITDA 2.5×; debt/equity 0.98×.
say: “Post-acquisition leverage would peak at 4.2× and fall below 3× within two years.”
rel: Gearing; debt-to-equity; net debt; operating leverage; covenant.
vs: Financial leverage (debt) vs operating leverage (fixed costs). In the UK “gearing” is used for financial leverage.
:::

## Margin, markup, ROI and ROE

:::term Margin | الهامش | 
def: A profit measure expressed as a percentage of revenue (selling price). Gross margin = gross profit ÷ revenue; operating margin = operating profit ÷ revenue; net margin = net income ÷ revenue.
ar: الهامش هو مقياس للربح يُعبَّر عنه كنسبة مئوية من الإيرادات أو سعر البيع. مثلاً: هامش مجمل الربح = مجمل الربح ÷ الإيرادات.
why: Margins allow comparison across sizes and periods and show how much of each dollar of revenue becomes profit.
use: Traders quote deals in margin per unit (e.g., $12/tonne); retailers in gross margin %; banks in net interest margin.
ex: Cedar Line: gross margin 30%; EBITDA margin 12.5%; operating margin 10%; net margin 5.7%.
say: “At a 4% net margin, a 2% freight increase wipes out half the profit.”
rel: Markup; contribution margin; net interest margin; spread.
vs: Margin divides profit by **selling price**; markup divides profit by **cost**. In trading, “margin” can also mean collateral posted to an exchange or broker.
:::

:::term Markup | نسبة الإضافة على التكلفة | 
def: The amount added to cost to arrive at a selling price, expressed as a percentage of cost.
ar: نسبة الإضافة على التكلفة هي المبلغ المضاف إلى التكلفة للوصول إلى سعر البيع، وتُحسب كنسبة مئوية من التكلفة لا من سعر البيع.
why: Many businesses price with cost-plus rules; confusing markup with margin leads to underpricing.
use: “Cost plus 25%” is a markup. Retailers often think in markup; finance reports margin.
ex: An item costs $80 and sells for $100: markup = 20 ÷ 80 = 25%; margin = 20 ÷ 100 = 20%.
say: “We need a 30% gross margin, which means a markup of about 42.9% on landed cost.”
rel: Margin; cost-plus pricing; landed cost.
vs: Markup = profit/cost; margin = profit/price. Margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup); markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin).
:::

:::term Return on investment | العائد على الاستثمار | ROI
def: The gain (or loss) from an investment relative to its cost: ROI = (gain from investment − cost of investment) ÷ cost of investment. A flexible, non-standardised measure.
ar: العائد على الاستثمار هو الربح أو الخسارة من استثمار ما منسوباً إلى تكلفته: (العائد − التكلفة) ÷ التكلفة. هو مقياس مرن غير موحّد التعريف، ولا يأخذ في الحسبان القيمة الزمنية للنقود.
why: Used to justify projects, marketing spend and technology purchases. Because it ignores timing and is defined differently by different people, serious capital decisions also use NPV and IRR.
use: “The CRM project has a three-year ROI of 140%.” Always ask over what period and on what assumptions.
ex: A $200k automation project saves $90k a year for 3 years: ROI = (270 − 200) ÷ 200 = 35% over 3 years (undiscounted).
say: “Before we approve this, can we see the ROI on a discounted basis and the payback period?”
rel: NPV; IRR; payback; ROE; ROCE; ROA.
vs: ROI ignores the time value of money and the period length; a 35% ROI over 3 years is far worse than 35% over 1 year.
:::

:::term Return on equity | العائد على حقوق الملكية | ROE
def: Net income divided by average shareholders’ equity. Measures the return generated on the capital owners have invested and retained.
ar: العائد على حقوق الملكية هو صافي الدخل مقسوماً على متوسط حقوق المساهمين، ويقيس العائد المتحقق على الأموال التي استثمرها المالكون واحتجزوها في المنشأة.
why: Shareholders compare ROE to their cost of equity. ROE can be raised by better margins, faster asset turnover or more leverage (DuPont analysis) — and the last one adds risk.
use: Bank analysts focus heavily on ROE versus cost of equity; investors use ROE to judge management quality.
ex: Cedar Line: 680 ÷ ((3,800 + 4,280) ÷ 2) = 680 ÷ 4,040 = 16.8%.
say: “ROE looks strong at 17%, but a third of that comes from leverage rather than operating performance.”
rel: ROA; ROCE; ROIC; DuPont analysis; cost of equity.
vs: ROE is affected by leverage; ROA and ROIC are not (or less). A company can raise ROE by borrowing while becoming riskier.
:::

## Why Cedar Line ran short of cash: reconciling profit to cash

Cedar Line earned $680,000 of net income, yet its debt rose by $1.2m. The bridge from profit to cash shows why.

| Line | USD | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Net income | 680,000 | |
| + Depreciation and amortisation | 300,000 | Non-cash expense added back |
| − Increase in receivables | (1,100,000) | Revenue booked but not yet collected |
| − Increase in inventory | (900,000) | Cash paid for stock not yet sold |
| + Increase in payables | 200,000 | Supplier credit used |
| **Operating cash flow** | **(820,000)** | Negative despite profit |
| − Capital expenditure | (500,000) | Investing activity |
| **Free cash flow** | **(1,320,000)** | |
| − Dividends | (200,000) | Financing activity |
| + New borrowing | 1,200,000 | Financing activity |
| **Change in cash** | **(320,000)** | Cash fell from $0.72m to $0.40m (assumed) |

:::tip What a banker sees here
A credit officer reading these numbers would say: “Profitable, but growth is being funded by debt because DSO has stretched and inventory is building. I want to see the receivables ageing, any concentration in the two hotel chains, and whether inventory includes slow-moving stock.” This is the most common pattern of distress in growing trading companies — Chapter 17 analyses it in depth.
:::

:::ar لماذا تنفد السيولة من شركة رابحة؟
حققت الشركة صافي ربح قدره 680 ألف دولار، لكن تدفقها النقدي التشغيلي كان سالباً بنحو 820 ألف دولار. السبب أن الذمم المدينة ارتفعت بمقدار 1.1 مليون دولار (مبيعات لم تُحصَّل بعد)، وارتفع المخزون بمقدار 900 ألف دولار (بضاعة دُفع ثمنها ولم تُبع بعد). الربح المحاسبي يُسجَّل عند البيع، أما النقد فيتأخر حتى يدفع العميل. لذلك تموّل الشركات النامية نموها بالديون، وإذا لم تُدَر الذمم والمخزون بعناية فقد تتعثر رغم ربحيتها.
:::

## Comparison table: the twenty-one core terms

| Term | Formula / basis | Cash or accounting? | Main users | Typical trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Value of goods/services transferred, net of returns | Accounting | Everyone | Treating as cash |
| Sales | Goods sold; sometimes orders | Either | Sales, finance | Bookings vs revenue |
| Turnover | UK: revenue; also rate of replacement | Accounting | Regulators, UK firms | Staff vs revenue meaning |
| Gross profit | Revenue − COGS | Accounting | Pricing, procurement | Excluding freight/duties from COGS |
| Operating profit | GP − opex − D&A | Accounting | Management | One-off items |
| EBITDA | EBIT + D&A | Accounting proxy | Lenders, PE | Treating as cash; aggressive “adjustments” |
| EBIT | NI + interest + tax | Accounting | Analysts | Non-operating items included |
| Net income | All revenues − all expenses | Accounting | Shareholders | Ignoring working capital |
| Cash flow | Cash in − cash out | Cash | Treasury, banks | Ignoring timing |
| Working capital | AR + inventory − AP (trade) | Balance-sheet | Treasury, banks | Different definitions |
| CAPEX | Spending on long-lived assets | Cash now, expense later | Management, lenders | Ignored by EBITDA |
| OPEX | Running costs | Expense now | Budget owners | Confusing with COGS |
| AR | Owed by customers | Balance-sheet | Credit control | Ageing ignored |
| AP | Owed to suppliers | Balance-sheet | Procurement, treasury | Over-stretching suppliers |
| Liquidity | Short-term ability to pay | Cash | Treasury | Confused with solvency |
| Solvency | Long-term ability to pay | Balance-sheet | Lenders, courts | Confused with liquidity |
| Leverage | Debt / EBITDA, debt / equity | Ratio | Lenders, PE | Ignoring leases, guarantees |
| Margin | Profit ÷ price | Ratio | Everyone | Confused with markup |
| Markup | Profit ÷ cost | Ratio | Pricing | Confused with margin |
| ROI | (Gain − cost) ÷ cost | Ratio | Project sponsors | No time period |
| ROE | NI ÷ avg equity | Ratio | Shareholders | Boosted by leverage |

:::exercise Chapter 1 practice
1. A distributor buys a product for $64 and sells it for $80. Compute the markup and the margin.
2. Company A has revenue of $50m, EBITDA of $10m, D&A of $4m, interest of $2m and tax of $1m. Compute EBIT, profit before tax, net income and the EBITDA margin.
3. During a year, a company’s net income is $2m, D&A $1m, receivables increase by $3m, inventory decreases by $0.5m and payables increase by $0.2m. What is operating cash flow? What would you tell the CEO?
4. In a meeting, the sales director says: “We did $4m of sales in March, so cash is fine.” List three questions you should ask.
5. Company B has ROE of 24% and Company C has ROE of 15%. Under what circumstances could C be the better-run business?
:::

:::answer Chapter 1 answers
1. Profit = $16. Markup = 16 ÷ 64 = **25%**. Margin = 16 ÷ 80 = **20%**. Check: margin = 0.25 ÷ 1.25 = 0.20.
2. EBIT = 10 − 4 = **$6m**; PBT = 6 − 2 = **$4m**; net income = 4 − 1 = **$3m**; EBITDA margin = 10 ÷ 50 = **20%**.
3. OCF = 2 + 1 − 3 + 0.5 + 0.2 = **$0.7m**. Message: profit converted to cash at only 35% (0.7 ÷ 2) because receivables grew by $3m. Ask why — new large customers on long terms? Overdue invoices? Review credit terms and collections before increasing sales further.
4. (a) Is $4m bookings, invoices or recognised revenue? (b) On what payment terms, and how much has been collected? (c) What did fulfilling those orders cost in inventory and supplier payments, and when are those payments due?
5. If B’s ROE comes mainly from high leverage (e.g., debt/equity of 4×) while C has little debt, C may have a higher return on assets or ROIC and much lower risk. Use DuPont analysis: ROE = net margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier.
:::

:::summary Key takeaways
- Revenue, profit and cash are three different measurements of the same business; professionals always reconcile them.
- Each profit layer answers a different question and serves a different audience.
- Working capital is where growing businesses lose cash; EBITDA hides it.
- Margin and markup are different; know the conversion formulas.
- Return measures must be read together with the risk (leverage) that produces them.
:::
