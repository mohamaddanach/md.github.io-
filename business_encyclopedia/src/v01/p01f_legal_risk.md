# Legal, Risk, Compliance and Insurance Terminology

:::obj In this chapter
Domain 11 (legal and contractual), Domain 15 (risk management and compliance) and Domain 16 (insurance). Legal concepts are explained in general terms; rules vary by jurisdiction and actual transactions need advice from a qualified local lawyer. Part XII and Part XV give full treatment.
:::

## Legal and contractual terms

:::term Representations and warranties | الإقرارات والضمانات | R&W
def: Statements of fact made by a party in a contract (representations) and contractual promises that certain facts are true (warranties). If untrue, the other party may have remedies — damages, termination or indemnity — depending on the contract and governing law.
ar: الإقرارات هي بيانات بوقائع يقدمها أحد الأطراف في العقد، والضمانات وعود تعاقدية بأن وقائع معينة صحيحة. إذا ثبت عدم صحتها قد يحق للطرف الآخر المطالبة بالتعويض أو الفسخ أو الرجوع بالتعويض الخاص بحسب العقد والقانون الحاكم.
why: In acquisitions, loans and supply contracts, R&W allocate the risk of unknown facts to the party best placed to know them.
use: Seller in an SPA warrants that accounts are accurate, there is no undisclosed litigation, taxes are paid. A disclosure letter qualifies warranties.
ex: Seller warrants “no customer representing more than 10% of revenue has given notice of termination”.
say: “We need a specific warranty on tax, plus an indemnity for the open audit years.”
rel: Indemnity; disclosure letter; due diligence; W&I insurance; SPA.
vs: The legal distinction between representations and warranties differs between common-law and civil-law systems; in practice they are often drafted together.
:::

:::term Indemnity | التعويض / الالتزام بالتعويض | 
def: A contractual promise by one party to compensate the other for specified losses, often on a pound-for-pound basis, regardless of whether a breach occurred.
ar: تعهد تعاقدي من أحد الأطراف بتعويض الطرف الآخر عن خسائر محددة، غالباً بمبلغ مساوٍ للخسارة، بغض النظر عن وقوع إخلال بالعقد.
why: Gives stronger, more predictable protection than a claim for breach of warranty; often capped and time-limited.
use: IP infringement indemnities in software contracts; tax indemnities in acquisitions.
ex: Supplier indemnifies buyer for any third-party claim that the product infringes a patent.
say: “The indemnity is uncapped — we need to limit it to the contract value.”
rel: Warranty; limitation of liability; hold harmless; insurance.
vs: Indemnity (compensation for specified loss) vs damages for breach (must prove breach and loss, subject to remoteness/mitigation rules).
:::

:::term Force majeure | القوة القاهرة | FM
def: A contract clause excusing or suspending performance when events beyond a party’s reasonable control (war, natural disaster, epidemic, government action) prevent performance. Its scope depends on drafting; some legal systems also have statutory doctrines.
ar: بند في العقد يعفي الطرف من الأداء أو يعلّقه عند وقوع أحداث خارجة عن سيطرته المعقولة، كالحروب والكوارث الطبيعية والأوبئة والقرارات الحكومية. يتحدد نطاقه بصياغة العقد، وتعرف بعض الأنظمة القانونية القوة القاهرة بنصوص تشريعية.
why: Determines who bears the cost of disruption; poorly drafted clauses cause disputes.
use: Key points: list of events, causation, notice periods, mitigation, duration after which either party can terminate.
ex: A port closure by government order prevents loading for 3 weeks; seller gives FM notice within 5 days as required.
say: “Rising prices are not force majeure — the contract only covers events that make performance impossible.”
rel: Hardship; frustration; impossibility; termination; notice.
vs: Force majeure (performance prevented) vs hardship (performance more onerous; some laws and ICC clauses allow renegotiation).
:::

:::term Limitation of liability | تحديد المسؤولية | LoL
def: A clause capping the amount and/or types of loss a party can be liable for (e.g., cap at contract value; exclusion of indirect and consequential loss, loss of profit).
ar: بند يحدد سقفاً لمقدار الخسارة التي يُسأل عنها الطرف و/أو أنواعها، مثل تحديدها بقيمة العقد أو استبعاد الخسائر غير المباشرة والتبعية والربح الفائت.
why: Makes risk insurable and proportionate to the contract’s value; enforceability is subject to local law (often not for fraud, gross negligence, death or personal injury).
use: Negotiated heavily in IT, services, construction and logistics contracts.
ex: Logistics provider’s liability capped at 2 SDR/kg or the invoice value, whichever is lower.
say: “We can accept a cap of 100% of annual fees, excluding data-protection breaches.”
rel: Indemnity; exclusion clause; consequential loss; insurance.
vs: Cap on amount vs exclusion of loss types; both often appear together.
:::

:::term Conditions precedent | الشروط المسبقة (الواقفة) | CPs
def: Conditions that must be satisfied (or waived) before an obligation becomes effective — e.g., regulatory approval, board approval, delivery of security documents before a loan can be drawn.
ar: شروط يجب تحقيقها (أو التنازل عنها) قبل أن يصبح الالتزام نافذاً، مثل الموافقة التنظيمية أو موافقة مجلس الإدارة أو تسليم مستندات الضمان قبل سحب القرض.
why: Protects parties from being bound before key requirements are met; the timetable to closing is driven by CPs.
use: Loan agreements include CP checklists; SPAs include merger-control clearance as a CP.
ex: Drawdown conditional on registration of the mortgage and a legal opinion.
say: “All CPs are satisfied except the competition clearance — closing is pending.”
rel: Conditions subsequent; closing; long-stop date; signing vs closing.
vs: Condition precedent (before effect) vs condition subsequent (after effect, failure may end the obligation) vs covenant (ongoing promise).
:::

:::term Governing law and jurisdiction | القانون الحاكم والاختصاص القضائي | 
def: Governing law is the legal system that interprets the contract; jurisdiction (or arbitration) clause determines which courts or tribunal decide disputes.
ar: القانون الحاكم هو النظام القانوني الذي يُفسَّر العقد وفقه، وبند الاختصاص (أو التحكيم) يحدد المحاكم أو هيئة التحكيم المختصة بالفصل في النزاعات.
why: The same clause can mean different things under different laws; enforcement of judgments and awards depends on treaties (e.g., the New York Convention for arbitral awards).
use: International contracts often choose English law with arbitration (e.g., ICC, LCIA, DIAC, SCCA) or a regional centre.
ex: “This Agreement is governed by English law. Disputes shall be finally resolved by arbitration under the ICC Rules, seat London.”
say: “We need an arbitration clause — a foreign court judgment may not be enforceable in the buyer’s country.”
rel: Arbitration; litigation; seat; enforcement; New York Convention.
vs: Governing law (substance) vs seat of arbitration (procedural home) vs venue (physical location of hearings).
:::

:::term Arbitration | التحكيم | 
def: A private dispute-resolution process in which parties agree to submit disputes to one or more arbitrators whose award is binding and generally enforceable internationally.
ar: وسيلة خاصة لتسوية النزاعات يتفق فيها الأطراف على عرض نزاعاتهم على محكّم أو أكثر، ويكون قرارهم (حكم التحكيم) ملزماً وقابلاً للتنفيذ دولياً في الغالب.
why: Neutral forum, confidentiality, expert arbitrators and cross-border enforceability; but costs can be high.
use: Institutional (ICC, LCIA, SIAC, DIAC, etc.) vs ad hoc (UNCITRAL rules).
ex: A $3m quality claim between a Turkish seller and an Iraqi buyer resolved by ICC arbitration.
say: “For a contract this size, ad hoc arbitration with a sole arbitrator is more cost-effective.”
rel: Mediation; litigation; New York Convention; award.
vs: Arbitration (binding) vs mediation (facilitated negotiation, non-binding unless settled).
:::

:::term Termination clause | بند إنهاء العقد | 
def: Provisions setting out when and how a party may end a contract — for convenience, for material breach (often after a cure period), insolvency, change of control or prolonged force majeure — and the consequences.
ar: أحكام تحدد متى وكيف يجوز لأحد الأطراف إنهاء العقد، سواء للملاءمة أو للإخلال الجوهري (بعد مهلة للتصحيح عادة) أو للإفلاس أو تغيّر السيطرة أو استمرار القوة القاهرة، والآثار المترتبة على الإنهاء.
why: Determines how locked-in each party is and what happens to orders in progress, stock, payments and exclusivity.
use: Distribution agreements: notice periods, buy-back of stock, post-termination non-compete (subject to local law).
ex: “Either party may terminate on 90 days’ written notice; on termination, the principal shall repurchase saleable stock at net cost.”
say: “The supplier can terminate for convenience with 30 days’ notice — that’s too short for our investment.”
rel: Material breach; cure period; change of control; survival clauses.
vs: Termination for convenience (no reason needed) vs for cause (breach).
:::

## Risk management and compliance terms

:::term Counterparty risk | مخاطر الطرف المقابل | 
def: The risk that the other party to a contract or transaction fails to perform: does not pay, deliver, or honour a derivative or guarantee.
ar: خطر عدم وفاء الطرف الآخر في العقد أو الصفقة بالتزاماته، كعدم الدفع أو عدم التسليم أو عدم الوفاء بعقد مشتق أو ضمان.
why: Every open-account sale, prepayment, forward contract and deposit creates counterparty exposure.
use: Mitigated by credit limits, L/Cs, guarantees, credit insurance, collateral, netting and diversification.
ex: Prepaying 50% to a new supplier creates counterparty risk of $250k; an advance-payment guarantee mitigates it.
say: “We need to mitigate counterparty risk — the buyer is new and in a high-risk jurisdiction.”
rel: Credit risk; performance risk; settlement risk; credit limit; collateral.
vs: Counterparty risk (specific counterparty failure) vs market risk (price movements).
:::

:::term Credit risk | مخاطر الائتمان | 
def: The risk of loss because a borrower or customer fails to meet payment obligations. Measured by probability of default (PD), loss given default (LGD) and exposure at default (EAD): expected loss = PD × LGD × EAD.
ar: خطر الخسارة الناتجة عن عجز المقترض أو العميل عن الوفاء بالتزامات الدفع. يُقاس باحتمال التعثر والخسارة عند التعثر والتعرض عند التعثر: الخسارة المتوقعة = PD × LGD × EAD.
why: The core risk of banks and of any company selling on credit.
use: Credit scoring, ratings, limits, provisioning, pricing.
ex: EAD $1m, PD 2%, LGD 45% → expected loss $9,000.
say: “The expected loss on this exposure is low, but the concentration is too high.”
rel: Counterparty risk; default; rating; ECL; collateral.
vs: Expected loss (priced and provisioned) vs unexpected loss (covered by capital).
:::

:::term Operational risk | المخاطر التشغيلية | 
def: The risk of loss from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems, or from external events (Basel definition, which includes legal risk but excludes strategic and reputational risk).
ar: خطر الخسارة الناتجة عن قصور أو فشل الإجراءات الداخلية أو الأفراد أو الأنظمة، أو عن أحداث خارجية. يشمل وفق تعريف بازل المخاطر القانونية، ولا يشمل المخاطر الاستراتيجية ومخاطر السمعة.
why: Fraud, errors, cyber attacks and system outages cause some of the largest losses in business.
use: Controls: segregation of duties, maker-checker, reconciliations, access controls, business continuity.
ex: A payment clerk changes a supplier’s bank details after a fake email — $180k lost.
say: “This is an operational-risk issue: no callback verification on bank-detail changes.”
rel: Internal control; fraud; cyber risk; business continuity; RCSA.
vs: Operational risk (process failure) vs credit risk (counterparty) vs market risk (prices).
:::

:::term Compliance | الامتثال | 
def: Ensuring that an organisation follows applicable laws, regulations, standards and internal policies, and managing the risk of non-compliance.
ar: ضمان التزام المنشأة بالقوانين واللوائح والمعايير والسياسات الداخلية المطبقة، وإدارة مخاطر عدم الامتثال.
why: Non-compliance leads to fines, licence loss, criminal liability and reputational damage.
use: Functions: AML, sanctions, anti-bribery, data protection, competition, market conduct.
ex: A trading company introduces sanctions screening of vessels and counterparties after a near-miss.
say: “Compliance has to clear the counterparty before we sign.”
rel: AML; sanctions; three lines model; regulatory risk; code of conduct.
vs: Compliance (rules) vs risk management (all risks) vs internal audit (independent assurance).
:::

:::term Anti-money laundering | مكافحة غسل الأموال | AML
def: Laws, regulations and procedures to prevent criminals from disguising illegally obtained funds as legitimate income. Money laundering stages: placement, layering, integration.
ar: القوانين واللوائح والإجراءات التي تهدف إلى منع المجرمين من إخفاء الأموال غير المشروعة وإظهارها كدخل مشروع. مراحل غسل الأموال: الإيداع، والتمويه (التغطية)، والدمج.
why: Banks, and increasingly non-bank businesses (dealers in precious metals, real estate, company service providers), have AML obligations.
use: KYC, transaction monitoring, suspicious activity/transaction reporting (SAR/STR) to the financial intelligence unit.
ex: Repeated invoices for goods priced far above market, paid by unrelated third parties, are a trade-based laundering red flag.
say: “The transaction pattern triggered an AML alert; we need the underlying contracts.”
rel: KYC; CFT; FATF; STR; TBML; sanctions.
vs: AML (criminal proceeds) vs sanctions (prohibited parties/countries, regardless of crime) vs anti-bribery.
:::

:::term Sanctions | العقوبات | 
def: Restrictive measures imposed by governments or international bodies (e.g., the UN Security Council, the US through OFAC, the EU, the UK) against countries, entities, individuals, vessels or sectors: asset freezes, trade bans, financial restrictions.
ar: تدابير تقييدية تفرضها الحكومات أو الهيئات الدولية (كمجلس الأمن والولايات المتحدة عبر مكتب مراقبة الأصول الأجنبية والاتحاد الأوروبي والمملكة المتحدة) على دول أو كيانات أو أفراد أو سفن أو قطاعات، مثل تجميد الأصول وحظر التجارة والقيود المالية.
why: Violations carry severe penalties; banks reject payments involving sanctioned parties, and USD payments may bring US jurisdiction.
use: Screening names, vessels, ports, goods and ownership (including entities 50%+ owned by listed persons under some regimes).
ex: A bank refuses an L/C because the nominated vessel has called at a sanctioned port.
say: “Before we quote, compliance needs to screen the end user and the vessel.”
rel: Export controls; OFAC SDN list; secondary sanctions; screening; dual-use.
vs: Sanctions (legal prohibitions) vs a bank’s risk appetite (may refuse business that is legal but high-risk — “de-risking”).
:::

:::term Risk register | سجل المخاطر | 
def: A document listing identified risks with their causes, likelihood, impact, rating, owner, controls and actions.
ar: وثيقة تسرد المخاطر المحددة وأسبابها واحتمالية حدوثها وأثرها وتقييمها والمسؤول عنها والضوابط والإجراءات المتخذة.
why: Makes risks visible and assigns accountability.
use: Updated regularly; reviewed by management and risk committees.
ex: “Key supplier insolvency — likelihood medium, impact high — owner: Head of Procurement — action: qualify second source by Q2.”
say: “Add the FX exposure to the risk register with treasury as owner.”
rel: ERM; heat map; risk appetite; control; KRI.
vs: Risk register (inventory of risks) vs issue log (problems that have already happened).
:::

## Insurance terms

:::term Premium | قسط التأمين | 
def: The price paid by the insured to the insurer for coverage over a period.
ar: المبلغ الذي يدفعه المؤمَّن له لشركة التأمين مقابل التغطية خلال فترة محددة.
why: A cost of transferring risk; pricing reflects probability and severity of loss, deductibles and the insured’s risk management.
use: Expressed as an amount or a rate (e.g., 0.15% of insured value for marine cargo).
ex: Cargo insured value $110k (CIF + 10%) × 0.15% = $165 premium.
say: “Raising the deductible to $25k would cut the premium by about 18%.”
rel: Underwriting; rate; deductible; sum insured.
vs: Premium (price) vs sum insured (maximum covered value).
:::

:::term Deductible | التحمّل / المبلغ المقتطع | 
def: The portion of each loss borne by the insured before the insurer pays (called “excess” in UK usage).
ar: الجزء من كل خسارة يتحمله المؤمَّن له قبل أن تدفع شركة التأمين.
why: Eliminates small claims and aligns incentives; higher deductibles lower premiums.
use: Per claim, per event or annual aggregate.
ex: Loss $40k, deductible $5k → insurer pays $35k.
say: “We self-insure the first $50k of each loss through the deductible.”
rel: Excess; self-insured retention; premium; coverage limit.
vs: Deductible (insured’s share of each loss) vs limit (maximum insurer pays).
:::

:::term Exclusion | الاستثناء | 
def: A provision in an insurance policy that removes specified perils, property or circumstances from coverage.
ar: بند في وثيقة التأمين يستبعد مخاطر أو ممتلكات أو ظروفاً معينة من التغطية.
why: Many disputed claims turn on exclusions; reading them is essential before relying on a policy.
use: Common exclusions: wilful misconduct, inherent vice, insufficient packing, delay, war and strikes (unless bought back), sanctions, cyber.
ex: Cargo damaged because of poor packing is excluded under standard Institute Cargo Clauses.
say: “Check whether the policy excludes losses from delay — our main risk is spoilage.”
rel: Coverage; endorsement; peril; warranty (insurance).
vs: Exclusion (removed from cover) vs condition (insured must comply for cover to respond).
:::

:::term Underwriting | الاكتتاب التأميني | 
def: The process by which an insurer (or a bank, for securities and loans) assesses a risk, decides whether to accept it, and sets terms and price.
ar: العملية التي تقيّم فيها شركة التأمين (أو البنك في حالة الأوراق المالية والقروض) المخاطر وتقرر قبولها وتحدد شروطها وسعرها.
why: Good underwriting is the core skill of insurers and lenders.
use: Underwriters review proposal forms, loss history, surveys, financials.
ex: A warehouse with sprinklers and a clean 5-year loss record gets a lower rate.
say: “The underwriter wants a risk survey before quoting the property cover.”
rel: Premium; risk assessment; proposal form; loss ratio.
vs: Insurance underwriting vs securities underwriting (bank commits to buy unsold securities in an issue).
:::

:::term Reinsurance | إعادة التأمين | 
def: Insurance purchased by insurers to transfer part of their risk to reinsurers.
ar: تأمين تشتريه شركات التأمين لنقل جزء من مخاطرها إلى شركات إعادة التأمين.
why: Allows insurers to write large risks and protects them from catastrophes.
use: Treaty (portfolio) vs facultative (individual risk); proportional vs non-proportional.
ex: A local insurer keeps 10% of a $200m refinery risk and reinsures 90%.
say: “Capacity in the local market depends on reinsurers’ appetite for this risk.”
rel: Retention; cession; treaty; facultative; fronting.
vs: Reinsurance (insurer to reinsurer) vs co-insurance (several insurers share directly).
:::

:::term Subrogation | الحلول (حق الرجوع) | 
def: The insurer’s right, after paying a claim, to step into the insured’s position and recover the loss from a responsible third party.
ar: حق شركة التأمين، بعد دفع التعويض، في أن تحل محل المؤمَّن له في الرجوع على الطرف المسؤول عن الخسارة.
why: Insured must not prejudice recovery rights (e.g., by signing waivers with carriers).
use: Cargo insurers recover from carriers within limits of carrier liability.
ex: Insurer pays $80k for damaged cargo and claims against the shipping line.
say: “Notify the carrier in writing within the time limit to protect the insurer’s subrogation rights.”
rel: Claim; carrier liability; waiver of subrogation.
vs: Subrogation (insurer recovers) vs contribution (sharing between insurers).
:::

:::exercise Practice: Chapter 9
1. A supplier proposes: “Supplier’s total liability shall not exceed the fees paid in the previous 3 months.” Your annual contract is $1.2m. Why might you object, and what would you propose?
2. Identify the risk type: (a) customer goes bankrupt owing $300k; (b) euro strengthens before an EUR payment; (c) employee pays a fake invoice; (d) a new law bans your product.
3. A cargo worth $200k CIF is insured at 110% with a $2k deductible. Damage of $30k occurs. What does the insurer pay? If it were insured at only $150k, what might happen?
4. What is the difference between force majeure and hardship?
:::

:::answer Answers: Chapter 9
1. Three months’ fees ≈ $300k may be far below the losses a failure could cause (e.g., data breach, business disruption). Propose a cap of 12 months’ fees (or a fixed amount) with carve-outs for data protection, confidentiality, IP indemnities, fraud and wilful misconduct, and check the supplier’s insurance.
2. (a) Credit/counterparty risk; (b) market (FX) risk; (c) operational risk (fraud/process); (d) regulatory/legal (and strategic) risk.
3. Insurer pays 30,000 − 2,000 = **$28,000**. If underinsured (sum insured below value), many policies apply average: payment reduced in proportion (sum insured ÷ actual value), e.g., 150 ÷ 220 × loss — check the policy wording.
4. Force majeure: an event makes performance impossible or prevented; performance is suspended/excused. Hardship: performance remains possible but has become excessively onerous; parties may renegotiate (if the contract or law provides).
:::
