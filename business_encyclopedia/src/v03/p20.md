This part is a working reference for the calculations that business professionals do every day — in quotations, credit decisions, shipment costing, loan negotiations, budget reviews and investment memos. Each calculation card follows the same seven elements: the formula, a plain-English explanation, an Arabic explanation, a simple example, a realistic business example, a worked solution, and common mistakes.

Use it as a workbook: cover the solution, compute it yourself (on paper, in a spreadsheet, or in Python), then check. Speed and accuracy in these calculations are part of professional credibility — in a negotiation, the person who can compute the effect of a 2% discount on margin in ten seconds controls the conversation.

# Commercial Arithmetic: Percentages, Margins, Discounts, Commissions and Pricing

## Percentages and percentage points

:::calc Percentage change
f: % change = (New − Old) ÷ Old × 100
en: How much something grew or shrank relative to where it started. A change in a rate is expressed in percentage points (pp); the relative change of that rate is a percentage.
ar: نسبة التغير = (القيمة الجديدة − القيمة القديمة) ÷ القيمة القديمة. أما التغير في نسبة مئوية (مثل الهامش من 20% إلى 22%) فيُعبَّر عنه بالنقاط المئوية (2 نقطة مئوية)، وهو يعادل زيادة نسبية قدرها 10%.
simple: Price rises from $80 to $100: (100 − 80) ÷ 80 = 25%. Falling back from $100 to $80 is −20%, not −25%.
biz: Gross margin improved from 20% to 22% while revenue grew from $40m to $46m. Describe both changes correctly.
sol: Revenue growth = (46 − 40) ÷ 40 = **15%**. Margin change = **+2 percentage points**, which is a **10% relative improvement** (2 ÷ 20). Gross profit grew from $8.0m to $10.12m = **+26.5%**.
err: Saying “margin rose 2%” when it rose 2 percentage points; assuming a 20% fall followed by a 20% rise returns to the start (0.8 × 1.2 = 0.96); averaging percentages of different bases.
:::

:::calc Reverse percentage (tax-inclusive amounts)
f: Net amount = Gross amount ÷ (1 + rate)    Tax = Gross − Net = Gross × rate ÷ (1 + rate)
en: When a price includes tax (VAT, GST), you cannot take the tax rate of the gross amount to find the tax; divide by one plus the rate.
ar: إذا كان المبلغ شاملاً للضريبة فإن صافي المبلغ = المبلغ الإجمالي ÷ (1 + نسبة الضريبة). الخطأ الشائع هو حساب نسبة الضريبة من المبلغ الإجمالي مباشرة.
simple: $115 including 15% VAT → net $100, VAT $15 (not 15% × 115 = $17.25).
biz: A UAE supplier invoice shows AED 52,500 including 5% VAT. How much is input VAT, and what is the cost if VAT is recoverable?
sol: Net = 52,500 ÷ 1.05 = **AED 50,000**; VAT = **AED 2,500**. If the buyer is VAT-registered and can recover input VAT, the cost is AED 50,000; the AED 2,500 is a temporary cash outflow recovered through the VAT return.
err: Computing VAT as 5% × 52,500 = 2,625; treating recoverable VAT as a cost in pricing; forgetting that VAT is a cash-flow timing item for registered businesses.
:::

## Margins, markups and pricing

:::calc Gross margin and markup
f: Margin = (Price − Cost) ÷ Price    Markup = (Price − Cost) ÷ Cost
Margin = Markup ÷ (1 + Markup)    Markup = Margin ÷ (1 − Margin)
en: Margin is profit as a share of the selling price; markup is profit as a share of cost. The same deal always has a higher markup than margin.
ar: الهامش = الربح ÷ سعر البيع، ونسبة الإضافة على التكلفة = الربح ÷ التكلفة. للصفقة نفسها تكون نسبة الإضافة دائماً أعلى من الهامش. مثلاً: إضافة 25% على التكلفة تعطي هامشاً قدره 20%.
simple: Cost $75, price $100: margin 25%, markup 33.3%.
biz: A distributor’s policy is a 35% gross margin. The sales team uses a 35% markup on landed cost. Product landed cost is $64. What price does each method give, and what margin does the sales team actually achieve?
sol: Correct price = 64 ÷ (1 − 0.35) = **$98.46**. Sales team price = 64 × 1.35 = **$86.40** → margin = 22.40 ÷ 86.40 = **25.9%**, nearly 9 percentage points below policy.
err: Using markup and margin interchangeably; computing price as cost × (1 + target margin); calculating margin on cost excluding freight and duties.
:::

:::calc Price from a target margin
f: Price = Cost ÷ (1 − Target margin)
en: To achieve a margin m on the selling price, divide cost by (1 − m).
ar: للوصول إلى هامش مستهدف على سعر البيع، يُقسم سعر التكلفة على (1 − الهامش المستهدف).
simple: Cost $60, target margin 40% → price = 60 ÷ 0.6 = $100.
biz: A trader’s landed cost is $11.72 per unit. Commission to an agent is 3% of the selling price and the target net margin after commission is 25%. Find the price.
sol: Price must cover cost plus 28% of price (25% margin + 3% commission): P = 11.72 ÷ (1 − 0.28) = **$16.28**. Check: commission 0.49; margin 16.28 − 11.72 − 0.49 = 4.07 = 25.0% of price.
err: Adding percentages of price to cost (11.72 × 1.28 = 15.00 → only 18.9% margin after commission).
:::

## Discounts and commissions

:::calc Chain (successive) discounts
f: Net price = List × (1 − d₁) × (1 − d₂) × … × (1 − dₙ)    Effective discount = 1 − Π(1 − dᵢ)
en: Discounts applied one after another do not add up; each applies to the already reduced price.
ar: الخصومات المتتالية لا تُجمع، لأن كل خصم يُطبق على السعر بعد الخصم السابق. خصم 20% ثم 10% يساوي خصماً فعلياً قدره 28% وليس 30%.
simple: 20% then 10%: 0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72 → effective discount 28%.
biz: List price $2,500; trade discount 25%, quantity discount 5%, prompt-payment discount 2%. Net price and effective discount?
sol: 2,500 × 0.75 × 0.95 × 0.98 = **$1,745.63**; effective discount = 1 − 0.69825 = **30.2%**.
err: Adding discounts (25 + 5 + 2 = 32%); forgetting that prompt-payment discounts depend on payment timing and may not be taken.
:::

:::calc Commission (flat, tiered, margin-based)
f: Flat: Commission = Rate × Base    Tiered: Σ (rate of tier × amount within tier)    Margin-based: Rate × Gross profit
en: Commission rewards salespeople or agents; the base (revenue or margin) and the tier structure shape their behaviour.
ar: العمولة مكافأة للبائع أو الوكيل، وتُحسب على أساس المبيعات أو هامش الربح، وقد تكون بشرائح متدرجة. حساب العمولة على المبيعات قد يدفع البائع إلى منح خصومات كبيرة، أما حسابها على الهامش فيربط مصلحته بربحية الشركة.
simple: 4% of $50,000 sales = $2,000.
biz: Agent earns 3% on sales up to $1m and 5% above, per year. Sales $1.6m. Alternatively the company proposes 18% of gross profit; gross margin on these sales is 24%. Compare.
sol: Tiered: 3% × 1,000,000 + 5% × 600,000 = 30,000 + 30,000 = **$60,000**. Margin-based: 18% × (24% × 1.6m) = 18% × 384,000 = **$69,120**. The margin-based plan pays more at this margin but would pay less if the agent sold at heavy discounts — aligning incentives.
err: Applying the higher tier rate to all sales; paying commission on invoiced but uncollected sales without clawback; ignoring that commissions on discounted deals reduce margin twice.
:::

:::calc Blended (weighted average) margin and mix effect
f: Blended margin = Σ (share of revenueᵢ × marginᵢ)
en: The overall margin is a weighted average; a shift in sales mix changes it even if each product’s margin is unchanged.
ar: الهامش الإجمالي متوسط مرجّح لهوامش المنتجات بحسب حصة كل منها من الإيرادات. تغيّر مزيج المبيعات يغيّر الهامش الإجمالي حتى لو بقيت هوامش المنتجات ثابتة.
simple: A: 60% of sales at 40%; B: 40% at 20% → 0.6 × 40 + 0.4 × 20 = 32%.
biz: Next year the mix moves to 45% A / 55% B with unchanged product margins. Revenue rises 10% from $10m. What happens to gross profit?
sol: New blended margin = 0.45 × 40 + 0.55 × 20 = **29%**. Gross profit = 11m × 29% = **$3.19m** vs $3.20m this year — revenue +10%, gross profit flat. This is a **mix effect**.
err: Averaging margins without weights; attributing margin decline to “pricing” when it is mix.
:::

:::exercise Practice questions
1. Cost $42, target margin 30%. Price? What markup is that?
2. List price $800; discounts 15% and 10%; then 5% VAT on the net. Total invoice?
3. A price was cut 10% and volume rose 12%. Revenue change?
4. Margin fell from 18.0% to 16.5%. Express the change in pp and in relative terms.
:::

:::answer Answers to the practice questions
1. 42 ÷ 0.70 = **$60.00**; markup = 18 ÷ 42 = **42.9%**.
2. 800 × 0.85 × 0.90 = 612.00; VAT 30.60 → **$642.60**.
3. 0.90 × 1.12 = 1.008 → **+0.8%**.
4. **−1.5 pp**; relative change = −1.5 ÷ 18 = **−8.3%**.
:::

# Interest, Loans and Foreign Exchange Calculations

## Interest

:::calc Simple interest with day count
f: Interest = Principal × Annual rate × (Days ÷ Day basis)
en: Short-term loans, trade finance, deposits and money-market instruments usually pay simple interest for the period, calculated on actual days over a 360- or 365-day year depending on currency and contract.
ar: الفائدة البسيطة = أصل المبلغ × سعر الفائدة السنوي × (عدد الأيام ÷ أساس السنة). يُستخدم أساس 360 يوماً للدولار واليورو في الغالب، و365 يوماً للجنيه الإسترليني وبعض العملات المحلية؛ يجب الرجوع إلى العقد.
simple: $10,000 at 6% for 90 days (act/360) = 10,000 × 0.06 × 90 ÷ 360 = $150.
biz: A bank provides a $2m import loan for 120 days at 3M SOFR 4.30% + 2.95% margin (act/360). Interest due at maturity?
sol: Rate 7.25%. Interest = 2,000,000 × 0.0725 × 120 ÷ 360 = **$48,333.33**. (On act/365 it would be $47,671.23.)
err: Using 365 for USD loans documented on act/360; forgetting fees (arrangement, L/C commission) in the all-in cost.
:::

:::calc Compound growth and CAGR
f: FV = PV × (1 + r)ⁿ    CAGR = (End value ÷ Start value)^(1/n) − 1
en: Compound growth applies a rate to an amount that already includes previous growth. CAGR is the constant annual rate that turns the start value into the end value.
ar: النمو المركب يطبّق المعدل على مبلغ يشمل النمو السابق. ومعدل النمو السنوي المركب (CAGR) هو المعدل الثابت الذي يحوّل القيمة الأولى إلى القيمة الأخيرة خلال عدد السنوات.
simple: $1,000 at 10% for 3 years = 1,000 × 1.331 = $1,331.
biz: Revenue grew from $12.0m to $19.3m over 4 years. What is the CAGR? What would revenue be in 2 more years at the same rate?
sol: CAGR = (19.3 ÷ 12.0)^(1/4) − 1 = 1.6083^0.25 − 1 = **12.6%**. In 2 years: 19.3 × 1.126² = **$24.5m**.
err: Dividing total growth by years (60.8% ÷ 4 = 15.2% overstates); counting the number of data points instead of periods.
:::

:::calc Flat rate vs effective rate
f: Flat interest = Principal × Flat rate × Years (charged on the original principal). Effective rate: solve Principal = Payment × [1 − (1 + r)⁻ⁿ] ÷ r for r.
en: Flat-rate quotes charge interest on the full original principal even though it is repaid gradually, so the true (effective) rate is much higher.
ar: الفائدة الثابتة (Flat) تُحتسب على أصل القرض الأصلي طوال المدة رغم سداده تدريجياً، لذلك يكون المعدل الفعلي أعلى بكثير — قرابة الضعف تقريباً في القروض المقسطة.
simple: $10,000 for 1 year at 10% flat repaid monthly: interest $1,000; effective APR ≈ 18%.
biz: A vehicle loan of $30,000 for 3 years at 8% flat, monthly instalments. Compute the instalment and approximate effective rate.
sol: Interest = 30,000 × 8% × 3 = $7,200 → instalment = 37,200 ÷ 36 = **$1,033.33**. Solving for r: ≈ 1.21% per month → nominal APR ≈ **14.6%**, EAR ≈ **15.6%** — almost double the quoted 8%.
err: Comparing a flat-rate quote with a reducing-balance rate as if they were equivalent.
:::

## Loans

:::calc Loan payment and amortisation schedule
f: PMT = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)⁻ⁿ]; each period: Interest = Opening balance × r; Principal = PMT − Interest; Closing = Opening − Principal
en: An annuity loan has equal payments; early payments are mostly interest, later ones mostly principal.
ar: في القرض ذي الأقساط المتساوية تكون الدفعات الأولى معظمها فوائد والأخيرة معظمها سداد لأصل الدين. يُحسب القسط بالمعادلة أعلاه، ثم يُعد جدول السداد فترةً بعد فترة.
simple: $10,000 over 2 years, annual payments, 10%: PMT = 10,000 × 0.1 ÷ (1 − 1.1⁻²) = $5,761.90.
biz: Equipment loan $240,000, 2 years, quarterly payments, 8% p.a. (2% per quarter). Compute the payment and the first three lines of the schedule. Compare with equal-principal repayment.
sol: PMT = 240,000 × 0.02 ÷ (1 − 1.02⁻⁸) = 4,800 ÷ 0.146510 = **$32,762.36**.

| Quarter | Opening | Interest | Principal | Closing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 240,000.00 | 4,800.00 | 27,962.36 | 212,037.64 |
| 2 | 212,037.64 | 4,240.75 | 28,521.61 | 183,516.03 |
| 3 | 183,516.03 | 3,670.32 | 29,092.04 | 154,423.99 |

Equal principal: $30,000 principal per quarter + interest (Q1 payment $34,800, falling to $30,600 in Q8); total interest lower ($21,600 vs $22,098.88) because principal is repaid faster early on.
err: Treating the whole instalment as an expense (only interest is); using the annual rate with quarterly periods; forgetting fees and balloon payments.
:::

## Foreign exchange

:::calc Currency conversion and bid/ask
f: Quote “BASE/QUOTE = rate” means 1 BASE = rate × QUOTE. Bank buys base at the bid, sells base at the ask (offer). Quote amount = Base amount × rate; Base amount = Quote amount ÷ rate.
en: Always identify the base currency. You buy the base currency at the higher (ask) price and sell it at the lower (bid) price; the difference is the bank’s spread.
ar: في التسعيرة EUR/USD = 1.0850، العملة الأساسية هي اليورو، أي أن اليورو الواحد يساوي 1.0850 دولار. يشتري البنك العملة الأساسية بسعر الشراء (Bid) ويبيعها بسعر البيع (Ask) الأعلى، والفرق هو هامش البنك.
simple: USD/JPY 150.00: ¥1,500,000 = $10,000.
biz: EUR/USD bid 1.0848, ask 1.0852. An importer must pay EUR 250,000; an exporter receives EUR 250,000. What USD amounts apply?
sol: Importer buys EUR (base) at the ask: 250,000 × 1.0852 = **$271,300**. Exporter sells EUR at the bid: 250,000 × 1.0848 = **$271,200**. The $100 difference on each side is the spread (corporate spreads on smaller amounts are often wider).
err: Inverting the quote (dividing instead of multiplying); using the mid-rate for budgeting without the spread; ignoring bank charges on transfers.
:::

:::calc Cross rates
f: A/C = A/B × B/C;  if both quoted against USD: (A/USD) = (A/USD), (USD/C) → A/C = A/USD × USD/C; for two USD-base quotes: C₁/C₂ = (USD/C₂) ÷ (USD/C₁)
en: When two currencies are not directly quoted, derive their rate through a common currency (usually USD), making sure the units cancel.
ar: عندما لا تتوافر تسعيرة مباشرة بين عملتين، نحسب السعر عبر عملة وسيطة (غالباً الدولار) مع التأكد من أن الوحدات تُلغي بعضها بشكل صحيح.
simple: EUR/USD 1.0850, USD/AED 3.6725 → EUR/AED = 1.0850 × 3.6725 = 3.9847.
biz: A Saudi importer receives a quotation of CNY 480,000. USD/CNY = 7.20 and USD/SAR = 3.75. Cost in SAR?
sol: SAR/CNY = 7.20 ÷ 3.75 = 1.92 CNY per SAR → SAR cost = 480,000 ÷ 1.92 = **SAR 250,000** (equivalently 480,000 ÷ 7.20 = $66,666.67 × 3.75).
err: Multiplying when you should divide; ignoring that each leg has its own bid/ask spread.
:::

:::calc Forward rate from interest differentials
f: Forward = Spot × (1 + i_quote × d/basis) ÷ (1 + i_base × d/basis);  Forward points = Forward − Spot
en: A forward rate is not a forecast: it is the spot rate adjusted for the interest-rate difference between the two currencies over the period. The higher-interest currency trades at a forward discount.
ar: سعر الصرف الآجل ليس توقعاً لسعر المستقبل، بل هو السعر الفوري معدلاً بفارق أسعار الفائدة بين العملتين خلال المدة. العملة ذات الفائدة الأعلى تُتداول بخصم في السوق الآجلة.
simple: Spot 1.0000; quote-currency rate 5%, base 3%, 1 year → forward ≈ 1.0194.
biz: EUR/USD spot 1.0850; USD 3-month rate 4.40%; EUR 2.40%; 90 days, act/360 for both. Compute the forward and the points.
sol: Forward = 1.0850 × (1 + 0.044 × 0.25) ÷ (1 + 0.024 × 0.25) = 1.0850 × 1.011 ÷ 1.006 = **1.0904**; forward points ≈ **+54 pips** (EUR at a forward premium).
err: Treating forward points as the bank’s fee; reading the forward as the expected future spot; using the wrong currency as base.
:::

:::exercise Practice questions
1. Interest on GBP 1,500,000 for 61 days at 6.2% (act/365)?
2. Monthly payment on $85,000, 4 years, 9% p.a.?
3. USD/EGP 49.20 and EUR/USD 1.0700: EUR/EGP?
4. An exporter will receive USD 1m in 6 months; USD rate 4.5%, AED rate 4.4% (both act/360); USD/AED spot 3.6725. Compute the 6-month forward.
:::

:::answer Answers to the practice questions
1. 1,500,000 × 0.062 × 61 ÷ 365 = **GBP 15,542.47**.
2. r = 0.0075, n = 48: (1.0075)⁴⁸ = 1.4314 → PMT = 85,000 × 0.0075 ÷ (1 − 1/1.4314) = 637.50 ÷ 0.30138 = **≈ $2,115**.
3. EUR/EGP = 1.0700 × 49.20 = **52.64**.
4. Forward = 3.6725 × (1 + 0.044 × 0.5) ÷ (1 + 0.045 × 0.5) = 3.6725 × 1.022 ÷ 1.0225 = **3.6707** (USD at a slight discount; in practice the pegged forward market has its own liquidity and spreads).
:::

# Profitability, Working Capital and Financial Ratio Calculations

:::calc Contribution margin and multi-product break-even
f: CM per unit = Price − Variable cost; CM ratio = CM ÷ Price; Break-even (multi-product) = Fixed costs ÷ Weighted CM per mix unit
en: Contribution is what each sale contributes to fixed costs and profit. With several products, break-even depends on the sales mix.
ar: هامش المساهمة هو ما تساهم به كل وحدة مبيعة في تغطية التكاليف الثابتة وتحقيق الربح. وعند تعدد المنتجات تعتمد نقطة التعادل على مزيج المبيعات.
simple: Price 50, variable 30 → CM 20, CM ratio 40%; fixed costs 100,000 → break-even 5,000 units.
biz: Product A: price 100, CM 40. Product B: price 50, CM 10. Sales mix 1 A : 3 B (in units). Fixed costs $350,000. Break-even units of each?
sol: Weighted CM per mix (1A + 3B) = 40 + 30 = 70 → mixes = 350,000 ÷ 70 = 5,000 → **5,000 A and 15,000 B**; break-even revenue = 5,000 × 100 + 15,000 × 50 = **$1.25m**.
err: Using simple average CM (25) instead of the mix-weighted one; including fixed costs allocated per unit in “variable cost”.
:::

:::calc Liquidity ratios
f: Current ratio = Current assets ÷ Current liabilities; Quick ratio = (Cash + Receivables + Short-term investments) ÷ Current liabilities; Working capital = Current assets − Current liabilities
en: Measures of short-term ability to pay. A high ratio built on slow-moving stock or doubtful receivables is not true liquidity.
ar: نسب السيولة تقيس قدرة المنشأة على سداد التزاماتها قصيرة الأجل. لكن نسبة مرتفعة قائمة على مخزون راكد أو ذمم مشكوك في تحصيلها لا تعني سيولة حقيقية.
simple: CA 600, CL 400 → current ratio 1.5×; working capital 200.
biz: Cash 0.3m; receivables 2.5m; inventory 3.2m; prepayments 0.2m; payables 2.4m; short-term debt 1.6m; current portion of term loan 0.5m. Compute current and quick ratios and comment.
sol: CA = 6.2m; CL = 4.5m → current ratio **1.38×**; quick = (0.3 + 2.5) ÷ 4.5 = **0.62×**. Liquidity depends heavily on converting inventory; a lender will examine inventory ageing and the 13-week cash forecast.
err: Including long-term items; ignoring the current portion of long-term debt; treating all inventory as easily saleable.
:::

:::calc DSO, DIO, DPO and cash conversion cycle
f: DSO = Receivables ÷ Revenue × 365; DIO = Inventory ÷ COGS × 365; DPO = Payables ÷ COGS (or purchases) × 365; CCC = DIO + DSO − DPO; Cash released = Δdays × daily revenue (or daily COGS)
en: These convert balance-sheet amounts into days of activity, showing how long cash is tied up in the operating cycle.
ar: هذه المؤشرات تحوّل أرصدة الميزانية إلى عدد أيام من النشاط: أيام تحصيل الذمم المدينة، وأيام بقاء المخزون، وأيام سداد الموردين. ودورة التحويل النقدي = أيام المخزون + أيام التحصيل − أيام السداد.
simple: Receivables 100, revenue 1,200 → DSO = 30.4 days.
biz: Revenue $48m; COGS $36m; receivables $9.2m; inventory $7.4m; payables $4.9m. Compute the days and CCC; what cash is released if DSO falls to 55 days?
sol: DSO = 9.2 ÷ 48 × 365 = **70.0**; DIO = 7.4 ÷ 36 × 365 = **75.0**; DPO = 4.9 ÷ 36 × 365 = **49.7**; CCC = **95.3 days**. DSO to 55: 15 days × 48m ÷ 365 = **$1.97m** released.
err: Using year-end balances when the business is seasonal (use averages); mixing revenue and COGS denominators; comparing DSO across firms with different VAT treatment of receivables.
:::

:::calc Leverage and coverage ratios
f: Debt/Equity = Total debt ÷ Equity; Net debt/EBITDA = (Debt − Cash) ÷ EBITDA; Interest cover = EBIT (or EBITDA) ÷ Interest; DSCR = (EBITDA − Tax paid − Maintenance capex ± ΔWC) ÷ (Interest + Scheduled principal)
en: Measures of the debt burden and the ability to service it from earnings and cash flow. Facility agreements define each precisely.
ar: نسب الرافعة والتغطية تقيس عبء الدين والقدرة على خدمته من الأرباح والتدفقات النقدية. وتحدد اتفاقيات التسهيلات تعريف كل نسبة بدقة.
simple: Debt 60, cash 10, EBITDA 25 → net debt/EBITDA 2.0×.
biz: EBITDA $18m; EBIT $12m; interest $3m; tax paid $2m; maintenance capex $2.5m; working-capital increase $1.5m; scheduled principal $6m; debt $54m; cash $6m; equity $40m. Compute all four ratios.
sol: D/E = **1.35×**; net debt/EBITDA = 48 ÷ 18 = **2.67×**; interest cover (EBIT) = **4.0×** (EBITDA basis 6.0×); DSCR = (18 − 2 − 2.5 − 1.5) ÷ (3 + 6) = 12 ÷ 9 = **1.33×**.
err: Using a different definition from the one in the loan agreement; forgetting leases (post-IFRS 16) or guarantees; using EBITDA instead of cash available for DSCR.
:::

:::calc Return ratios
f: ROA = Net income ÷ Avg total assets; ROE = Net income ÷ Avg equity; ROCE = EBIT ÷ (Total assets − Current liabilities); ROIC = EBIT × (1 − t) ÷ (Net debt + Equity)
en: Return ratios relate profit to the resources used. ROIC compared with WACC shows whether the business creates value.
ar: نسب العائد تربط الربح بالموارد المستخدمة لتحقيقه. ومقارنة العائد على رأس المال المستثمر (ROIC) بتكلفة رأس المال (WACC) تبيّن ما إذا كانت الشركة تخلق قيمة.
simple: NI 10, average equity 50 → ROE 20%.
biz: EBIT $12m; tax 20%; net debt $48m; equity $40m; WACC 9.5%. Does the company create value?
sol: NOPAT = 9.6m; invested capital 88m → ROIC = **10.9%** > WACC 9.5% → creates value, with a spread of 1.4 percentage points (~$1.2m of economic profit a year).
err: Using closing rather than average balances when they change significantly; comparing ROE across firms with very different leverage.
:::

:::exercise Practice questions
1. CM ratio 35%, fixed costs $840,000. Break-even revenue? Revenue for $210,000 profit?
2. Revenue $73m, receivables $14.6m. DSO? If revenue grows 20% at the same DSO, how much more cash is tied up in receivables?
3. EBITDA $10m, debt $38m, cash $3m, covenant 3.25×. How much can EBITDA fall before a breach (debt unchanged)?
:::

:::answer Answers to the practice questions
1. BE = 840,000 ÷ 0.35 = **$2.4m**; with profit: 1,050,000 ÷ 0.35 = **$3.0m**.
2. DSO = 14.6 ÷ 73 × 365 = **73 days**. Receivables at +20% revenue: 14.6 × 1.2 = 17.52 → **+$2.92m**.
3. Net debt 35 → EBITDA at breach = 35 ÷ 3.25 = 10.77 > 10 → **already in breach** (3.5×). EBITDA must rise to $10.77m, or net debt fall to $32.5m.
:::

# Trade Costs, Investment Returns, Valuation, Probability and Forecasting

## Landed cost and freight

:::calc Landed cost per unit
f: Landed cost = Purchase price + Freight + Insurance + Customs duty + Non-recoverable taxes + Port/terminal and clearance charges + Inland transport + Bank/financing costs + Other (inspection, certification) — per unit = total ÷ units
en: The full cost to get goods into your warehouse ready for sale. Duty is usually calculated on the customs value (often CIF), and import VAT on the duty-paid value.
ar: التكلفة الواصلة هي التكلفة الكاملة لوصول البضاعة إلى المستودع جاهزة للبيع: سعر الشراء + الشحن + التأمين + الرسوم الجمركية + الضرائب غير المستردة + رسوم الميناء والتخليص + النقل الداخلي + تكاليف التمويل والمصارف. تُحسب الرسوم الجمركية عادةً على القيمة الجمركية (غالباً CIF).
simple: Goods $10,000 FOB + freight $1,200 + insurance $50 = CIF $11,250; duty 5% = $562.50; local charges $400 → landed $12,212.50.
biz: 2,000 units at $18.00 FOB Ningbo; sea freight $2,800 per 40ft container (one container); insurance 0.12% of 110% of CIF (approximate on CIF of FOB+freight); duty 5% of CIF; port and clearance $650; inland trucking $300; L/C and bank charges $420. Landed cost per unit?
sol: FOB = 36,000; freight 2,800 → C&F 38,800; insurance = 0.0012 × 1.1 × 38,800 ≈ **$51.22**; CIF ≈ 38,851.22; duty 5% = **1,942.56**; other = 650 + 300 + 420 = 1,370. Landed total ≈ **$42,163.78** → **$21.08 per unit** (17.1% above FOB).
err: Pricing on FOB; forgetting that duty applies to CIF, not FOB, in many countries; omitting bank, demurrage and inspection costs; treating recoverable VAT as a cost.
:::

:::calc Chargeable weight and freight allocation
f: Air: volumetric weight (kg) = L × W × H (cm) ÷ 6,000; chargeable weight = max(actual, volumetric). Sea LCL: revenue ton (W/M) = max(tonnes, m³). Allocation of shared costs: by value, weight, volume or units — choose the driver that best reflects consumption.
en: Carriers charge for the space cargo occupies as well as its weight. When one shipment carries several products, freight must be allocated to compute product margins.
ar: يحسب الناقل الجوي الوزن الحجمي (الطول × العرض × الارتفاع بالسنتيمتر ÷ 6000) ويُحاسَب على الأكبر بين الوزن الفعلي والحجمي. وفي الشحن البحري الجزئي يُحاسَب على الأكبر بين الأطنان والأمتار المكعبة. وعند شحن منتجات عدة معاً تُوزَّع التكلفة حسب القيمة أو الوزن أو الحجم.
simple: A 60 × 50 × 40 cm carton of 18 kg: volumetric = 120,000 ÷ 6,000 = 20 kg → chargeable 20 kg.
biz: (a) Air shipment of 10 such cartons at $4.50/kg. (b) LCL shipment: 3.2 tonnes, 7.5 m³, rate $65 per W/M. (c) A 40ft container freight of $3,000 carries products X ($60,000, 20 m³), Y ($20,000, 30 m³) and Z ($20,000, 16 m³). Allocate by value and by volume.
sol: (a) 10 × 20 = 200 kg chargeable × 4.50 = **$900**. (b) 7.5 W/M × 65 = **$487.50**. (c) By value: X **$1,800**, Y **$600**, Z **$600**. By volume (66 m³): X **$909**, Y **$1,364**, Z **$727**. Bulky low-value Y is much less profitable on a volume basis — usually the more accurate driver for sea freight, while insurance follows value.
err: Using actual weight for light, bulky air cargo; allocating all freight by value (overstates margins of bulky items); forgetting minimum charges.
:::

## Returns and valuation

:::calc NPV and IRR (quick reference)
f: NPV = Σ CFₜ ÷ (1 + r)ᵗ; IRR = r where NPV = 0
en: NPV measures value created at a required return; IRR is the break-even return. Full treatment: {{ch:Capital Budgeting}}.
ar: صافي القيمة الحالية يقيس القيمة المضافة عند معدل العائد المطلوب، ومعدل العائد الداخلي هو المعدل الذي تصبح عنده صافي القيمة الحالية صفراً.
simple: −100 today, +60 in years 1 and 2 at 10%: NPV = −100 + 54.55 + 49.59 = +4.13; IRR ≈ 13.1%.
biz: A forklift costing $45,000 saves $14,000 a year (after tax) for 4 years; residual $5,000 in year 4; required return 10%.
sol: PV of savings = 14,000 × 3.16987 = 44,378; PV residual = 5,000 × 0.68301 = 3,415 → NPV = 44,378 + 3,415 − 45,000 = **+$2,793**. IRR ≈ **12.7%**.
err: Discounting the time-0 flow; ignoring residual value or tax; using Excel’s NPV on the whole range.
:::

:::calc Holding-period return, annualised return and MOIC
f: HPR = (End value − Start value + Income) ÷ Start value; Annualised = (1 + HPR)^(12/months) − 1; MOIC = Total value returned ÷ Capital invested
en: Total return includes income (dividends, coupons); annualising allows comparison across holding periods. MOIC ignores time; IRR does not.
ar: العائد خلال فترة الاحتفاظ = (القيمة النهائية − القيمة الأولية + الدخل) ÷ القيمة الأولية. ولمقارنة استثمارات بآجال مختلفة نحوّل العائد إلى معدل سنوي. ومضاعف رأس المال (MOIC) لا يأخذ الزمن في الحسبان بخلاف معدل العائد الداخلي.
simple: Buy at 100, sell at 110 after 1 year, dividend 3 → 13%.
biz: Shares bought at $40, a $1.60 dividend received, sold at $46 after 18 months. Compute HPR and annualised return. Separately: a private investment returns 2.0× in 3 years vs 2.5× in 6 years — compare.
sol: HPR = (46 − 40 + 1.6) ÷ 40 = **19.0%**; annualised = 1.19^(12/18) − 1 = **12.3%**. 2.0× in 3 years → IRR = 2^(1/3) − 1 = **26.0%**; 2.5× in 6 years → 2.5^(1/6) − 1 = **16.5%**. Higher MOIC, lower IRR.
err: Ignoring income or costs; comparing returns over different periods without annualising; confusing MOIC with IRR.
:::

:::calc Valuation from multiples
f: Implied EV = Multiple × Metric (EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales, EV/EBIT); Implied equity = EV − Net debt (and other adjustments); Implied equity (P/E) = P/E × Net income
en: Multiples apply the market’s pricing of comparable companies to the target’s metrics. EV multiples pair with pre-interest metrics; equity multiples with post-interest metrics.
ar: التقييم بالمضاعفات يطبّق تسعير السوق لشركات مماثلة على مؤشرات الشركة المستهدفة. مضاعفات قيمة المنشأة تُطبّق على مؤشرات قبل الفوائد، ومضاعفات حقوق الملكية على مؤشرات بعد الفوائد.
simple: EBITDA 10 × 7 = EV 70; net debt 20 → equity 50.
biz: Target: EBITDA $14m; net income $6m; net debt $35m; 5m shares. Peers: EV/EBITDA 7.5×, P/E 12×. Implied value per share by each method?
sol: EV/EBITDA: EV = 105m → equity 70m → **$14.00/share**. P/E: 12 × 6 = 72m → **$14.40/share**. Consistent within 3%.
err: Applying EV multiples to net income; using forward multiples on trailing metrics; forgetting dilution from options.
:::

## Probability, sensitivity and forecasting

:::calc Expected value
f: E[X] = Σ pᵢ × xᵢ (probabilities sum to 1). Expected loss on credit = PD × LGD × EAD.
en: The probability-weighted average outcome. Use it to compare risky choices, but also look at the downside: an option with good expected value may still be unacceptable if its worst case is ruinous.
ar: القيمة المتوقعة هي متوسط النتائج المرجّح باحتمالاتها. تُستخدم للمقارنة بين الخيارات الخطرة، لكن يجب أيضاً النظر إلى أسوأ الاحتمالات، فقد يكون خيار ذو قيمة متوقعة جيدة غير مقبول إذا كانت خسارته المحتملة مدمّرة.
simple: 60% chance of +100, 40% chance of −50 → 60 − 20 = +40.
biz: Selling $100,000 on 60-day open account earns a $12,000 margin. Probability of default 4%; loss given default 60% of the receivable. Credit insurance costs 0.5% of the invoice. Expected value with and without insurance (ignore deductibles)?
sol: Without insurance: paid (96%) → +12,000; default (4%) → 12,000 − 60,000 = −48,000. EV = 11,520 − 1,920 = **+$9,600**. With insurance (90% indemnity assumed on the loss): default outcome = 12,000 − 500 − 6,000 = 5,500; paid = 11,500. EV = 0.96 × 11,500 + 0.04 × 5,500 = **+$11,260**. Insurance increases expected value here and caps the downside.
err: Using point estimates as certainties; ignoring correlation (many customers defaulting together in a downturn); forgetting the cost of the risk-reduction tool.
:::

:::calc Sensitivity of profit to a driver
f: Δ Profit = Δ Driver × Sensitivity; Profit elasticity = %Δ Profit ÷ %Δ Driver
en: Measures how much the result moves when one input changes. High sensitivities identify where to focus negotiation, hedging and monitoring.
ar: تحليل الحساسية يقيس مقدار تغير النتيجة عند تغير أحد المدخلات. الحساسيات العالية تحدد أين يجب التركيز في التفاوض والتحوط والمتابعة.
simple: If 1 cent on the fuel price per litre changes costs by $5,000, a 20-cent rise costs $100,000.
biz: A distributor: revenue $40m; COGS 75% (60% of it priced in EUR); other costs $7m; profit $3m. Sensitivity of profit to a 5% EUR appreciation (no price pass-through)?
sol: EUR-linked COGS = 0.75 × 40 × 0.60 = $18m → +5% = **+$0.9m cost** → profit falls to $2.1m (**−30%**). Profit elasticity to EUR = −30% ÷ 5% = **−6**.
err: Changing several inputs at once and calling it sensitivity; ignoring pass-through and hedges already in place.
:::

:::calc Simple forecasting: moving average, trend and error
f: n-period moving average = (Sum of last n values) ÷ n; Linear trend: y = a + b × t, with b = Σ(t − t̄)(y − ȳ) ÷ Σ(t − t̄)² and a = ȳ − b × t̄; MAPE = average of |Actual − Forecast| ÷ Actual
en: Basic statistical forecasts provide a baseline that judgement and drivers then adjust. Measure forecast accuracy to know how much to trust it.
ar: التنبؤات الإحصائية البسيطة كالمتوسط المتحرك وخط الاتجاه توفر خط أساس يُعدَّل لاحقاً بالحكم المهني ومعرفة المحركات. ويُقاس متوسط الخطأ المطلق النسبي (MAPE) لمعرفة مدى موثوقية التنبؤ.
simple: Last three months: 100, 110, 120 → 3-month moving average forecast = 110 (lags a rising trend).
biz: Monthly sales (units): 120, 132, 128, 140, 150, 146. Forecast month 7 by 3-month moving average and by linear trend. If actual month 7 is 158, compute each forecast’s percentage error.
sol: MA = (140 + 150 + 146) ÷ 3 = **145.3**. Trend: t̄ = 3.5, ȳ = 136; Σ(t − t̄)(y − ȳ) = 98; Σ(t − t̄)² = 17.5 → b = **5.6**; a = 136 − 5.6 × 3.5 = 116.4 → forecast = 116.4 + 5.6 × 7 = **155.6**. Errors: MA |158 − 145.3| ÷ 158 = **8.0%**; trend **1.5%**.
err: Using moving averages for trending or seasonal data without adjustment; extrapolating trends far ahead; never measuring forecast accuracy.
:::

1. FOB value $50,000; freight $3,500; insurance 0.15% on 110% of (FOB + freight); duty 10% on CIF; clearance $800. Landed cost?
2. An investment of $200,000 returns $260,000 after 2.5 years with no interim income. Annualised return?
3. Three outcomes for a tender: win at full margin $400k (30%), win with reduced scope $150k (20%), lose −$60k bid costs (50%). Expected value?
4. Peers trade at 1.8× EV/Sales; target sales $25m; net debt $12m. Implied equity value?
:::

:::answer Answers to the practice questions
1. C&F 53,500; insurance = 0.0015 × 1.1 × 53,500 = 88.28; CIF = 53,588.28; duty = 5,358.83; landed = 53,588.28 + 5,358.83 + 800 = **$59,747.11**.
2. 1.3^(1/2.5) − 1 = 1.3^0.4 − 1 ≈ **11.1%**.
3. 0.3 × 400 + 0.2 × 150 + 0.5 × (−60) = 120 + 30 − 30 = **+$120k**.
4. EV = 45m → equity = **$33m**.
:::

:::summary Part XX summary
- Know which base a percentage uses (price vs cost; old vs new value; gross vs net of tax).
- Discounts compound; commissions and terms change effective prices.
- Interest depends on rate, day count and compounding; flat rates hide high effective costs.
- In FX, identify the base currency, use bid/ask correctly, and remember forwards reflect interest differentials, not forecasts.
- Convert balance sheets into days to understand cash tied up in operations.
- Landed cost, not supplier price, is the basis for pricing imports.
- Expected values support decisions only together with an understanding of the downside.
:::
